# Workflow: Competitive map

**Produces:** the alternatives and competition map: direct competitors, indirect
competitors, substitutes, doing nothing and building it internally, positioned on
the dimensions buyers actually choose on, with each claim attributed to whoever
made it.

## Trigger

A concept needs to be placed against what buyers use today, a competitor moved
and the picture needs redrawing, or a study asserts a gap in the market that
nobody has checked against the do nothing option.

## Steps

1. **Start from the buyer's current behaviour, not from a vendor list.** The
   question is what the buyer does today when the problem occurs. A spreadsheet,
   an assistant, a phone call and tolerating the problem are all alternatives,
   and three of those never appear in a vendor list.
2. **Enumerate the five classes.** Direct (same job, same approach), indirect
   (same job, different approach), substitute (different job, removes the need),
   do nothing (the most common winner), internal build (the default in
   enterprise). A map missing do nothing and internal build is a map of a market
   that does not exist.
3. **Fix the comparison dimensions from evidence.** Take them from what buyers
   said in Customer Discovery {OS} insights, from review language, and from how
   the alternatives themselves sell. Do not invent dimensions on which your
   concept happens to win. Register the dimensions before scoring anything.
4. **Collect per alternative under preflight.** Positioning, pricing and price
   architecture, features that are table stakes versus differentiating, proof
   they offer, distribution and channel, business model, apparent segment focus.
   Every collection lane passes the source preflight first, and a lane behind
   authentication or a paywall stops rather than being worked around.
5. **Attribute every claim.** A vendor's own site is a claim by that vendor. A
   review is a claim by that reviewer, with its sampling bias. An analyst
   quadrant is a paid or unpaid claim by that analyst. None of these are
   `MEASUREMENT` records, and treating them as such is how a map becomes a
   marketing summary.
6. **Read the reviews for the workaround, not for the star rating.** What people
   do when the product fails them, what they hacked together, and what they left
   for. Keep exact language as evidence, with its locator.
7. **Plot the strategic groups and the value curve.** Where alternatives cluster,
   what every cluster over-serves, and what nobody serves. A genuinely empty
   space is a finding that needs an explanation: usually somebody tried it, and
   the reason it failed is the most valuable thing on the map.
8. **Assess switching.** What the buyer must abandon, migrate, relearn or
   renegotiate. Switching cost is frequently the whole answer, and it is measured
   in the buyer's terms, not in feature counts.
9. **Test the moat claim adversarially.** For every advantage claimed for your
   concept, name what a funded incumbent would do about it within twelve months,
   and how long the advantage survives that.
10. **Record what changes the decision.** State which parts of the map, if
    different, would flip the market decision. That is what `MONITOR` will watch
    later, and it is the only part of a competitive map with a shelf life worth
    maintaining.

## Completion test

- All five alternative classes are present, including do nothing and internal
  build, each with evidence rather than an assertion of irrelevance.
- The comparison dimensions were registered before any scoring, and they come
  from buyer evidence.
- Every claim on the map is attributed to its source, and vendor claims are
  labelled as vendor claims.
- At least one alternative is described in the words of a real user, with a
  locator.
- Every empty space on the map has either an explanation or an explicit
  `UNKNOWN`.
- Switching cost is stated per alternative, in the buyer's terms.
- Each claimed advantage carries the incumbent response that would erode it.
- The map names which of its facts would change the market decision if they moved.
