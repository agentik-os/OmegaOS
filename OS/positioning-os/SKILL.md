# Positioning {OS}

The category you compete in and the claim you own inside it.

## When to use this

Use this OS when the question is **what are we, compared to what, and what do
we claim that a competitor cannot claim without lying**. Concretely: choosing
or naming a category, drafting a claim, testing a claim against real customer
language, resolving two claims that contradict each other, deciding what to be
deliberately worse at, or retiring a claim the evidence no longer supports.

Near neighbours it is confused with:

- **Brand {OS}** dresses the claim. If the question is how it sounds or looks,
  that is Brand. If the question is whether it is true and defensible, that is
  Positioning.
- **Offer {OS}** decides what is actually sold. Positioning decides what you
  say you are for; Offer decides what a buyer receives.
- **Content {OS}** decides what to publish and where. Positioning decides what
  every published thing has to be consistent with.
- **Market Research {OS}** gathers the evidence. Positioning spends it on a
  decision.
- **Storyteller {OS}** carries the claim in narrative form. Storyteller may
  support a claim, and may never invent one.

The discriminating question: **could a competitor put this exact sentence on
their own site without it becoming false?** If yes, it is not a position, and
this is the OS to run.

## Capabilities

- Build the competitive set from how buyers actually compare, with each rival's
  claim quoted from source.
- Decide a category, or declare an invented one with its demand cost stated.
- Draft a claim that names its exclusion, in the customer's vocabulary.
- Test a claim against recorded customer utterances and published rival claims.
- Maintain the claim ledger: evidence, expiry condition, last tester, last date.
- Detect contradiction between two live claims and escalate rather than pick.
- Retire an expired claim and notify every downstream consumer of it.
- Emit the positioning statement in the one form every downstream unit reads.

## Procedure

1. Establish the competitive set. Ask who the buyer compared you against the
   last time they bought, not who you consider a peer.
2. Pull each rival's published claim verbatim, with source and capture date.
   Anything unverifiable is dropped, not paraphrased.
3. Read the customer language corpus from Customer Discovery {OS}. Extract the
   nouns and verbs buyers actually use for the problem.
4. Decide the category. If demand evidence is absent, say so and mark the
   category invented before proceeding.
5. Draft the claim. Force the exclusion: write the sentence that says what you
   are worse at, and refuse the claim if that sentence is empty.
6. Test. Compare the claim against customer utterances for recognition, and
   against rival claims for distinctiveness. A claim a rival could also make
   fails here.
7. Write the ledger entry: claim, evidence, expiry condition, tester, date.
   Refuse the write if any field is empty.
8. Check the whole ledger for contradiction. Contested pairs go to a human.
9. Emit the positioning statement and the ledger to the downstream units.
10. Schedule the review. On expiry, retest or retire; never let a claim age
    into folklore.

## Handoffs

- **Brand {OS}** receives the positioning statement and the category decision.
  It expects a claim it may express but not alter, and the exclusion, because a
  visual system that contradicts the exclusion undoes the position.
- **Offer {OS}** receives the claim the offer must honour. It expects the
  exclusion, so scope does not drift back into ground the position abandoned.
- **Content {OS}** receives the positioning statement and the claim ledger. It
  expects every live claim to carry its evidence, so no published asset makes a
  claim the ledger cannot support.
- **Sales {OS}** receives the statement and the ledger. It expects to know
  which claims are live, which are contested, and which name a competitor.
- **Storyteller {OS}** receives the claim a story may support. It expects the
  claim and its evidence class, and it will refuse to dramatise a claim that
  the ledger marks contested or expired.
- **Growth {OS}** receives the positioning statement so experiments test the
  position rather than drifting away from it.
- **Affiliate {OS}** receives the statement to judge what fits the claim before
  agreeing to promote anything.
