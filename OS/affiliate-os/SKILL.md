# Affiliate {OS}

Sell someone else's real product to an audience you own, without borrowing
liability you cannot discharge.

## When to use this

Use Affiliate {OS} when the product being promoted is not yours: a partner
programme, a referral arrangement, a sponsored recommendation, a tool you use
and would earn on. It is the right unit whenever the answer to "who fixes it
when it breaks" is "not us".

Near neighbours it is confused with:

- **Content {OS}** owns the editorial surface the promotion runs on. Content
  decides what gets published and when. Affiliate decides whether this partner
  deserves that slot at all. If the question is calendar or format, it is
  Content.
- **Growth {OS}** owns the channel as an experiment. Affiliate hands Growth a
  promotion to measure; Growth does not choose the partner.
- **Sales {OS}** owns selling your own offer to a named pipeline. If you can
  change the price, the scope or the guarantee, it is Sales, not Affiliate.
- **Revenue {OS}** owns the money once it is earned. Affiliate produces the
  revenue event and stops there.

The discriminating question: **can you change the product?** If yes, this is
the wrong unit.

## Capabilities

- Vet a partner product against real evidence of use and score its trust cost.
- Record programme terms so a mid-promotion change is detectable, not a shock.
- Draft a jurisdiction-appropriate disclosure and gate the promotion on it.
- Build a promotion plan with a stop condition written before launch.
- Reconcile own attribution against the partner's report and name the gap.
- Produce affiliate revenue events in the shape Revenue {OS} expects.
- Withdraw from a partnership in a way the audience is told about.

## Procedure

1. **Establish use.** Ask what was purchased, when, and what actually happened.
   No use evidence, no candidacy. Do not proceed on the partner's own material.
2. **Score the trust cost before reading the rate.** Who in the audience would
   be harmed if this product underdelivers, and what would that cost. Write the
   verdict before the commission is discussed.
3. **Check coherence with the claim.** Pull the positioning claim. A product
   that fits the audience but contradicts the claim is a decline, stated as a
   contradiction, not resolved here.
4. **Record the terms.** Commission, attribution window, payout schedule, claim
   restrictions, how terms may change, and how to exit.
5. **Write the disclosure and publish it first.** The promotion does not go live
   before the disclosure is live and visible where the audience reads.
6. **Build the promotion.** Every asset traces to something the operator has
   actually experienced. Every asset names who the customer contacts when the
   product fails. Write the stop condition now, not later.
7. **Get explicit approval on the exact copy**, then hand the plan to Content
   {OS} for publishing.
8. **Reconcile.** At each payout period, compare own attribution to the partner
   report. Emit the revenue event and any discrepancy as a finding.
9. **Exit when the stop condition fires.** Take assets down, tell the audience
   what changed, record why.

## Handoffs

| Receiver | What it gets | What it expects |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue {OS} | affiliate revenue event, reconciliation finding | an amount, a period, a partner, and the attribution source |
| Content {OS} | promotion plan, approved assets, disclosure record | a slot request with the disclosure already live |
| Growth {OS} | the promotion as a measurable channel | a stated success threshold and a stop condition |
| Positioning {OS} | a coherence contradiction, when one is found | the claim it contradicts, named |

Affiliate {OS} never hands anything to Delivery & Customer Success {OS}: there
is no fulfilment obligation here, and pretending otherwise is the failure mode
this unit exists to prevent.
