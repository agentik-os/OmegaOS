# Deal Flow {OS}

Source, filter and track opportunities at the top of the funnel.

## When to use this

Use Deal Flow when the question is about many opportunities at once:

- opportunities are arriving and nothing is logged consistently;
- the operator cannot say how many live opportunities there really are;
- the same kind of bad fit keeps consuming an afternoon before being declined;
- a channel needs building, working or retiring;
- passes are being made by silence and relationships are quietly decaying;
- the operator wants to know which source is actually worth their time.

Near neighbours, and the one line that separates them:

| If the question is | Use |
|---|---|
| one named target driven to a close | Acquisition {OS} |
| whether a claim is true | Due Diligence {OS} |
| why this bet should work, and what would kill it | Investment Thesis {OS} |
| how much to commit, and against what policy | Capital {OS} |
| what happens to a position after the deal | Portfolio Management {OS} |

The rule: Deal Flow screens many and commits to none. The moment one
opportunity gets a campaign, a valuation or a negotiation, it has left Deal
Flow.

## Capabilities

- Write and version a screen that produces a qualify or pass decision inside a
  stated time budget.
- Log an opportunity once, with a single attributed source and the raw claim
  kept verbatim.
- Screen an opportunity against the written criteria and record the decision
  with its reason.
- Track stages, with a next action, an owner and a date on every live item.
- Detect and report stalled items rather than carrying them as live.
- Merge duplicate records without losing any referring source.
- Draft a pass that preserves the relationship, for a human to send.
- Build and work sourcing channels: proprietary outreach, referrers, brokers,
  communities, published screens, inbound.
- Maintain the source ledger: qualified opportunities per channel, and the time
  each channel costs.
- Produce funnel metrics: volume, conversion by stage and by source, time in
  stage, and ranked pass reasons.
- Hand a qualified opportunity onward with the packet the receiving OS expects.

## Procedure

1. Establish or load the screen. If none exists, write it before touching the
   pipeline, and check it against the current allocation policy from
   Capital {OS}.
2. Intake: log every opportunity once, with source, date and the counterparty's
   own words kept separate from any summary.
3. Deduplicate against the register before creating a new record.
4. Screen inside the time budget. Produce qualify, pass, or abstain with the
   missing criterion named.
5. On pass: draft the pass, record the reason against the source, and stop.
6. On qualify: assign the stage, the next action, an owner and a date, then
   build the handoff packet.
7. Hand off to Investment Thesis {OS}, Due Diligence {OS} or Acquisition {OS},
   and record the transfer of ownership explicitly.
8. Sweep the register on a cadence: age every stage, mark stalled items, and
   surface them.
9. Close the period with the funnel report, and feed the ranked pass reasons
   back into the next version of the screen.

## Handoffs

| Receives from | What |
|---|---|
| Capital {OS} | current allocation policy and pacing, which bound what is worth screening at all |
| Investment Thesis {OS} | retired categories, so the screen stops producing dead candidates |
| Network {OS} | the relationship graph behind referral sources |

| Hands to | What that OS expects |
|---|---|
| Investment Thesis {OS} | a qualified opportunity with the claim set stated as claims, so the thesis can be written against them |
| Due Diligence {OS} | the specific claims that would change the decision, not the whole file |
| Acquisition {OS} | one named target, the source, the contact history, and the reason it qualified |
| Capital {OS} | the qualified opportunity and its expected cheque range, for pacing, never as an approval request |
| Context & Memory {OS} | the screen, the register, the source ledger and every stage transition |
