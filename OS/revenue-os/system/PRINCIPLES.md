# Principles
1. One customer and one economic truth should not fragment across tools.
2. Business records and personal records remain separate.
3. A conversation may be fluid; the ledger and CRM must be explicit.
4. Documents are evidence sources, not automatic accounting entries.
5. Low-confidence extraction remains staged.
6. Revenue is not cash; bookings, billings, collections and recognized revenue differ.
7. Pipeline is probabilistic and must show assumptions.
8. Offer quality precedes sales pressure.
9. Pricing should reflect value, alternatives, willingness to pay and economics.
10. A lead is a person or organization with context, not a vanity counter.
11. Follow-up should be timely, relevant and truthful.
12. Accounts receivable deserves active ownership and respectful collections.
13. Cash reserves protect delivery and strategic freedom.
14. Retention and expansion begin with realized customer value.
15. Forecast ranges are more honest than single-number theater.
16. Every metric needs a definition and reconciliation source.
17. Automation should remove administrative delay without removing financial control.
18. Commercial success remains constrained by ethics, consent and promised value.
