## 1. Adaptation law

Adapt **story function**, not surface wording.

Freeze Story DNA, then map each channel across:

| Dimension | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Audience state | What do they know, feel, and expect before this? |
| Native unit | Spoken beat, slide, paragraph, post, scene, chart, or live movement? |
| Entry | Where can the audience enter without background? |
| Reveal | What can be delayed, and what must be clear immediately? |
| Proof | What evidence can this medium carry? |
| Voice | Spoken, written, visual, conversational, or executive? |
| Interaction | Scroll, listen, watch, reply, decide, or act? |
| Exit | Memory, reflection, click, conversation, purchase, or decision? |

Do not adapt a private, nuanced story into a high-compression public channel when context loss changes its ethics.

## 2. Short spoken video

Use for one moment, one change, one meaning.

### Native structure

1. **0–3 seconds: entry** — honest tension, image, consequence, or contradiction.
2. **orientation** — one breath of context.
3. **pressure** — what became difficult or uncertain.
4. **hinge** — choice, discovery, or reversal.
5. **change** — observable result or belief update.
6. **echo** — compact meaning or audience transfer.

### Writing rules

- Write for the mouth, not the eye.
- Use breath-sized sentences.
- Place essential context before pronoun ambiguity.
- Remove setup that matters only to the storyteller.
- Let visual evidence carry what speech need not explain.
- Avoid three hooks before the story begins.
- Use captions for access, not as a second competing script.

### Performance check

- Can the first sentence be understood with sound off when captioned?
- Does the speaker reach the actual event quickly?
- Is there one visible turn?
- Does the final line change or echo the opening?
- Would removing the CTA improve dignity or meaning?

## 3. Carousel

Use for stepwise reveal, contrast, teaching, or evidence-rich story.

### Native unit

Each slide performs one function. Default 7–10 slides, not a rule.

1. cover: precise promise or contradiction;
2. scene/problem;
3. expectation or old belief;
4. escalation or evidence;
5. choice/pivot;
6. consequence;
7. principle;
8. boundary or counterpoint;
9. application;
10. proportionate CTA or recall.

### Design-writing rules

- Make each slide understandable and incomplete enough to continue.
- Keep chronology and argument visually legible.
- Use a real artifact, number, quote, or image only when permission and provenance are clear.
- Do not repeat the spoken-video script line by line.
- Put nuance in the slides where it changes interpretation, not only in a caption no one must read.
- End on meaning, not an engagement trick.

## 4. LinkedIn

Use for professional judgment, decisions, lessons, leadership, and evidence-backed transformation.

### Strong jobs

- reveal how a decision was made;
- teach through a real work event;
- show a failed assumption and correction;
- connect human consequence to a business/AI mechanism;
- build trust through specificity and limits.

### Structure

`specific professional tension → event/decision → consequence → mechanism → boundary → practical transfer`

### Avoid

- motivational theater;
- fake dialogue with “my mentor said”;
- inflated revenue or productivity claims;
- vulnerable opening unrelated to the professional insight;
- “ten lessons” extracted from one weak anecdote;
- universal advice from a single case.

Use evidence beside story for outcome claims. Distinguish a customer anecdote from representative performance.

## 5. X

### Single post

Use for one sharp observation, moment, contrast, or rule. Preserve one proof detail. Do not compress away the truth qualifier.

### Thread

Use when causality, sequence, or evidence needs multiple units.

1. honest promise;
2. minimum context;
3. pressure sequence;
4. pivot;
5. evidence or artifact;
6. lesson with boundary;
7. transfer or decision.

Every post should advance the story or argument. Remove recap posts that only manufacture length.

Avoid “I spent 100 hours so you don't have to” unless the hours and resulting synthesis are real.

## 6. YouTube

Use for layered context, multiple scenes, proof, teaching, and visible process.

### Episode architecture

1. cold open: consequential moment or question;
2. audience contract: what they will understand or experience;
3. orientation and stakes;
4. attempt/escalation sequence;
5. evidence, artifacts, or demonstrations;
6. midpoint update or complication;
7. decisive test/choice;
8. result and honest limits;
9. principle and transfer;
10. next episode/open loop only if real.

### Retention without manipulation

- Create genuine questions, not artificial withholding.
- Change mode when the idea changes: scene, explanation, demo, reflection, data.
- Use callbacks to consolidate meaning.
- Preview later evidence only when it will actually appear.
- Cut repetition before adding spectacle.

### Build-in-public series

Keep a project ledger across episodes:

- current objective;
- prior decision;
- assumption under test;
- new evidence;
- mistake or constraint;
- next decision;
- unresolved risk.

This prevents hindsight mythology and lets the audience witness genuine uncertainty.

## 7. Podcast and interview

### Host/interviewer

- Ask for scenes before opinions.
- Follow surprising nouns, choices, and contradictions.
- Ask “what happened next?” before “how did that feel?”
- Separate what the guest knew then from what they know now.
- Do not rush to summarize an unresolved story.
- Confirm boundaries before sensitive detail.

### Guest/storyteller

Prepare:

- three story seeds;
- one proof detail each;
- one belief changed;
- one boundary/nuance;
- one sentence that reconnects to the episode topic.

Do not memorize full prose. Memorize beats and exact facts.

## 8. Keynote and live story

### Narrative-argument weave

Use:

`STORY → INSIGHT → EVIDENCE → APPLICATION → CALLBACK`

The story earns attention and creates a mental model. Evidence supports generalization. Application gives transfer.

### Timing map

For each movement track:

- minutes;
- audience question;
- story/idea function;
- energy level;
- visual support;
- callback;
- transition purpose.

### Live delivery

- Memorize opening, ending, facts, names, and key transitions.
- Hold the middle as beat cards.
- Use pauses at meaning turns, not every dramatic noun.
- Let laughter finish.
- Do not perform emotion you do not feel.
- Retain a recovery path if time is cut.

### Opening options

- enter a scene;
- present a contradiction;
- reveal a consequential choice;
- show an object/artifact;
- ask a genuine decision question.

Avoid long biography, gratitude ritual, or agenda slide before relevance.

## 9. Sales and pitch

Use story to clarify stakes and mechanism; use proof to justify belief.

### Story portfolio for sales

- **origin story** — why this problem matters;
- **problem story** — what the status quo costs;
- **customer story** — what changed for a relevant buyer;
- **objection story** — how a reasonable concern was tested;
- **product story** — why a key feature/mechanism exists;
- **vision story** — what becomes possible;
- **values story** — how the company behaves under pressure.

### Decision sequence

`recognize problem → care about consequence → understand mechanism → trust proof → see fit → take next step`

Do not use emotional transport to hide pricing, limitations, implementation cost, or weak evidence.

## 10. Brand narrative system

Treat brand story as governed consistency across stories, not one “once upon a time.”

### Brand narrative constitution

- worldview: what the brand believes about the category;
- human tension: what people struggle with;
- role: guide, tool, challenger, host, platform, craftsperson, community, or other truthful role;
- promise: what change the brand supports;
- mechanism: how it works;
- proof: why to believe;
- enemy: behavior/system/problem, not a dehumanized group;
- voice: identity and expression rules;
- forbidden moves: claims, clichés, and exploitation the brand rejects;
- story families: origin, product, customer, people, culture, place, future, failure, proof.

### Luxury and private-network stories

- Lead with discernment, provenance, access, care, craft, or meaningful transformation—not empty superlatives.
- Let specific standards and moments prove exclusivity.
- Avoid mass-market urgency tactics that damage distance and trust.
- Do not confuse secrecy with value.
- Make hospitality visible through actions, not adjectives.

## 11. Customer case study

### Evidence stack

1. customer's own context and goal;
2. baseline with measurement date;
3. alternatives tried;
4. selection reason;
5. intervention and customer's actions;
6. measured result and window;
7. other relevant causes;
8. remaining limitations;
9. permission for quote, identity, and channels.

### Formats

- proof card;
- narrative case study;
- sales-call story;
- video testimonial;
- keynote example;
- product learning note.

Create separate approved versions because consent and context differ.

## 12. Data story

### Decision-first structure

1. What decision is waiting?
2. What did we expect or use as baseline?
3. What changed or differs?
4. How large and certain is the difference?
5. What likely drives it?
6. Who or what is affected?
7. What action follows?
8. What should be measured next?

### Chart relationship

Use the chart for exact quantitative relationships. Use prose and story for context, stakes, and decision. Annotate uncertainty and denominators.

Avoid:

- cherry-picked time windows;
- causal language for correlation;
- a single customer story presented as distribution-wide evidence;
- decorative human detail unrelated to the decision;
- scale-free percentages;
- “data speaks for itself.”

## 13. Long-form narrative

Use for documentary, book, essay, case narrative, or long video.

### Architecture layers

- governing question;
- central change;
- chronological spine;
- thematic strands;
- character arcs;
- evidence and source system;
- scene/summary rhythm;
- reveals;
- motifs/callbacks;
- counterevidence;
- ending state and unresolved residue.

Maintain a source ledger. Long form multiplies opportunities for chronology drift and invented bridges.

## 14. Localization and dubbing

Translate Story DNA before words.

### Preserve

- facts and truth qualifiers;
- relationship and status;
- humor function;
- emotional temperature;
- core image;
- cultural meaning;
- CTA intent.

### Adapt

- idiom;
- sentence rhythm;
- cultural references requiring context;
- spoken mouthfeel;
- subtitle length;
- emphasis and pause;
- platform expectations.

Do not make the English version sound like a different founder. Keep a bilingual term bank for names, products, claims, recurring phrases, and forbidden translations.

For AI dubbing, verify pronunciation, names, numbers, pauses, emotional congruence, and consent for voice use.

## 15. Content-series architecture

Build series from a **narrative question**, not a topic label.

Examples:

- Can one founder build this product without becoming trapped by it?
- What changes when a Chief AI Officer treats AI as an operating model, not a tool?
- Can a private network create value without attention metrics?

### Episode engine

Each episode contains:

- current objective;
- pressure or uncertainty;
- action/test;
- evidence;
- change in understanding;
- next consequential question.

Track episodes in the story bank. Avoid claiming victory before the evidence exists. An unfinished journey can be compelling because the truth remains open.
