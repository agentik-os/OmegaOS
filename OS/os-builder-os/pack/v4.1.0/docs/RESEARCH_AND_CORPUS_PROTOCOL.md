# Research and corpus protocol

## Objective

Build a source base that is broad enough to cover the domain, selective enough to avoid noise, and structured enough to support claim-level synthesis.

## The Corpus 360 model

Every domain should be examined through seven lenses:

1. **Bestsellers:** influential ideas and widely adopted language.
2. **Foundations:** classics that created major schools or enduring models.
3. **Evidence-led works:** books and sources grounded in research or formal methods.
4. **Practitioner works:** repeatable field procedures and operational detail.
5. **Specialist works:** subdomain depth that broad books omit.
6. **Current works:** recent developments, updated editions and new conditions.
7. **Critical works:** counter-evidence, limitations, misuse and failure analysis.

The final corpus is not a popularity ranking. It is a coverage portfolio.

## Candidate scoring

Score each candidate from 0 to 5 on:

- domain relevance;
- source authority;
- evidence transparency;
- practical actionability;
- unique coverage contribution;
- influence on the field;
- currentness where needed;
- counterpoint value;
- transferability to the target user;
- known limitation severity.

Use the score to support judgment, not replace it.

## Retention rules

Retain a source when it materially contributes at least one of:

- a unique mechanism;
- a major school of thought;
- a validated procedure;
- a missing subdomain;
- a useful counterargument;
- a critical failure mode;
- a current update;
- a canonical vocabulary used by practitioners.

Reject or deprioritize when:

- it is substantially redundant;
- access is too weak to support reliable extraction;
- claims are unverifiable and add no unique operational value;
- it is outdated without historical or conceptual importance;
- it is purely inspirational and cannot be translated into rules or practice;
- it is promotional material masquerading as evidence.

## Per-book analysis independence

First-pass book analyses should be independent to reduce convergence and anchoring.

Each analyst receives:

- the build contract;
- the book-analysis schema;
- the target OS extraction objective;
- access limitations and provenance requirements.

The analyst should not receive the conclusions of other book analysts until extraction is complete.

## Saturation rule

Corpus discovery can stop when:

- every critical subdomain has adequate sources;
- every major school is represented;
- additional candidates are mostly redundant;
- key contradictions have evidence on both sides;
- risks and failure modes are covered;
- currentness requirements are met.

A numeric target can guide planning, but saturation decides completion.

## Non-book expansion

For each major book-derived claim, ask:

- Is this empirical, conceptual, procedural, normative or rhetorical?
- Does current evidence support it?
- Does it apply to the target user and context?
- Is there official guidance or regulation?
- Are there failed implementations or adverse effects?
- Has the domain changed since publication?

Use Research {OS} to fill the resulting gaps.

## Copyright and access integrity

- Analyze books using lawful access, user-provided material, licensed sources, public author material and reliable secondary evidence.
- Do not claim full-text review when only summaries or excerpts were available.
- Paraphrase the source knowledge.
- Store brief excerpts only when necessary and legally permitted.
- Record edition and access level because editions may differ materially.
