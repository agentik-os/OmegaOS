# Book deep extraction standard

Every retained book produces both a human-readable analysis and a schema-valid structured record.

## Required sections

### 1. Identity

- title;
- author;
- edition and publication date;
- domain category;
- access basis;
- analyst confidence.

### 2. Thesis and problem

- central thesis;
- problem the author is solving;
- target reader;
- promised transformation;
- underlying worldview.

### 3. Conceptual system

- core concepts;
- causal mechanisms;
- models and frameworks;
- sequence or lifecycle;
- definitions;
- prerequisites.

### 4. Operational intelligence

- diagnostics;
- decision rules;
- procedures;
- exercises;
- scripts or templates;
- cadence;
- metrics;
- review loops;
- stopping rules;
- escalation rules.

### 5. Evidence and limitations

- evidence used by the author;
- empirical support;
- anecdotes or unsupported assertions;
- scope conditions;
- risks;
- contraindications;
- likely author incentives or blind spots;
- dated assumptions.

### 6. Comparative value

- unique contribution;
- overlaps;
- contradictions;
- school of thought;
- concepts likely to survive synthesis;
- concepts likely to be rejected or made conditional.

### 7. OS compilation candidates

- candidate principles;
- candidate states;
- candidate diagnostics;
- candidate rules;
- candidate commands;
- candidate workflows;
- candidate metrics;
- candidate eval scenarios;
- candidate handoffs to other OSs.

## Validation rules

An analysis fails validation when:

- the access basis is hidden;
- thesis and operational content are conflated;
- unsupported claims are presented as facts;
- limitations are absent;
- no OS compilation candidates are extracted;
- the analysis is generic enough to fit another book unchanged;
- the structured record does not match the schema.
