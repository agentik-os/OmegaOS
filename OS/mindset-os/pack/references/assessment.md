## 1. Assessment rules

- Ask only questions that change the plan.
- Accept uncertainty; mark unknowns instead of forcing certainty.
- Separate current facts, interpretations, desired future, and social comparison.
- Review the last 14–30 days of behavior, not only self-image.
- Seek disconfirming evidence and hidden costs.
- Do not diagnose personality, ADHD, trauma, depression, anxiety, or addiction.
- Run the safety screen before aggressive change.

## 2. Twelve-domain map

Score each domain from 0 to 10 using recent evidence. A score is a navigation aid, not a clinical measure.

| Domain | What to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Safety & stability | acute distress, medical issues, housing, substances, crisis support |
| Meaning & spirituality | purpose, faith, coherence, gratitude, service, existential conflict |
| Identity & self-trust | promises kept, self-story, shame, integrity, standards |
| Mental & emotional fitness | awareness, flexibility, rumination, fear, coping, recovery |
| Sleep & recovery | duration, regularity, quality, fatigue, rest, workload |
| Physical energy | movement, training, nutrition, hydration, pain, health care |
| Attention & environment | distraction, digital use, workspace, cues, task switching |
| Execution & learning | priorities, shipping, practice, feedback, completion |
| Work & value creation | problem quality, capability, offer, customer value, distribution |
| Money behavior | clarity, cash flow, debt, runway, savings, risk, lifestyle inflation |
| Relationships & network | trust, belonging, conflict, boundaries, reciprocity, loneliness |
| Joy & aliveness | play, nature, creativity, beauty, novelty, recovery, celebration |

## 3. Quick audit

Ask five questions, then draft a baseline:

1. What change would make the next 90 days meaningfully different?
2. What repeatedly stops you, despite knowing what to do?
3. What are your sleep, energy, and stress like during a normal week?
4. What promise to yourself do you most need to start keeping?
5. What must this transformation never cost you?

Then request or infer a 0–10 rating for the 12 domains. Mark inferred ratings as assumptions.

## 4. Deep audit

### Present truth

- Current location, household, obligations, work, income structure, debt/runway if relevant.
- Typical weekday and weekend, based on calendar rather than aspiration.
- Current sleep/wake times and energy curve.
- Current movement, food pattern, stimulants, alcohol, nicotine, supplements, medications where voluntarily disclosed.
- Current spiritual practice and preferred worldview.
- Current support system and professional care.

### Identity history

- Roles learned in family, school, work, culture, and relationships.
- Repeated labels: “lazy,” “gifted,” “too much,” “not enough,” “lucky,” “outsider,” etc.
- Moments of earned self-respect.
- Promises repeatedly broken and conditions around them.
- Successes the user discounts and failures the user overgeneralizes.

### Ambition and money

- Exact meaning of the wealth target.
- Why that number, why now, and whose approval it represents.
- Current wealth engine and evidence of market demand.
- Attitude toward selling, asking, risk, debt, spending, saving, ownership, and patience.
- “Enough” threshold and lifestyle inflation rules.
- Ethical red lines.

### Competing commitments

For every stated goal, ask what the user may be protecting by not achieving it:

- protection from judgment;
- protection from responsibility;
- belonging to an old identity or group;
- avoiding boredom, rejection, visibility, or constraint;
- preserving optionality;
- avoiding proof that the strategy is wrong.

Treat these as hypotheses, not accusations.

### Environment

- Physical cues and friction.
- Phone, notifications, social media, open tabs, and message expectations.
- People who normalize or punish the target behavior.
- Access to food, training, nature, quiet, and recovery.
- Calendar ownership and meeting load.
- Financial environment: accounts, automations, spending triggers.

### Recent evidence

Ask for three examples each:

- action aligned with target identity;
- avoidance or self-sabotage;
- rapid recovery;
- value created for another person;
- behavior driven by comparison or fear.

## 5. Bottleneck selection

Score candidate bottlenecks from 1–5:

```text
priority = (impact × leverage × urgency × controllability)
           ÷ (burden × risk × dependency)
```

Use judgment; do not pretend the score is objective. Select one primary bottleneck and at most two support bottlenecks.

Common primary bottlenecks:

- sleep instability;
- emotional avoidance;
- undefined target;
- task overload;
- no market feedback;
- no distribution behavior;
- inconsistent selling;
- cash panic;
- distraction-rich environment;
- loneliness or unaddressed conflict;
- identity tied to novelty rather than completion.

## 6. Baseline outputs

Produce:

1. Current-season statement.
2. 12-domain scorecard with evidence and confidence.
3. Primary bottleneck and causal hypothesis.
4. Assets and strengths already available.
5. Risks and non-negotiables.
6. One 90-day outcome and one 30-day stabilization outcome.
7. Five “Not Now” items.
8. First 24-hour action.

Do not give a giant protocol before the baseline reveals capacity.
