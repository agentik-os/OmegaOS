## 1. Purpose

Compile scattered beliefs, experiences, faith, values, lessons, and ambitions into explicit decision rules. A personal philosophy should help the user choose under pressure; it should not become a collection of attractive slogans.

Use the compiler when:

- the same result or mistake repeats;
- goals conflict;
- the user knows what to do but repeatedly chooses differently;
- wealth ambition has no ethical or lifestyle definition;
- inherited beliefs about money, work, love, health, or worth dominate behavior;
- self-education is broad but not applied;
- a life transition makes the old rules obsolete.

## 2. Philosophy data model

Represent each philosophy item as:

```yaml
id: PHIL-001
domain: money
statement: "Security requires keeping every opportunity open."
type: inherited_rule
source: family / culture / experience / faith / mentor / evidence / reaction
confidence: 0-100
evidence_for: []
evidence_against: []
protective_function: "Avoid regret and scarcity."
current_benefit: "Encourages opportunity awareness."
current_cost: "Creates dispersion and unfinished work."
context_where_useful: "Early exploration."
context_where_harmful: "Committed execution cycle."
replacement: "Explore in a bounded window; once committed, finish or explicitly stop."
behavioral_test: "Use Now/Next/Not Now for four weeks."
review_date: YYYY-MM-DD
status: retain / revise / retire / test
```

Do not require YAML in normal coaching output. Preserve the fields conceptually.

## 3. Source audit

Trace the origin of current rules:

- family scripts and money stories;
- religion or spiritual teaching;
- school and authority experiences;
- peer group and class expectations;
- mentors, books, creators, and algorithms;
- success experiences;
- humiliation, betrayal, scarcity, loss, or trauma;
- national/cultural norms;
- professional identity;
- chosen principles based on evidence.

Ask:

- Who taught this explicitly or implicitly?
- What happened when the user first adopted it?
- What did the belief protect?
- Is the original environment still present?
- Does the rule generalize too far?
- What relationship or identity might be threatened by changing it?

Treat trauma-related material within coaching boundaries. Do not force excavation.

## 4. Domain belief audit

### Self and worth

- Is worth inherent, earned, or constantly at risk?
- What must happen before rest, love, or pride is allowed?
- Does failure describe an event or the whole person?
- Is asking for help compatible with strength?

### Time

- Is time something to fill, protect, trade, invest, enjoy, or survive?
- Does urgency create importance?
- Is being busy proof of value?
- What deserves unstructured time?

### Work and discipline

- Does hard work mean hours, discomfort, completion, or value?
- Is discipline punishment or keeping a chosen agreement?
- When should persistence end?
- Is rest part of work quality?

### Learning

- Is knowledge collected, applied, taught, or embodied?
- Can the user act before knowing everything?
- What type of feedback is trusted?
- Which skill would change current results?

### Money and wealth

- Is money safety, freedom, power, love, proof, danger, corruption, or stewardship?
- Is high income confused with wealth?
- What is enough?
- What may never be done for money?
- Does the user allocate before spending?
- What is believed about ownership, profit, selling, debt, investing, and giving?

### Marketplace and service

- Does the user believe value speaks for itself?
- Is selling service, manipulation, rejection, or diagnosis?
- Which problems deserve solving?
- Is market price confused with human worth?

### Relationships and association

- Must belonging be purchased through agreement or rescue?
- Can boundaries coexist with love?
- Is networking transactional or relational?
- Whose standards shape the user most?

### Health and energy

- Is sleep optional when ambition rises?
- Is exercise care or punishment?
- Is food fuel, comfort, morality, culture, or control?
- What body signals are ignored?

### Faith and spirituality

- Is prayer relationship, surrender, request, discernment, or transaction?
- Does suffering indicate failure of faith?
- Are intuitions tested with ethics and facts?
- Does ambition serve stewardship or self-deification?

### Failure, risk, and uncertainty

- Does uncertainty prevent action?
- Does a bad outcome prove a bad decision?
- Is every setback personal?
- What risk could remove the user from the game?

### Lifestyle

- Is enjoyment delayed until success?
- Does luxury mean quality, status, beauty, access, or belonging?
- Which forms of richness cost little?
- What should wealth amplify?

## 5. Philosophy debugging

Use the `TRACE` protocol:

```text
T — Trigger: What situation activated the rule?
R — Rule: What did the user believe must be true or done?
A — Action: What behavior followed?
C — Consequence: What immediate and delayed result occurred?
E — Edit: Retain, narrow, replace, or test the rule.
```

### Debug tests

- **Reality:** Is it factually supportable?
- **Scope:** In what conditions is it true or false?
- **Agency:** Does it identify a possible response?
- **Ethics:** What happens to other people if followed?
- **Health:** Does it require chronic self-harm or neglect?
- **Strategy:** Does it improve the causal path to the goal?
- **Relationship:** Does it deepen trust or justify exploitation?
- **Downside:** What could go wrong if believed too strongly?
- **Identity:** Does it allow learning and repair?
- **Spiritual integrity:** Does it align with the user's chosen faith without bypassing reality?

## 6. Principle compilation

Compile principles in operational language:

```text
Principle name: Truth before ego
Decision: Seek disconfirming evidence before scaling.
Trigger: Excitement about a new product or strategy.
Behavior: Run a bounded customer/market test.
Boundary: Do not use “testing” to delay every commitment.
Evidence: Test completed and decision recorded.
Repair: Pause expansion and obtain missing evidence.
```

Good principles:

- clarify a tradeoff;
- indicate behavior;
- include a boundary or exception;
- are short enough to remember;
- survive bad days;
- can be audited from evidence.

Weak principles:

- “Be successful.”
- “Always think positive.”
- “Never quit.”
- “Money flows effortlessly.”
- “Only associate with winners.”

Replace them with contextual rules.

## 7. Decision laws

Develop no more than 12 core laws. Candidate structure:

1. **Safety before optimization.**
2. **Truth before identity protection.**
3. **One decisive outcome before expansion.**
4. **Finish, explicitly stop, or renegotiate—never drift indefinitely.**
5. **Value and service before status.**
6. **Ownership and compounding before lifestyle inflation.**
7. **Reversible tests fast; irreversible decisions slow.**
8. **Allocate resources before consumption expands.**
9. **Relationships are ends as well as sources of support.**
10. **Learning must change action, capability, or judgment.**
11. **Rest, beauty, faith, and joy belong in the present life.**
12. **A lapse requires repair, not identity collapse.**

Personalize rather than adopting the candidates verbatim.

## 8. Mental diet and self-education

### Input inventory

For seven days capture:

- person/source;
- minutes;
- topic;
- emotional effect;
- belief installed;
- action generated;
- trust/evidence quality.

Classify inputs:

- truth and primary evidence;
- craft and capability;
- faith/philosophy;
- business/market;
- health;
- relationships;
- culture/art;
- entertainment/play;
- outrage, comparison, or noise.

Do not eliminate all entertainment. Design a portfolio that serves the whole life.

### Applied curriculum

For each 90-day cycle choose:

```text

Live problem:
Primary source/course/mentor:
Practice cadence:
Feedback source:
Weekly output:
Graduation evidence:
Not Now subjects:
```

Use `learn -> apply -> explain -> measure -> update`.

## 9. Philosophy Constitution template

Produce:

### Preamble

Who the user is becoming, whom the life serves, and what may not be sacrificed.

### Reality commitments

How facts, evidence, uncertainty, feedback, and error are treated.

### Identity and worth

Stable human worth plus trainable standards and responsibility.

### Faith and meaning

Chosen spiritual orientation, prayer, surrender, service, discernment, and boundaries.

### Work and value

Definition of useful work, marketplace value, service, selling, excellence, and completion.

### Wealth and stewardship

What money is for, enoughness, ownership, allocation, risk, giving, and lifestyle rules.

### Health and energy

Sleep, food, movement, recovery, medical care, substances, and unsafe optimization boundaries.

### Relationships and association

Trust, love, boundaries, community, influence, repair, and non-transactional care.

### Learning and inputs

Self-education, sources, application, journal, and mental diet.

### Time and attention

Priority, calendar, digital rules, work in progress, and rest.

### Failure and repair

How setbacks, wrongdoing, bad luck, and system failure are classified and repaired.

### Lifestyle and legacy

Art of living now, future freedom, beauty, contribution, and what success should leave behind.

### Twelve laws

The final concise operating principles.

## 10. Review and versioning

Version philosophy rather than rewriting it emotionally every week.

### Weekly

- one rule observed in action;
- one contradiction;
- one lesson to capture;
- no major constitution rewrite unless safety requires it.

### Monthly

- review repeated results and errors;
- test one philosophy item;
- update confidence/evidence.

### Quarterly

- revise principles supported by multiple observations;
- archive retired rules with the reason;
- check coherence with goals, health, relationships, faith, and wealth engine.

### Annual

- issue a new version with date;
- summarize what changed and why;
- preserve a record of prior versions;
- choose the next learning and character emphasis.

Never use philosophy versioning to hide broken promises. Record repair explicitly.
