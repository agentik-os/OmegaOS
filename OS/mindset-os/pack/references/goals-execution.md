## 1. Goal doctrine

- A goal chooses a tradeoff; it is not a wish list.
- Strategy states what will not be done.
- Outcome goals guide direction; process goals produce evidence; standards protect identity.
- Do not use an arbitrary wealth number without a causal wealth engine.
- Capacity is a constraint. Plan from calendar, cash, energy, relationships, and health.
- Ambition may be large while the next action remains small and testable.

## 2. Goal stack

Build from direction to action:

| Horizon | Function | Required content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 years | direction | life, contribution, ownership, relationships, health, freedom |
| 3 years | strategic position | capabilities, assets, reputation, team, distribution, capital |
| 1 year | outcomes | 3–5 measurable changes with guardrails |
| 90 days | transformation | one decisive outcome, supporting systems, evidence |
| 30 days | installation | stabilize a keystone and complete a feedback cycle |
| Week | result | one completed/observable deliverable |
| Day | execution | decisive action before reactive work |

Do not demand precision where uncertainty is high. Use directional hypotheses and update dates.

## 3. Outcome contract

For each outcome define:

```text
Outcome:
Why now:
Baseline:
Target and date:
User/customer value created:
Lead indicators:
Lag indicators:
Quality bar:
Constraints:
Anti-goals:
Dependencies:
Downside risks:
Kill/pivot criteria:
Review cadence:
Next physical action:
```

### Lead indicators

Behaviors within direct influence: qualified conversations, proposals, product experiments, published work, focused practice, workouts, bedtime consistency.

### Lag indicators

Delayed results: revenue, net worth, customers, body composition, audience, promotions, health markers.

Never punish a good process for short-term noisy outcomes. Never excuse a failing strategy indefinitely because effort was high.

## 4. WOOP and if-then planning

Use mental contrasting with implementation intentions:

1. **Wish:** important, challenging, feasible within the horizon.
2. **Outcome:** best meaningful result and felt consequence.
3. **Obstacle:** the primary internal behavior or reaction likely to interfere.
4. **Plan:** “If [specific cue/obstacle], then I will [specific response].”

Examples:

- If I open a new idea during the build block, then I add one line to `Not Now` and return within 60 seconds.
- If I avoid a sales follow-up because of discomfort, then I send the two-sentence version before editing it.
- If I sleep under six hours, then I use the recovery day protocol and avoid major irreversible decisions.
- If a financial opportunity promises fast guaranteed returns, then I pause 72 hours and obtain independent review.

## 5. Ninety-day cycle

### Week 0 — Baseline and design

Audit, select one transformation, remove commitments, define metrics, schedule actions, and prepare environment.

### Weeks 1–2 — Stabilize

Run only floor and standard routines. Observe friction. Do not add more practices because motivation is high.

### Weeks 3–6 — Build evidence

Increase repetitions, ship weekly, collect external feedback, and test the primary limiting-belief hypothesis.

### Weeks 7–10 — Leverage

Improve quality, delegate or automate only repeated proven work, strengthen distribution, and protect recovery.

### Weeks 11–12 — Consolidate

Close loops, measure outcomes and costs, document learning, celebrate honestly, and decide continue/pivot/stop.

### Graduation criteria

Graduate a behavior only when it is reliable across normal disruption. A streak in perfect conditions is insufficient.

## 6. Weekly and daily execution

### Weekly result

Choose one result that is visible by Friday. It must advance the 90-day objective and be small enough to finish.

### Daily decisive action

Do it before open-ended communication when possible. Define start cue, duration, done condition, and fallback version.

### Work blocks

- Deep build: 45–90 minutes.
- Administrative batch: 20–45 minutes.
- Learning: 20–30 minutes connected to a live problem.
- Capture: under 5 minutes; processing occurs later.
- Shutdown: review, capture, choose tomorrow's first action.

Adjust duration to capacity. Do not turn long hours into the metric.

## 7. Founder and ADHD-aware focus

Use these rules when attention variability, novelty seeking, or completion difficulty is present, whether or not ADHD is diagnosed:

- One active “must finish” outcome.
- `Now / Next / Not Now` visible list.
- Externalize memory; never rely on keeping tasks in mind.
- Separate idea capture from project activation.
- Define “done” before beginning.
- Start with a two-minute launch action.
- Use body doubling or accountability if helpful.
- Limit work-in-progress to one major and two minor items.
- Time-box research and require a decision artifact.
- Pair novelty with completion: new exploration unlocks after a closeout.
- Reduce hidden tasks by writing physical next actions.
- Use an immediate restart after distraction, not a next-day restart.

Never equate attention variability with laziness or moral failure.

## 8. Learning and deliberate practice

Connect learning to performance:

```text
Live problem -> target subskill -> focused practice -> rapid feedback
             -> correction -> spaced retrieval -> real-world application
```

Require a learning output: decision, experiment, explanation, shipped artifact, or changed behavior. Avoid consuming knowledge as emotional avoidance.

Use:

- retrieval practice;
- spaced repetition;
- interleaving where appropriate;
- decomposition into subskills;
- immediate corrective feedback;
- teaching or explanation;
- after-action reviews;
- error logs.

## 9. Decision protocols

### Reversible vs irreversible

Move fast on reversible, bounded experiments. Slow down on legal, health, reputation, financial, relationship, or irreversible commitments.

### Ten-ten-ten

How will the decision feel or matter in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years?

### Pre-mortem

Assume the plan failed. List causes across market, execution, energy, relationships, finance, ethics, and environment. Convert high-probability causes into controls.

### Opportunity filter

Score strategic alignment, value created, ownership, learning, relationship quality, reversibility, energy cost, distraction cost, downside, and timing.

### Decision journal

Record date, context, options, assumptions, probability estimates, emotional state, chosen option, guardrails, and review date. Judge process separately from outcome luck.
