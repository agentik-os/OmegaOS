## 1. Cadence doctrine

Cadence converts values into repeated contact with reality. Each ritual needs a time budget, output, and stop condition. Do not make reflection longer than execution.

Use three intensity levels:

- **Floor:** preserve identity and safety.
- **Standard:** normal operating rhythm.
- **Deep:** optional retreat or expanded session.

## 2. Daily protocols

### Morning floor — 3 to 7 minutes

1. Three slow, comfortable breaths or a brief grounding.
2. Prayer/intention: “Who do I choose to be today?”
3. Read the 90-day outcome and identity standard.
4. Choose today's decisive action.
5. Start or schedule its first physical step.

### Morning standard — 20 to 45 minutes

1. Wake, light, water, and basic care.
2. Prayer or meditation.
3. Brief gratitude and surrender.
4. Process visualization plus obstacle/if-then plan.
5. Review energy and calendar.
6. Choose one decisive work result and relationship/health non-negotiables.
7. Begin before reactive communication where feasible.

### Morning deep — 60 to 90 minutes

Add spiritual study, journaling, longer meditation, exercise, or learning only if sleep and schedule support it. It is not required for identity success.

### Midday reset — 1 to 5 minutes

- stop;
- breathe or orient;
- name current state;
- ask whether the day still serves the decisive action;
- remove or renegotiate one distraction;
- resume with a physical next step.

### Evening floor — 3 minutes

1. Record one identity vote.
2. Name one open loop for tomorrow.
3. Gratitude, prayer, or release.
4. Protect sleep.

### Evening standard — 10 to 20 minutes

- What did I finish or ship?
- Where did I keep/break a promise?
- What did I learn?
- What needs repair or follow-up?
- What is tomorrow's first action?
- Close work, prepare environment, and downshift.

Do not use the evening review for exhaustive self-criticism.

## 3. Weekly review

Time budget: 30–60 minutes.

### Part A — Evidence

- promises kept and repaired;
- shipped work and external value;
- lead and lag indicators;
- sleep/energy pattern;
- training/movement consistency;
- spiritual/relationship nourishment;
- money and cash visibility;
- distractions and avoidance;
- joy, play, and recovery.

### Part B — Learning

- biggest win and why it happened;
- biggest friction and causal hypothesis;
- five-piece trace: philosophy -> attitude -> activity -> results -> lifestyle;
- lesson or repeated error worth recording in the applied journal;
- what to stop, continue, start, simplify;
- assumption updated;
- conversation or repair needed;
- one system change.

### Part C — Commit

- next week's single result;
- three supporting actions;
- calendar blocks;
- one health floor;
- one spiritual/relationship act;
- explicit Not Now list;
- if-then plan for the primary obstacle.

## 4. Monthly, quarterly, and annual reviews

### Monthly — system health

- 12-domain trend;
- recurring bottleneck;
- financial clarity and runway;
- workload and recovery;
- habit graduation/removal;
- relationship health;
- one simplification.

### Quarterly — transformation

- outcome vs baseline;
- value created and captured;
- identity evidence;
- strategy and market feedback;
- health and relationship costs;
- capabilities/assets built;
- continue, pivot, stop, or scale;
- next 90-day outcome.

### Annual — life direction

- meaningful moments and contribution;
- health trajectory;
- people loved, neglected, lost, or found;
- work and wealth engine;
- owned assets and dependencies;
- failures and repaired harm;
- enoughness and lifestyle;
- current season;
- 3-year strategic position and next-year outcomes.

Include celebration and grief. An annual review is not only an audit.

## 5. Thirty- and ninety-day transformations

### 30-day stabilization

Use when basics are unreliable. Focus on sleep/wake opportunity, daily floor, one work completion block, regular nourishment/movement, and weekly review.

### 90-day identity cycle

Choose:

- one identity standard;
- one decisive external outcome;
- one energy keystone;
- one relationship/spiritual practice;
- one wealth behavior;
- one scorecard.

Do not install an entirely new life at once.

## 6. Reset protocols

### Sixty-second reset

1. Stop and exhale slowly.
2. Say what is happening in one factual sentence.
3. Name the next physical action.
4. Do it for two minutes.

### After a missed day

- no compensation marathon;
- identify whether cause was capacity, environment, emotion, ambiguity, or choice;
- repair any affected promise;
- perform the floor at the next safe cue;
- change one condition.

### After a bad week

1. Protect sleep and essential obligations.
2. Cancel or park nonessential commitments.
3. Return to the single 90-day outcome.
4. Run a short after-action review.
5. Schedule one completion and one support conversation.
6. Use floors for seven days before scaling.

### After a major failure

- stabilize and seek support;
- separate harm, loss, strategy, execution, and luck;
- communicate and repair promptly;
- protect against irreversible reactions;
- conduct a review after acute emotion settles;
- choose close, recover, or retest based on evidence.

## 7. Special-day protocols

### Low sleep

Reduce intensity, protect food/hydration, use light movement, avoid major irreversible decisions, and restore sleep opportunity. Do not “win the day” with excess stimulants.

### Travel

Use floor prayer/meditation, walking, basic nourishment, one decisive micro-action, and normal resumption without punishment.

### Crisis or grief

Suspend optimization. Focus on safety, care, connection, essential obligations, professional support, and humane minimums.

### High opportunity / launch

Define temporary sprint duration, sleep floor, nutrition/movement minimum, relationship communication, decision rights, stop date, and recovery block. Do not let temporary intensity become identity.

### Retreat day

Use silence, prayer/meditation, nature, deep review, future direction, and strategy. End with fewer commitments and a scheduled first action.
