## 1. Habit doctrine

Habits are context-linked automatic tendencies built through repetition. They are not proof of virtue. Formation time varies widely; avoid fixed “21-day” promises.

Use habits for stable repeated behaviors. Use checklists for complex sequences, calendar blocks for time-specific work, projects for finite outcomes, and principles for novel decisions.

## 2. Behavior diagnosis

For a behavior that is not happening, inspect:

- clarity: is the action physically specific?
- cue: is there a stable trigger?
- ability: is it small enough for current capacity?
- friction: are tools, location, or setup obstructing it?
- reward: is completion emotionally or socially legible?
- emotion: what discomfort is being avoided?
- identity: does it conflict with a self-story or group belonging?
- timing: is it placed at a low-energy or interrupted period?
- competition: what behavior currently wins at that cue?
- consequence: are rewards delayed and costs immediate?

Do not prescribe more motivation until the failure mechanism is identified.

## 3. Habit installation

Use this template:

```text
Identity served:
Behavior:
Floor version:
Standard version:
Deep version:
Cue:
Location:
Setup completed beforehand:
Immediate completion signal/reward:
Likely obstacle:
If-then recovery:
Tracking method:
Graduation criterion:
```

### Installation sequence

1. Choose one keystone behavior.
2. Make the floor almost impossible to refuse.
3. Attach it to a stable cue or location.
4. Prepare the environment in advance.
5. Practice at the intended cue.
6. Record completion without moral judgment.
7. Review friction weekly.
8. Expand only after reliability.

Use `never miss twice` only as a gentle recovery heuristic. A person may miss twice due to illness or crisis; the deeper rule is “return at the next safe opportunity.”

## 4. Environment design

### Physical

- place desired tools in the path of action;
- remove setup steps;
- create single-purpose zones where possible;
- make sleep, movement, healthy food, prayer, and focused work visually obvious;
- place temptations out of immediate reach rather than demanding constant resistance.

### Digital

- default notifications off except essential people/systems;
- use full-screen/single-task work;
- block or log out of high-friction distraction sources during focus windows;
- keep a capture inbox and scheduled processing time;
- create a clean startup screen containing the decisive action;
- separate creation tools from consumption feeds.

### Social

- tell relevant people the boundary and why;
- spend time with people for whom the desired behavior is normal;
- create an accountability agreement focused on evidence and repair;
- avoid relationships built on humiliation, coercion, or public shame.

### Financial

- automate essential bills and chosen savings where appropriate;
- introduce waiting periods for discretionary purchases;
- remove saved cards from high-trigger shopping contexts if useful;
- separate operating, tax, reserve, investing, and spending purposes as appropriate;
- add independent review for high-risk decisions.

## 5. Commitment and accountability

Choose the lightest effective mechanism:

- calendar commitment;
- visible checklist;
- coach or partner check-in;
- body doubling;
- public or group commitment;
- pre-commitment contract;
- financial stake only when safe and non-coercive.

Accountability reports should include:

```text
Promised | Done | Evidence | Friction | Repair | Next promise
```

Do not use penalties that threaten basic needs, intensify shame, encourage unsafe exercise/food restriction, or create financial harm.

## 6. Breaking unwanted patterns

Map the loop:

```text
Trigger -> body/emotion -> story -> urge -> action -> immediate payoff -> delayed cost
```

Interventions can target:

- trigger removal;
- added friction or delay;
- alternative response serving the same need;
- urge surfing;
- environment change;
- social support;
- replacement reward;
- sleep/stress correction;
- professional treatment where dependence, compulsion, or risk is present.

Use a lapse review, not a shame spiral:

- What was the trigger?
- What need was the behavior trying to meet?
- What protection was missing?
- What is the next safe interruption point?
- What environment change can occur within 24 hours?

## 7. Routine design

Routines are containers, not sacred scripts. Every routine must have:

- purpose;
- entry cue;
- ordered steps;
- time budget;
- floor/standard/deep versions;
- interruption rule;
- done condition;
- review date.

Avoid stacking prayer, meditation, journaling, visualization, breathwork, reading, workout, cold exposure, and planning into a mandatory two-hour morning ritual. Select the smallest combination that changes the day.

## 8. Automation and scaling

Do not automate a personal or business routine before it is understood. A useful rule is to run a workflow manually enough times to identify stable steps, exceptions, and failure modes before automating.

Automate reminders, capture, data aggregation, and recurring setup. Preserve human judgment for values, relationships, health, irreversible decisions, spiritual discernment, and strategy.
