# Deal Structuring {OS}

Terms, instruments and incentives that survive contact with reality.

## When to use this

Use Deal Structuring when the question is about the shape of the money rather
than whether to spend it:

- choosing between equity, convertible, note, seller financing, earnout or a
  revenue share, and being able to say why the others were rejected;
- working out what each party actually receives at a bad, an average and a good
  exit;
- deciding whether a liquidation preference, a ratchet or a guarantee is worth
  what it costs in goodwill;
- setting vesting, an option pool and management incentives that still work in
  year three;
- designing an earnout that does not become a lawsuit;
- preparing for a negotiation by ranking terms in cash rather than in feeling;
- checking executed documents against what was actually agreed.

Near neighbours, and the one line that separates them:

| If the question is | Use |
|---|---|
| who to contact, by when, and what happens if the date slips | Acquisition {OS} |
| whether the numbers being modelled are true | Due Diligence {OS} |
| whether this bet is worth making at all | Investment Thesis {OS} |
| how much may be committed, against what policy | Capital {OS} |
| how a reserved matter is actually voted on later | Board {OS} |

The rule: Deal Structuring writes the right, Board {OS} exercises it.
Acquisition runs the process, Deal Structuring decides what the process is
negotiating about.

## Capabilities

- Select an instrument and record why each rejected alternative was rejected.
- Build a complete cap table, including old convertibles, warrants, unissued
  options and side letters.
- Model the waterfall at low, middle and high exit values, in cash per party.
- Price a liquidation preference, a ratchet, a guarantee or a deferred payment
  in cash rather than in principle.
- Size downside protection to a named risk, and reject protections with no
  named risk.
- Model an earnout with the counterparty controlling the metric.
- Set vesting, option pool size and pool timing, and show who each choice
  dilutes.
- Assemble a term sheet as a draft for legal review, with the binding parts
  flagged for the lawyer first.
- Rank terms by cash value and build the trade set and the walk away terms.
- Reconcile executed documents line by line against the agreed terms.
- State the tax questions a qualified adviser must answer, without answering
  them.

## Procedure

1. Load the complete existing cap table. If instruments are missing, stop and
   name them: a waterfall on a partial cap table is worse than none.
2. Choose the instrument for the situation, and write down why each rejected
   alternative was rejected.
3. Model the cap table pre and post, then the waterfall at low, middle and high
   exits, in cash for every party.
4. Take the verified findings from Due Diligence {OS} and re-run the model on
   them. Label every input that is still unverified.
5. Size the protections: each one names the risk it exists for and its cost to
   the other side.
6. Design the incentives, then model the gamed case for every earnout and every
   metric linked payment.
7. Rank the terms by cash value at the middle exit, and build the trade set and
   the walk away terms.
8. Assemble the term sheet, marked as a draft for legal review, with the parts
   that are typically binding flagged first.
9. Hand the agreed substance to Acquisition {OS} to carry into the room, and
   the structural tax questions to the adviser.
10. When executed documents return, reconcile them line by line and raise every
    difference before completion.
11. Hand the resulting rights and obligations to Board {OS} and the reporting
    obligations to Portfolio Management {OS}.

## Handoffs

| Receives from | What |
|---|---|
| Acquisition {OS} | the offer hypothesis, the price range and every structural question the negotiation raised |
| Due Diligence {OS} | verified findings, and the ones that change the numbers the model runs on |
| Investment Thesis {OS} | the thesis and its kill criteria, which say what the structure must survive |
| Capital {OS} | the approved commitment amount and the policy constraints on instrument and concentration |

| Hands to | What that OS expects |
|---|---|
| the operator's lawyer | the term sheet draft, the flagged binding parts, and the specific drafting questions |
| the tax adviser | the structural tax questions, stated precisely, with the alternatives being compared |
| Acquisition {OS} | agreed terms and the trade set, ready to carry into a negotiation |
| Board {OS} | the rights, reserved matters and information rights that now have to be operated |
| Portfolio Management {OS} | the reporting obligations and the follow on rights the structure creates |
| Capital {OS} | the commitment shape, including any obligation to fund later |
