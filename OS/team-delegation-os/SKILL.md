# Team & Delegation {OS}

Hand work off with a brief precise enough to be checked, an authority level
stated out loud, and check-ins sized to the consequence rather than to anxiety.

## When to use this

Use it when there is more work than capacity, when the same task keeps coming
back wrong, when someone is ready for more responsibility, when a first hire or
a contractor arrives, and before committing someone else's time to a deadline.

Typical openings: I do not have time for this, I tried delegating it and had to
redo it, they keep asking me what to do, I want to give them more, I need to
take this back.

## Capabilities

- Split a load into a delegate list and a keep list, with a reason on each side
  that is not habit.
- Match work to a person and to an authority level justified by evidence.
- Write a brief with the six required parts: outcome, constraints, definition of
  done, do-not-touch, deadline, check-ins.
- State the authority level explicitly: decide alone, decide and tell me,
  propose and wait, or ask first.
- Design check-ins by consequence, agreed in advance, never as surprise
  inspections.
- Accept or correct returned work against the brief only, and name everything
  missing in one pass.
- Give correction that names an observable behaviour and a specific alternative.
- Raise or lower authority on evidence, and say why.
- Recall work cleanly, naming capacity, priority or fit rather than leaving a
  verdict hanging.
- Spot when three identical briefs mean a missing procedure.

## Procedure

1. List the load. Mark each item delegable or not, with the reason.
2. For anything kept, test the reason. "Faster if I do it" is true once and
   false by the fourth time.
3. Match the person, using their evidence on similar work, their load and what
   they want to learn.
4. Set the authority level from consequence and evidence, and write it down.
5. Write the brief: outcome, constraints, definition of done, do-not-touch,
   deadline, check-ins, escalation path.
6. Have the receiver restate the outcome and the definition of done. If they
   cannot, the brief is unfinished.
7. Check in at the agreed points only. Unblock; do not take over.
8. On return, judge against the brief. Accept, or name everything missing in one
   complete list.
9. Give correction tied to the brief and to an observable behaviour.
10. Adjust authority on the evidence, in either direction, and say why.

## Handoffs

| Send to | What | What they expect |
|---|---|---|
| Execution {OS} | returned work and anything coming back to the user | one physical next action and a deadline |
| Project {OS} | work package status and any slip | position and slip in days, on the day it is known |
| Process & SOP {OS} | a brief that has now been written three times | the steps, the decisions and the quality bar |
| Client {OS} | a delegated promise that will be late | who, what, by when, and the recovery |
| Review & Governance {OS} | systemic failure patterns, and policy-crossing authority changes | evidence and the decision requested |
| Context & Memory {OS} | what each person has delivered against a brief | confirmed, inspectable, removable |
