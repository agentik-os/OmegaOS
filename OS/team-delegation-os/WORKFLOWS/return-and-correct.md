# Workflow: return and correct

Produces acceptance against the brief, or one complete list of what is missing,
plus correction that changes the next result rather than only this one.

## Trigger

Delegated work is returned, at a check-in or at the deadline.

## Inputs

- The returned work.
- The brief, including the definition of done and the do-not-touch list.
- The check-in record.
- The history of previous returns from this person on this kind of work.

## Steps

1. **Read the brief before the work.** Judging first and consulting the brief
   afterwards is how taste gets smuggled into acceptance.
2. **Check against the definition of done, item by item.** Each item is met or
   not met. There is no partial credit in this step.
3. **Check the do-not-touch list** separately. A breach there matters even when
   the outcome is good, and it is a conversation about authority rather than
   quality.
4. **Decide: accept, or return once.** If the definition of done is met, accept,
   even if you would have done it differently. Preference is not a defect.
5. **If returning, name everything in one list.** Complete, specific, and tied to
   the brief. A second round with new objections destroys both the deadline and
   the willingness to try again.
6. **Separate brief defects from execution defects.** If the brief did not ask
   for it, its absence is your defect. Say so, and fix the brief for next time.
7. **Give correction on observable behaviour.** What was done, what to do
   instead, and how to tell the difference next time. Never about the person's
   qualities.
8. **Say what was right.** Specifically, not as a softener. People repeat what is
   named, and the unnamed good work quietly disappears.
9. **Record the acceptance** and update the person's evidence for this work type.
   That record is what later justifies an authority change.
10. **Route the consequences.** Accepted work goes back to Project {OS} or
    Execution {OS}. Late work with a client promise goes to Client {OS} the same
    day. Three identical briefs go to Process & SOP {OS}.

## Completion test

- The work was judged against the written definition of done, item by item.
- The result is accept, or one complete list of gaps, never a drip of new
  objections.
- Brief defects have been separated from execution defects and named as such.
- Correction names an observable behaviour and a specific alternative.
- The evidence record for this person and this work type has been updated.
- Any client-visible lateness was routed to Client {OS} on the day.

## Failure paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| the work meets the brief but the outcome is poor | accept it, then fix the brief; rejecting on unwritten criteria is the fastest way to stop people trying |
| the same defect appears for the third time | stop correcting individually; it is an SOP problem or a matching problem, and it goes to Process & SOP {OS} or back to `MATCH` |
| the work is very late and unfinished | recover the deadline first through Project {OS} and Client {OS}, and hold the correction conversation separately |
| several people fail on the same work | systemic; route to Operations {OS} rather than delivering the same feedback repeatedly |
