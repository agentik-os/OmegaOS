# Workflow: the first handoff

Produces a brief the receiver can restate, an authority level stated out loud,
and a check-in schedule agreed before the work starts.

## Trigger

Work is about to move to another person for the first time, or a previous
attempt to delegate this work came back wrong.

## Inputs

- The work package and its deadline, usually from Project {OS} or Execution
  {OS}.
- The receiver: what they have delivered before on similar work, their current
  load, what they want to learn.
- The definition of done, borrowed from an SOP or a quality bar where one
  exists.
- The consequence of the work going wrong, and who would notice.

## Steps

1. **Test that it should be delegated at all.** If Operations {OS} would delete
   this work, delegating it only moves the waste. If it is your accountability
   and nobody else can carry it, keep it and say why.
2. **Choose the person on evidence**, not on availability alone. Availability
   picks the person who is behind on everything.
3. **Set the authority level** from consequence and evidence: decide alone,
   decide and tell me, propose and wait, or ask first.
4. **Write the outcome, not the steps.** What is true when this is finished, in
   one sentence, described from the point of view of whoever consumes it.
5. **Write the definition of done.** Checkable by someone else. If an SOP exists,
   point at it instead of restating it.
6. **Write the constraints:** budget, tools, people they may involve, deadlines
   that are not theirs to move.
7. **Write the do-not-touch list.** What must not change, who must not be
   contacted, what must not be spent. This is the shortest section and prevents
   the most expensive surprises.
8. **Agree the check-ins,** placed where a correction is still cheap: after the
   first meaningful decision, not the day before the deadline. Agree them, so
   they are not experienced as inspection.
9. **Write the escalation path:** what to do when blocked, who to reach, and how
   long to wait before reaching them.
10. **Have the receiver restate the outcome and the definition of done in their
    own words.** If the restatement is wrong, the brief is unfinished, and this
    is the cheapest possible moment to discover it.
11. **Record the handoff:** the item leaves Execution {OS} and becomes a tracked
    promise with a return date.

## Completion test

- The brief has all six parts: outcome, constraints, definition of done,
  do-not-touch, deadline, check-ins.
- The authority level is written and the receiver can say what they may decide
  alone.
- The receiver restated the outcome and the definition of done correctly.
- Check-ins are agreed and placed where correction is still cheap.
- The escalation path is known before it is needed.
- The delegator's own load actually decreased.

## Failure paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| no definition of done can be written | do not delegate; nobody understands the task well enough yet |
| the receiver's restatement is wrong twice | rewrite the brief rather than explaining it again; the defect is in the writing |
| the delegator wants to specify every step | that is not delegation; either write an SOP with Process & SOP {OS} or keep the task |
| the receiver has no evidence on this kind of work | lower the authority level, add one early check-in, and say plainly that both are temporary |
