# Workflow: the authority review

Produces a deliberate change of authority level, in either direction, justified
by evidence and communicated to the person.

## Trigger

The review cadence fires, or evidence accumulates: several clean deliveries at
the current level, or a decision made outside the granted authority.

## Inputs

- The authority level currently held, per work type.
- The acceptance records since the last review.
- Check-in records: how often the person needed unblocking, and on what.
- Any breach of the do-not-touch list, and its context.
- What the person says they want to own next.

## Steps

1. **Review per work type, not per person.** Authority on client communication
   and authority on spending are different questions with different evidence.
2. **Count clean deliveries at the current level.** Accepted on the first return,
   inside the deadline, no do-not-touch breach.
3. **Count the interventions.** Every time the delegator had to decide something
   the receiver could not, note whether the block was authority, information or
   capability. Those three have different answers, and only the first one is
   fixed by raising authority.
4. **Consider raising** when clean deliveries accumulate and the interventions
   were authority blocks. Raise one level, not two.
5. **Consider lowering** after a decision outside the granted authority with a
   real consequence, or a pattern of returns. Lower one level, say exactly what
   would raise it again, and set the date it is reviewed.
6. **Never lower silently.** Quietly checking work more often without saying so
   is the most corrosive move available, and it is always noticed.
7. **Say it directly**, with the evidence and with what changes in practice
   tomorrow.
8. **Update the check-in schedule to match.** Higher authority means fewer
   check-ins; that is what makes the raise real rather than ceremonial.
9. **Record the change,** its evidence and its date. If the change crosses a
   policy boundary, such as spending limits or client-facing commitments, route
   it to Review & Governance {OS} for approval first.
10. **Set the next review date.**

## Completion test

- Authority is recorded per person and per work type, with a date.
- Any change is justified by counted evidence, not by impression.
- A lowering was communicated directly, with the specific path back.
- The check-in schedule was updated to match the new level.
- Policy-crossing changes were approved by Review & Governance {OS} before
  taking effect.
- The next review date is set.

## Failure paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| the evidence is thin either way | leave the level unchanged, say so, and set a shorter review interval |
| a single bad outcome under pressure | do not lower on one event; look at whether the brief and the check-ins were adequate first |
| the person wants more authority than the evidence supports | name exactly what evidence would justify it, and give them a bounded task that can produce it |
| the delegator keeps intervening at a level they granted | that is the delegator's defect; either lower the level explicitly or stop intervening |
