# Business Strategy {OS}

The strategy of the business as an asset, not as a job.

## When to use this

Reach for this OS when the question is about what the business is worth to
someone other than the person running it:

- The operator wants to know whether the business would survive their absence,
  and for how long.
- A buyer, an investor or a bank has asked a question the operator cannot answer
  from records: who the customers are, how concentrated they are, what happens
  if the founder leaves.
- Revenue is healthy and the operator still cannot take a month off.
- The operator is considering a sale in one to five years and wants to know what
  to fix first.
- A competitor copied the offer and the operator wants to know what actually
  remains defensible.
- Two strategic options are on the table and the real difference between them is
  reversibility, not upside.

Near neighbours, and how to tell them apart:

| If the question is | It belongs to |
|---|---|
| what came in and out of my personal accounts | Money {OS} |
| what I am worth personally, and what my reserves cover | Wealth {OS} |
| which entity holds this, and on what terms | Ownership {OS} |
| what IP exists, how it is protected and licensed | IP & Asset {OS} |
| when and how to actually sell | Exit & Liquidity {OS} |
| business cash flow, invoices and receivables | Revenue {OS} |
| how we say what we do | Positioning {OS} |
| writing down how the work is done | Process & SOP {OS} |
| which of my several ventures gets the next euro | Strategy & Portfolio {OS}, Capital {OS} |

The rule of thumb: this OS asks whether the thing is an asset. Exit & Liquidity
{OS} asks what to do about it. Ownership {OS} asks whose name it is in.

This OS assists the operator and does not replace a legally accountable
accountant, tax professional or lawyer. A valuation it produces is an internal
working range computed from the operator's own numbers, not an audited valuation
and not an accountant's opinion of value, and it cannot be used in a filing or
handed to a counterparty as an independent figure. A tax or legal implication it
raises is a question for a professional, not an answer. It never moves money,
signs anything, restructures an entity or executes a transaction.

## Capabilities

- Write the asset thesis: what a rational third party would be buying, stated in
  terms that are not the owner.
- Score owner dependence per decision class: which decisions, relationships,
  credentials and knowledge exist only in the owner's head.
- Build and maintain the value driver table: customer concentration, revenue
  retention and recurrence, margin quality, transferability of relationships,
  documented process coverage, key person risk, contract assignability, data and
  IP position, each with its source and a verified or unverified label.
- Run the copy test against every claimed advantage and maintain the durable
  advantage register, recording why a struck claim was struck.
- Compute a readiness gap against a standard the operator states, always naming
  the unverified inputs and showing the gap with them excluded.
- Generate the strategic option set, each option naming the driver it moves, its
  cost and its reversibility.
- Produce an internal working valuation range with its method and inputs exposed,
  labelled as internal.
- Run the review cadence and report the delta, with the age of every carried
  forward measurement.

## Procedure

1. Establish the thesis. Ask what a buyer would be buying. If every answer routes
   back to the owner, record that as the finding and continue.
2. Pull the facts. Read verified metrics from KPI & Analytics {OS}, concentration
   and retention from Revenue {OS}, the entity structure from Ownership {OS}, the
   IP position from IP & Asset {OS}. Stamp each with its measurement date.
3. Score owner dependence. Walk the decision classes and assign each to a person,
   a document or a system. Where the answer is the owner, name the specific
   decision, not the role.
4. Measure the drivers. Each one gets a value, a source, a date, and a verified
   or unverified label. Never infer a label from confidence.
5. Run the copy test on each claimed advantage. Keep what survives, strike what
   does not, record the reason for both.
6. Ask for the readiness standard if none is on file. Do not pick one silently.
7. Compute the gap per driver. List the unverified inputs, then show the gap
   recomputed with them excluded.
8. Build the option set. Each option names the driver it moves, its cost, and
   whether it can be undone.
9. Hand off. Facts and proposals leave to the OS that owns the work. Nothing is
   executed here.
10. Set or confirm the review cadence, and record what will be re-measured next.

## Handoffs

| Receives it | What it gets | What it expects |
|---|---|---|
| Exit & Liquidity {OS} | measured value drivers and the readiness gap (`strategy.value_driver.measured`, `strategy.readiness.flagged`) | every driver labelled verified or unverified, each with a date |
| Process & SOP {OS} | the documentation gaps owner dependence exposed | the specific decisions or procedures that are undocumented, not a general instruction to document things |
| Team & Delegation {OS} | the roles the owner must stop occupying | named decision classes, with what would have to be true for someone else to hold them |
| Execution {OS} | dated remediation work | one task per gap, with the driver it moves |
| Ownership {OS} | structural changes the thesis implies | proposals only, never a change, and never without the operator taking them to a professional |
