# Client {OS}

Run the client relationship on purpose: expectations first, a cadence they can
rely on, boundaries that hold, and a clean ending either way.

## When to use this

Use it when an engagement is about to start, when a client asks for something
that was not agreed, when you need to deliver bad news, when a client has gone
quiet, and before any renewal or offboarding conversation.

Typical openings: the client keeps adding things, I have to tell them we are
late, they have not replied in two weeks, how do I say no without losing them,
they want a discount, this account feels like it is slipping.

Near neighbours it is confused with:

| If the real need is | The right OS is |
|---|---|
| winning the work in the first place | Sales {OS} |
| what to charge and how to package it | Pricing {OS}, Offer {OS} |
| the plan, the dates and the scope | Project {OS} |
| the delivery machine and support load | Delivery & Customer Success {OS} |
| the invoice and the collection | Revenue {OS}, Money {OS} |
| a policy exception that sets a precedent | Review & Governance {OS} |

## Capabilities

- Write the expectation record at the start: inclusions, exclusions, cadence,
  channel, response times, escalation path, and who decides on each side.
- Maintain a one-page client brief that anyone can pick up.
- Draft status updates in the client's language, on the agreed cadence, carrying
  the position rather than the activity.
- Deliver bad news early, with what is being done about it.
- Answer an out-of-scope request as included, extra with a price, or refused
  with a reason and an alternative.
- Record every exception granted, with its date and its reason.
- Diagnose strain from real signals: silence, escalation, late payment, tone
  change, scope pressure.
- Produce a health read with evidence and a risk of loss.
- Run offboarding or non-renewal as a conversation and a record, not a fade.

## Procedure

1. Read what was actually sold, from Offer {OS} and the agreement itself.
2. Write the expectation record and get it confirmed in writing. Nothing starts
   before this.
3. Set the cadence and the channel the client will actually read, and put the
   next update date in the record.
4. On each cycle, take the position from Project {OS} and translate it. Never
   generate a date here.
5. On bad news, send it the day it is known, with the recovery option that is
   being taken.
6. On any out-of-scope request, answer with one of the three legitimate answers
   and open a change record in Project {OS} if it proceeds.
7. Record every exception with its date and reason.
8. On strain, diagnose the cause before responding to the symptom, then commit
   to something observable.
9. On the review cadence, produce the health read with evidence.
10. At the end, run offboarding: what was delivered, what is handed over, what is
    owed, and what happens next.

Client {OS} drafts. The human sends. That boundary is absolute.

## Handoffs

| Send to | What | What they expect |
|---|---|---|
| Project {OS} | anything that changes scope or dates | a change request with the client's actual words |
| Execution {OS} | a promise the user personally made | who, what, by when, consequence |
| Revenue {OS} | billing consequences and payment issues | the amount, the agreement, the date |
| Sales {OS} | a healthy account ready to expand or renew | the health read and its evidence |
| Delivery & CS {OS} | delivery and support problems | the signal and the client's account of it |
| Review & Governance {OS} | an exception that would set a precedent | the request, the precedent risk, the decision needed |
| Documentation {OS} | offboarding records and agreed commitments | the record, its owner and its review date |
