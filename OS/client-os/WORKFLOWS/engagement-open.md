# Workflow: opening the engagement

Produces the expectation record the whole relationship will be measured against,
confirmed in writing before any work starts.

## Trigger

The work is agreed. Sales {OS} has finished; delivery has not started.

## Inputs

- What was sold: the offer, the price, the inclusions and exclusions, in the
  words the client agreed to.
- The client's decider, and everyone else who will have opinions.
- The scope statement and dates from Project {OS}.
- The delivery cadence the team can actually sustain.

## Steps

1. **Read the agreement, not the memory of it.** The expectation record is built
   from what was signed and what was said in the sales conversation.
2. **State what they bought** in one paragraph, in their words, describing the
   outcome rather than the activity.
3. **State what they did not buy.** The exclusion list is the half that prevents
   nine months of friction. Include the things a reasonable client would assume.
4. **Set the cadence and the channel.** How often they hear from you, on which
   channel, and by which day. Choose a cadence the team can sustain in a bad
   week, not a good one.
5. **Set response times.** How fast a normal question is answered, and how fast
   an urgent one is, with a definition of urgent.
6. **Name the deciders.** Who decides on their side, who decides on yours, and
   what happens when the two disagree.
7. **Set the escalation path** on both sides, before anybody needs it.
8. **Name the red lines.** What is refused regardless of price, and what would
   end the engagement.
9. **Send it for written confirmation.** The human sends. Work does not start
   until the client has confirmed.
10. **Create the account brief** and register it so anyone who picks up the
    account is not starting from zero.

## Completion test

- The expectation record exists and the client has confirmed it in writing.
- The exclusion list is not empty.
- The cadence, the channel and the next update date are set.
- Response times and the definition of urgent are stated.
- Deciders and escalation paths exist on both sides.
- The one-page account brief exists and is reachable by the team.

## Failure paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| the client will not confirm in writing | send the record as a statement of understanding, note the absence of confirmation in the brief, and treat every scope question as high risk |
| the sales conversation promised something the delivery team cannot do | surface it now, before work starts; renegotiating on day one is cheap and on day sixty is not |
| the client wants a cadence the team cannot sustain | offer the sustainable cadence plus an escalation channel for urgency, and say why |
| nobody on the client side will own decisions | that is the top risk in the register; record it and escalate before starting |
