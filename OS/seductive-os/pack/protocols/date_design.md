# Protocol: Date Design

Designing a first or early date that is actually worth having, for both people. Run consent_check.md before any output here. The design goal is not to engineer an outcome, it is to build a context where two people can find out whether they like each other, cheaply, with a clean exit available to both.

The single most common design error is the expensive date. Cost, length and formality all raise the price of the other person's honesty. A four-hour dinner with a booking and a bill makes leaving early socially impossible, which means the user has bought politeness and cannot tell it apart from interest.

## Steps
1. Run the consent gate. A date proposed to someone who cannot refuse cheaply is not fixed by better design.
2. Establish what is actually known about them, from what they offered in conversation only. Do not research them. Searching an employer, an address, a family or an old account before a first date is surveillance, it reliably surfaces in conversation, and it reads exactly as what it is.
3. Choose the format by cost, not by impressiveness. First meeting: 60 to 90 minutes, daytime or early evening, somewhere with a natural end. Coffee, a walk, a drink, a market, an exhibition. The natural end is the feature.
4. Build in the ability to talk. Loud venues, cinemas and anything with a performance in front of it defer the actual question to a second date. They are second-date formats.
5. Give the activity something to look at that is not each other. A shared third thing (a place, an exhibit, a route, food being made) carries the conversation through the flat patches every first meeting has, and it lets both people look away without it meaning anything.
6. Set the logistics so both people are independently safe and independently mobile: a public place, each person arriving and leaving under their own power, neither dependent on the other for a lift, a route home, or a key. Never their home or the user's on a first meeting. Never a place either person cannot leave.
7. State the frame plainly when proposing: what, where, when, how long. "Coffee at 4 on Saturday, I have to be somewhere at 6" is a complete proposal with an exit built into it for both people.
8. Build the exit ramps, plural, and build them for both sides. See the table below. A date with no exit ramp is a trap wearing a nice shirt, and the person with less power in the situation feels it first.
9. Place the consent checkpoints. Three of them: at the proposal (is this easy to decline in one sentence), at the midpoint (are they adding to this or maintaining it), and at any escalation, physical or logistical, including extending the date. Each is a read plus a decision, not a question read off a card.
10. Handle escalation in increments the other person can answer. Move partway and let them close the distance, slow down and see whether they follow, ask plainly where the stakes are real. Asking works and does not kill anything worth keeping. Withdrawal at any stage, including after a yes, including mid-way, stops everything immediately with no sulking, no interrogation, no extraction of reassurance.
11. End it deliberately, at or before the planned time, ideally while it is still good. Ending on time is a signal of self-respect and it makes the next invitation easy to answer honestly.
12. Debrief once with interaction_debrief.md, inside the bound.

## Format selection
| Constraint | Format | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First meeting from an app | 45 to 75 minutes, daytime, public, one drink or a coffee | Neither person yet knows if the other is who they said. Short and public is the respectful default, not a lack of effort |
| First meeting from real life | 60 to 90 minutes, a shared third thing | Some rapport already exists, so the format can carry more |
| Second meeting | Longer, an activity with a story in it | The question is now compatibility, which needs something to happen |
| Either person is nervous | A walk | Side by side, moving, no forced eye contact, no bill, endable at any corner |
| Either person is partnered or the structure is negotiated | Ask what has been agreed before designing anything | The OS works within the agreement the user reports, and does not help conceal |

## Exit ramps, for both people
- A stated end time at the proposal, which gives both sides a costless exit that needs no excuse.
- Separate arrival and separate departure, always.
- No bill large enough that leaving early would feel like theft. Split by default unless a different arrangement is genuinely comfortable for both.
- Nothing pre-booked that cannot be abandoned without loss.
- An explicit, cheerful acceptance if they end it early. Someone leaving early has told the user something useful, and the correct response is warmth, not a repair attempt.
- The user's own exit ramp too. The user is also allowed to not be interested, to say so kindly, and to leave. This half is routinely missing from the genre.

## Stop rules
- No expensive gestures, no surprises the other person has to manage, no venue chosen to impress, no fake constraint on time. A grand gesture removes the freedom to decline and replaces it with a performance.
- No alcohol-dependent design. If the plan only works with several drinks in it, the plan is not the problem, and it routes to approach_anxiety_ladder.md.
- Never escalate with someone significantly intoxicated. Exchange details, leave, follow up sober. If the interest was real it survives the night, and a sober yes is worth more anyway.
- Never their home or the user's on a first meeting, in either direction, however the invitation is framed.
- No isolation of any kind: not moving the person away from their group, not to a second venue they do not know, not anywhere they cannot easily leave.
- A cancelled or postponed date with no new time offered is an answer. Route to post_rejection_debrief.md rather than proposing an alternative.
- Do not plan the second date during the first. It converts a good evening into a negotiation and prices the other person's honesty out of the conversation.

## Required closure
- Decision or output: the format, the venue class, the time, the stated duration and the exit ramps, plus the three consent checkpoints.
- Owner: the user proposes it and both people decide.
- Observable completion evidence: the invitation was made in one sentence, easy to decline, and the answer was taken as given.
- Review trigger: after the date, once, through interaction_debrief.md inside the bound.
- Memory and handoff instruction: first name at most in any record, no venue detail that identifies where a real person spends time, only with the user's consent. Route a no or a stall to post_rejection_debrief.md, and questions about exclusivity, pacing or a relationship decision to Alignment OS.
