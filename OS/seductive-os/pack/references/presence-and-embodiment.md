## 1. What presence is

Presence is where attention is pointed. It is not charisma, mood, energy, or a quality some people were born holding. It is the share of attention pointed at the world rather than at the self being observed.

```text
Attention budget in any interaction, 100 units:
  OUTWARD   what they said, their face, the room, the actual content
  INWARD    how am I doing, what do I look like, what do I say next
  PRESENCE  = the outward share
```

Self-focused attention is the mechanism behind most of what users describe as awkwardness, and reducing it is associated with lower social anxiety and better performance in social tasks **[E2]**. The practical consequence is the whole reason this document exists first: you cannot fix inward attention by adding more things to monitor. Every technique below is trained outside interactions so that inside them there is nothing to run.

Operational test, usable immediately: after any conversation, can the user name three specific things the other person said, unprompted? Three means they were there. Zero means they were watching themselves.

## 2. Presence versus confidence

| | Confidence | Presence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A prediction that things will go well | An attention placement |
| Where it comes from | Accumulated evidence and survived reps | A decision made in the moment |
| Availability today | Not directly, it lags the evidence | Immediately, on a bad day, while afraid |
| Trainable | Indirectly, through reps and recovery | Directly, as a skill |
| Failure when absent | Hesitation, avoidance | Absence, the other person talking to nobody |

Coach presence first because it is the one that pays today. Confidence is largely a downstream result of reps and survived outcomes **[E3]**, which is why training it head on produces affirmations rather than change. See [inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md) for the self-worth layer and [rejection-resilience.md](rejection-resilience.md) for the evidence layer.

A person can be terrified and fully present. That combination is common, it is workable, and it is the realistic target for a nervous user. Do not sell calm.

## 3. State: what actually shifts a nervous system

Arousal is physiology, and it responds to physical inputs faster than to reasoning. None of these require belief, which is why they work on a user who thinks the whole exercise is silly **[E3]**.

| Input | Do this | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breath | Exhale roughly twice as long as the inhale, three to six cycles. Long exhale, unforced | **[E2]** slow-paced breathing reduces acute arousal in most people, effect sizes modest |
| Orienting | Slowly turn the head and let the eyes actually land on four or five things in the room | **[E3]** from somatic practice, no strong trial base |
| Ground contact | Weight into both feet, unlock the knees, feel the floor. Standing on one hip is a hiding posture | **[E3]** |
| Temperature | Cold water on the wrists or face before entering | **[E2]** for acute arousal reduction |
| Movement | Ten minutes of walking or stairs before a social event | **[E2]** acute exercise improves mood and reduces state anxiety |
| Voice | Hum or speak aloud once before entering, so the first words in the room are not the first of the day | **[E3]** |

Two coaching notes. These shift arousal, not worth: a calmer body does not mean the user is more valuable, and framing it that way installs a dependency. And a user who cannot bring arousal down at all before every social contact is not lacking a technique, they are describing something closer to [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md), possibly **[C]**.

## 4. Posture and space, without the mythology

State the replication problem out loud, because the user has almost certainly met the folk version. The claim that expansive postures change testosterone and cortisol failed to replicate; the remaining self-report and behavioural effects are weaker and contested **[E2]**. Do not sell a hormone story. The alpha and beta taxonomy is not a real description of humans and the OS does not use it ([refusals.md](refusals.md) tier 3).

What survives the debunking is smaller and still worth training:

- A collapsed chest restricts breath, and restricted breath thins the voice. That is mechanical, not mystical.
- Taking normal space is a comfort signal to the people around you. A person visibly making themselves small makes others subtly responsible for them.
- Relaxation reads. Tension reads. People calibrate to the nervous system in front of them **[E3]**.

Three states, and only one is the target:

```text
COLLAPSED   chest in, shoulders forward, chin down, weight on one hip, arms crossing the body
BRACED      shoulders up and back hard, chest out, jaw set, chin high, no movement
RELAXED     shoulders down and back, spine long without effort, chin level, weight on both feet,
            able to move without a reset
```

Braced is what a user produces the first week after being told to fix their posture, and it reads as effort, which is worse than slouching. Coach relaxed, and coach it as something that happens before the room rather than during it.

## 5. Speed, stillness and the anxious tell

Speed of movement is one of the most legible anxiety signals **[E3]**. The specific tells:

- Micro-adjustments: the hair, the sleeve, the glass rotated, the phone flipped, the weight shifted every few seconds.
- The nod loop, nodding continuously while someone talks, which reads as wanting the turn to end.
- Laughing before the joke lands, or laughing at the end of one's own sentence.
- Speaking on the inhale, starting before the other person has finished.
- Walking faster than the room, arriving at a group already talking.

Stillness is the correction, and stillness is not rigidity. Rigidity is braced. The trainable version: let one movement finish before the next begins, and allow small stretches of doing nothing. A person who can be still without discomfort is easy to be near, and the ease is the whole product.

## 6. Eye contact as a graded skill

Treat this as a ladder, not a binary, and let the user find the rung that is a stretch rather than a performance.

```text
0  none. Eyes down, or scanning the room for exits
1  contact at handoff points only: the start of your turn, the end of theirs
2  contact while THEY speak, breaking away while YOU speak to think
3  comfortable contact through most of the exchange, breaks sideways or forward
4  sustained contact during a moment that carries weight, face soft
5  staring: contact held past the other person's comfort, face still, no blink
```

Level 3 is the working target for most people. Level 4 belongs to moments, not conversations. Level 5 is the standard overcorrection from a user who has just been told to hold eye contact more, and it is worse than level 1 because it transfers discomfort onto the other person, who now has to manage it.

Two details that carry most of the improvement:

- **Break sideways or forward, not down.** Breaking downward reads as submission or shame; breaking sideways reads as thinking **[E3]**.
- **Soften the face.** Sustained contact with a tense face is intensity, and intensity is not warmth.

Variance is real and large **[P]**. Direct sustained eye contact ranges from expected to rude across cultures, many autistic people find it costly or painful and offer engagement in other channels, and level 3 is not a universal standard of anything. Never coach eye contact as a moral property. If it costs the user significant capacity, spend that capacity on listening instead and let contact sit at level 2. See [social-calibration.md](social-calibration.md) section 10.

## 7. Voice

Voice carries more of the impression than most users expect and is one of the more trainable channels, because it responds to breath and can be drilled alone.

| Failure | What it sounds like | The correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No breath support | Thin, from the top of the chest, fades at the end of sentences | Speak on a full breath from lower in the body. Read aloud daily |
| Rising terminal | Statements end higher, turning them into requests for permission | Land the last word slightly lower. One sentence at a time **[E3]** |
| Speed | Fast, unbroken, no gaps, sentences chained with "and" | One idea, stop, breathe |
| Volume too low | Forces a second "sorry, what?", which the user then pays for socially | Calibrate to the far end of the group, not to your own ears |
| Volume too high | Broadcast register in a quiet room | Match the room's baseline, then sit one notch under |
| Flat monotone | Everything in the same register, no emphasis | Let interest change the pitch. This is a symptom of absence, treat section 1 |

On rising terminals, be honest about the limit: in some dialects and regions, rising intonation on statements is simply the dialect and carries no submission meaning at all **[P]**. Coach it only where the user's own recording sounds to them like asking permission. Never coach a user out of their own accent.

The volume note is the one users resist and the one that pays fastest. Being asked to repeat yourself three times costs far more than being slightly louder than you would prefer.

## 8. The pause

The pause is where most presence is won, and it is almost entirely a tolerance skill rather than a technique.

Say the thing. Stop. Do not add the qualifier, the joke that softens it, or the second version of the same sentence. The silence after you speak belongs to the other person, and filling it is the clearest available signal that you are managing their reaction.

The same tolerance applies before speaking: two seconds of thought before an answer reads as consideration, not as a gap to be plugged. A user who fills every silence is not being generous, they are relieving their own discomfort, and the other person is left with nowhere to enter.

Drill in section 17. Track it as a count, not a feeling: how many times in one conversation did you stop and not add anything.

## 9. Hands

The rule is simple: hands either do something or rest. The visible problem is hands that are half-doing something.

- Gesture inside the body's own width, at chest height. Gesture when the content wants it, not as decoration.
- Resting is legitimate: at the sides, one in a pocket, around a glass held at waist height rather than at the chest like a shield.
- Both hands in pockets combined with rounded shoulders is the classic hiding posture. Either alone is fine.
- Arms crossed is not a crime and is often just comfortable, but combined with a step back and a scan it becomes part of a cluster ([social-calibration.md](social-calibration.md) section 4).
- A phone in the hand is a permanent exit sign, held up for everyone. Pocket it before entering, and leave it there.

## 10. Touch as presence, not escalation

This section covers only the quality of contact. All questions of whether, when, and how far belong to [flirtation-and-escalation.md](flirtation-and-escalation.md) and are gated by [ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md).

Three presence properties distinguish contact that lands from contact that reads as a probe **[E3]**:

1. **Complete.** Contact given full attention, not delivered while looking elsewhere. A touch made while scanning away is felt as a test, because it is one.
2. **Unhurried.** Contact that arrives and leaves at the same speed as the rest of the person's movement. Fast in, fast out is the signature of nerves.
3. **Unambiguous in register.** A hand on a forearm during a shared laugh is legible. The same hand that lingers a beat past the laugh has changed the question without asking it, which is the moment calibration is owed.

Two hard notes. No touching at all is a fully valid default and many excellent interactions contain none. And touch norms vary enormously by culture, context and person **[P]**: what is unremarkable in one setting is an intrusion in another, and the correct source of truth is the person in front of the user, read in real time.

## 11. Entering a room

The entry sets a read that takes twenty minutes to revise, mostly because the user's own state at the door persists.

```text
1  Stop at the threshold. One breath. Feet still. This costs two seconds and nobody notices it.
2  Orient. Look around and actually see the room before moving into it.
3  Choose a destination. Move to a specific place at a normal walking pace.
4  Arrive and stop. Do not keep drifting.
```

The failure pattern is entering mid-scan: crossing the room while still deciding, at speed, ending at the phone, the bar or the wall. That sequence is legible from across the room, and it also leaves the user's attention inward for the first ten minutes, which is exactly the window where the room forms its read.

## 12. Warmth versus appeasement

This distinction matters more than any other item in this document, and most users have never had it named.

**Warmth** is: I am glad to be here, I am glad you are here, and I am not asking you for anything. It shows in a smile that involves the eyes, an unhurried face, and an open posture that does not tilt toward the other person. Observers distinguish a smile that engages the eyes from one that does not with reasonable reliability **[E2]**, though the popular framing overstates how cleanly the two separate.

**Appeasement** is a submission display, and it reads as one. Its markers cluster:

- eyebrows raised and held, chin tucked down
- a smile held past the moment that produced it
- laughter arriving before the joke lands, or attached to the user's own sentences
- apology as punctuation ("sorry, can I just", "sorry, quick question")
- a body angled up and in, head tilted, taking less space as the other person takes more
- agreement produced faster than thought

Appeasement is not warmth turned up. It is a different signal with a different meaning, and people receive it accurately: they either take charge of the interaction or quietly withdraw from it. It is also a self-respect problem before it is a body problem, so route persistent appeasement to [inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md) rather than drilling the face.

The correction is not to become cold. It is to separate the two channels: keep the warmth, remove the request. A useful check for the user: after each smile, ask whether it was a response to something or a bid for permission.

## 13. What degrades presence

| Degrader | Mechanism | The honest note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alcohol | Lowers self-monitoring, which feels exactly like presence from the inside, while degrading signal reading, memory and calibration | The subjective gain is real, the capability loss is real, and the second one is invisible to the user in the moment |
| Phone | Splits attention and broadcasts an exit. Even face down on the table it measurably occupies attention **[E2]** | Pocket, not table |
| Sleep debt | Degrades mood, attention and self-regulation **[E1]**; effects on reading other people's emotional expressions are real but thinner **[E2]** | The cheapest presence intervention available is sleep. Route to Health and Energy OS |
| Self-monitoring loops | Camera checking, mirror checking, rehearsing lines in the room | Adds an inward channel at the exact moment outward attention is needed |
| Caffeine overshoot | Converts baseline nerves into visible activation: speed, volume, micro-movement | Halve it before a high-arousal social event, do not add to it |

The alcohol row deserves an explicit coaching move. A user who reports that they can only relax socially after two drinks has found a real bottleneck, and the bottleneck is not the bar. Route to [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md), and if alcohol has become the precondition for social contact, label **[C]** and route out ([safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md)).

## 14. Diagnosis: which presence failure is this

Ask the user to describe a recent interaction, then classify. Do not treat more than one at a time.

| Failure | Reported as | Observable | First intervention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Absent** | "I go blank", "I do not remember what they said" | Cannot recall three specifics | Section 1, outward attention drills |
| **Braced** | "I feel stiff", "I am trying to hold myself right" | Held posture, still face, no movement | Section 4, drop the effort before entering |
| **Fast** | "I get manic", "I talk too much" | Speed, micro-movements, interrupting | Sections 3 and 5, exhale plus pause drill |
| **Collapsed** | "I feel small", "I shrink" | Chest in, low volume, breaks eye contact downward | Sections 4 and 7, then inner game |
| **Appeasing** | "People take over", "I am too nice" | Section 12 cluster | Section 12, then [inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md) |
| **Performing** | "I am on, but nothing connects" | Broadcast register, no reception, high energy | Section 1, listening drills only, cut output |

Performing is the failure most likely to be misread as success, including by the user. High output with zero reception is absence at a higher volume.

## 15. The 90-second pre-entry protocol

Run before a party, a date, an interview, a call, or walking into a bar. It is short on purpose: a long ritual will not be run.

```text
0:00  STOP     outside the door, or in the car, or in the stairwell. Feet still.
0:05  EXHALE   three breaths, exhale about twice the length of the inhale, unforced
0:25  GROUND   weight into both feet, unlock the knees, feel the floor through the shoes
0:40  ORIENT   look around, let the eyes land on four things, name them silently
0:60  DROP     shoulders down and back, unclench the jaw, tongue off the roof of the mouth
0:70  INTENT   one sentence, out loud if possible: what am I here to pay attention to
0:85  ENTER    normal pace, to a specific place, phone in the pocket
```

The INTENT line is the load-bearing one and it is always outward. "Notice three specific things about whoever I talk to" works. "Be charming" does not, because it points attention back at the self.

## 16. Practice tiers

| Tier | Cost | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Floor** | 2 to 5 minutes, alone, survives a bad day | Exhale ladder, one wall posture reset, read one page aloud |
| **Standard** | The normal week | Floor daily, plus the 90-second protocol before every social contact, plus two person-required drills from section 17, plus one phone-free social hour |
| **Deep** | Optional stretch, never the minimum | One level-3 eye contact conversation with a stranger, one recorded three-minute monologue reviewed for terminal pitch and pace, one social event entered fully sober and present |

Cap at one presence practice per cycle, per the SKILL doctrine. A user running six drills is running a monitoring checklist, which is the failure described in section 18.

## 17. Drills alone, drills that need a person

**Alone**

1. **Exhale ladder.** Six breaths, exhale roughly double the inhale. Note arousal before and after on a 0 to 10 scale. Two weeks of data tells the user whether this works for their body, which is better than trusting the claim **[E2]**.
2. **Wall reset.** Stand with heels, hips, upper back and head against a wall for thirty seconds. Step away and keep 70 percent of it. This teaches the difference between relaxed and braced through contrast.
3. **Read aloud, three minutes.** Any text. Full breath, land the last word of each sentence slightly lower. Record it once a week.
4. **The stop drill.** Say one true sentence out loud into a recorder. Stop. Count two seconds. Do not add anything. Ten reps. Most users cannot do five without adding a qualifier, and hearing that is the lesson.
5. **Orienting walk.** Ten minutes outdoors, no phone, no audio, eyes up, letting attention land on things at a distance. This is attention training, not a walk.

**Needs a person, low stakes, not approaching**

6. **Three specifics.** In any conversation, including with colleagues and family, recall three specific things they said afterwards. Log the count. This is the master drill for section 1.
7. **Level 2 to 3 with staff.** Eye contact at handoff points with a cashier or barista, plus one full sentence. Nothing else. Note carefully: this is a presence rep, never an approach. Their warmth is a working condition ([ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 7).
8. **Volume calibration.** In a group, aim your voice at the person furthest from you. Ask a friend afterwards whether it was loud, right or quiet.
9. **The pause, live.** Once per conversation, finish a sentence and let two seconds pass. Count how many times you managed it.
10. **Phone-free hour.** One social hour with the phone in a pocket or bag, not on the table. Log what changed.

Debrief every person-required rep through [interaction_debrief.md](../protocols/interaction_debrief.md), and put the weekly shape together with [weekly_practice.md](../protocols/weekly_practice.md).

## 18. How presence training goes wrong

One failure mode, and it is common enough to state as a standing warning: presence training becomes another self-monitoring channel. The user enters a conversation now checking their shoulders, their terminal pitch, their eye contact level and their hands, which is four new inward channels, which is less presence than they started with.

The rule that prevents it:

```text
Train the body OUTSIDE the interaction.
INSIDE the interaction, hold exactly one instruction, and it always points outward.
```

Rotate the single instruction weekly. Week one: recall three specifics. Week two: stop after one sentence. Week three: notice what they seem to want out of this conversation. Never two at once, and never a body cue as the in-interaction instruction.

Second failure mode: measuring presence by how the interaction went. Process only, per the SKILL scorecard. A grounded, attentive interaction that ended in a polite no is a successful rep.

## 19. Route out

Label **[C]** and route to a qualified professional, via [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md), when the user reports:

- distress about their body, appearance or a specific feature that dominates their attention, mirror or camera checking, or avoidance of being seen. This is not a style problem and coaching it as one causes harm.
- panic attacks, derealisation, or dissociating during or after social contact.
- social anxiety at a level that prevents work, study, or leaving the house.
- alcohol or another substance as a precondition for social contact.
- shaking, freezing or collapse that does not respond to any of section 3.

Stop the presence work, name it plainly and without alarm, and say what a practice plan is not. Everything below that threshold, including a great deal of ordinary fear, is trainable here and in [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md).
