# Attraction fundamentals

The honest model of what makes a person compelling, and a clear statement of what the evidence does and does not support. Read this before running `AUDIT` or `FULL_BUILD`. It is the document that stops a user spending a year on the wrong factor.

## 1. How to use this document

Two jobs. First, it holds the model the whole OS is built on, so that every practice can be traced to a factor rather than to folklore. Second, it is the corrective document: users arrive with a dense set of confident beliefs about attraction absorbed from a genre that has almost no evidentiary discipline, and a large part of coaching is subtraction.

Be honest about the epistemics as you go. Most of this domain is **[E3]** craft and **[P]** taste. A handful of findings are genuinely well replicated and they are named in section 4. Laundering craft into science makes the OS untrustworthy the first time a user checks, and users check.

## 2. The seven factors

```text
MAGNETISM = PRESENCE × WARMTH × SELF-RESPECT × CURIOSITY × COMPETENCE × CALIBRATION × CONSENT
```

Multiplicative on purpose. A zero anywhere zeroes the product, which is a better model of real experience than a sum: the extremely competent person with no warmth is not seven tenths of the way there, they are stuck. CONSENT sits inside the equation rather than outside it as a filter, because an interaction the other person is not free in produces nothing worth having ([ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 1).

The factors are not equally weighted for every person or every context, and the OS does not pretend to know the weights. What it can do reliably is find the factor that is near zero, which is usually obvious once someone looks, and which is almost never the factor the user came in worried about.

## 3. Factor by factor: present and absent

| Factor | Present, it looks like | Absent, it looks like | Trained in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Presence** | Attention outward, unhurried, remembers what you said, easy to be near | Talking to someone who is elsewhere. Fast, monitoring, blank afterwards | [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md) |
| **Warmth** | Glad you are here, generous with attention, not asking for anything | Cold, evaluating, transactional. Or appeasing, which is not warmth | [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md) section 12 |
| **Self-respect** | Has positions, states preferences, can be disappointed without collapsing, leaves when leaving is right | Supplication, over-accommodation, tolerating what should not be tolerated, resentment underneath | [inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md) |
| **Curiosity** | Genuinely interested, asks the follow-up, remembers, finds people worth knowing | The interview, or waiting for a turn to speak. Interest as a technique | [conversation-craft.md](conversation-craft.md) |
| **Competence** | Good at something real, and it shows without being displayed | Nothing being built, nothing being learned, no domain of mastery | Section 8 |
| **Calibration** | Reads what is there, adjusts, stops at the first clear signal | Pressure (over-reading) or chronic ambiguity (under-reading) | [social-calibration.md](social-calibration.md) |
| **Consent** | The other person is free, relaxed, and choosing. Their no is cheap | Persistence, ambiguity used as cover, an anxious yes accepted as a yes | [ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) |

Coaching note on warmth and self-respect: they fail together more often than separately, in the specific pattern where a user tries to buy warmth with self-abandonment. That combination reads as neither warm nor safe, and it is the most common profile in this pack.

## 4. What the evidence actually supports

Label everything. The user will be better served by an honest short list than a long confident one.

| Claim | Status | What it actually means for coaching |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Proximity and repeated exposure** increase liking. The mere-exposure literature is among the more replicated effects in the field | **[E1]** | Be somewhere repeatedly. Recurring contexts (a class, a league, a local, a volunteer shift) outperform one-shot encounters. The single most actionable finding here |
| **Similarity**, particularly in attitudes and values, predicts attraction. The complementarity claim ("opposites attract") has far weaker support | **[E1]** for similarity, **[E2]** against complementarity | Do not hide your positions to be universally palatable. Similarity cannot be found through a person with no stated views |
| **Reciprocity of liking**: learning that someone likes you tends to increase liking of them | **[E1]** | Legible interest is not weakness, it is one of the better-supported mechanisms available. The caveat matters: this is about selective liking. Generalized, indiscriminate liking does not carry the same weight **[E2]** |
| **Self-disclosure reciprocity** builds closeness, and structured escalating mutual disclosure reliably produces felt closeness in lab settings | **[E1]** in the lab, **[E2]** for transfer to real dating | The engine of [conversation-craft.md](conversation-craft.md) section 3 |
| **Arousal misattribution** (physiological arousal from another source read as attraction) | **[E2]**, weak and contested. Small samples, alternative explanations, mixed replication | Do not build a "take them somewhere frightening" tactic on it. It is a footnote, not a method |
| **Physical attractiveness** predicts initial attraction strongly at zero acquaintance, and its predictive power declines as people know each other, with idiosyncratic preference growing | **[E2]** | Appearance is a first filter, not the decisive factor most users believe it to be. It matters most in the context where users spend the most time worrying: photos and first seconds |
| **Stated preferences predict actual choices poorly.** Speed-dating and related work repeatedly find weak links between what people say they want and who they choose | **[E2]** | Stop optimizing for a checklist someone announced. Also: the user's own stated type is unreliable evidence about the user |
| **What predicts relationship satisfaction** is mostly relationship-level (perceived commitment, appreciation, conflict handling), not individual trait level | **[E2]** | Everything in this pack is about the first door. It is not the same skill as the rest of the house. See [long-term-desire.md](long-term-desire.md) |

Folk psychology that gets stated as fact and is not: universal sex-based preference rules presented as biology and then as instruction; evolutionary just-so stories deployed prescriptively; the "80/20" app statistic, which comes from platform engagement data and does not mean what it is used to mean; "dread game" and other manufactured-insecurity systems ([refusals.md](refusals.md)); and the underlying assumption that attraction is a stable valuation of a person rather than something that varies by context, mood, timing and pairing.

## 5. The replication problem, stated plainly

Say this to users, once, in plain language: social psychology went through a replication crisis, and attraction research was one of the worst-affected areas. A large number of famous, textbook, TED-stage findings did not survive direct replication or pre-registration. Small samples, undergraduate participants, flexible analysis and publication bias produced a literature that reads far more confidently than it should.

Specific casualties this pack refuses to cite as settled:

- the hormonal claims for expansive posture **[E2]**, addressed in [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md) section 4
- the "93 percent of communication is nonverbal" figure, which is a misquotation of a narrow study about incongruent emotional messages and was never a general claim **[E1]** on the misquote itself
- ego depletion as a general model of self-control
- most social priming results

The consequence for coaching is not nihilism. It is a hierarchy: prefer mechanisms with converging evidence and an obvious causal story (be around people repeatedly, disclose reciprocally, be warm and attentive), treat everything clever as suspect, and label craft knowledge as craft. [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md) holds the ledger of what in this pack rests on what, and it is the right place to send a user who wants to check.

A useful heuristic to hand over: any claim about attraction that is counterintuitive, precise, and easy to turn into a tactic is more likely to be a failed effect than a working one.

## 6. Variance in taste, and why a universal prescription is a red flag

**[P]**, and this is one of the most practically important sections in the document.

The variance between individuals in what they find compelling is enormous, and it is not noise around a universal mean. People differ by culture, subculture, generation, personal history, current life season, and what their last relationship was like. The same trait that ends one interaction is the reason another person stays.

Three consequences:

1. **Any universal prescription is a red flag.** "Women want X." "Men respond to Y." "Never do Z." A source confident about all people is a source that has not talked to many. This includes confident prescriptions from this pack: where something is taste, it is labelled **[P]**.
2. **Selection beats optimization.** A user cannot become maximally attractive to everyone, and attempting it produces a sanded-down person who is nobody's specific preference. The better strategy is to become clearly themselves and let the filter run, which also solves the compatibility problem in the same move.
3. **Rejection carries much less information than users think.** One person's no is mostly information about fit, timing and their evening. Aggregate patterns across many interactions carry signal; single instances rarely do ([rejection-resilience.md](rejection-resilience.md)).

## 7. Changeable versus fixed, and where the effort goes

Users systematically misallocate here. They believe the changeable list is short and appearance-dominated. It is neither.

| Highly changeable, high return | Changeable with effort | Slow or fixed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Presence and attention | Fitness, body composition | Height |
| Warmth in delivery | Wardrobe coherence and fit | Bone structure |
| Listening and follow-up | Voice (breath, pace, volume) | Baseline temperament |
| Grooming, fit of clothes, sleep | Social exposure volume | Age |
| Making interest legible | A domain of real competence | Much of neurotype |
| Calibration accuracy | Rejection tolerance | Disability and health constraints |
| Having positions and stating them | Self-worth architecture | Family and cultural starting point |

The correct allocation, in order **[E3]**:

```text
1  VOLUME       exposure to actual people. Nothing else matters at zero
2  PRESENCE     attention, the fastest-moving factor
3  LEGIBILITY   making interest answerable. Fixes the "fluent but nothing happens" profile
4  BASELINE     sleep, grooming, clothes that fit. Cheap, one-time, real
5  CURIOSITY    the conversational engine
6  COMPETENCE   a real domain. Slow, compounding, transfers to the rest of life
7  APPEARANCE   beyond baseline. Real returns, slowest, most over-weighted by users
```

Note where appearance sits. It is not absent, it is seventh, and its baseline tier (clothes that fit, grooming, sleep) is at position four because it is cheap and one-time. What users usually mean by "I need to look better" is a large slow project, and it is frequently self-worth wearing a mirror ([inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md)). Both can be true. Run [style-and-self-presentation.md](style-and-self-presentation.md) and the inner-game work together rather than sequencing them, and if body distress is running the request, label **[C]** and route via [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md).

## 8. Status, competence and mastery

The genuine finding underneath the folklore is unremarkable: people are drawn to others who are good at things and respected by people who know them. The folklore turns that into a display problem, which inverts it.

| Honest signal | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Actual competence surfacing incidentally, because it came up | The information arrives without a bid attached, so it is believed |
| Other people's genuine regard, visible in how they treat the user | Third-party evidence, which is the only kind that cannot be self-supplied |
| Absorption in the work itself | Absorption is attractive independently of the domain, and it cannot be faked |
| How the user treats people with less power than them | Read fast, weighted heavily, and almost never performed correctly |
| Being learnable: taking correction without defending | Rare, and reads as security rather than deference |

Performed status backfires and it backfires specifically **[E3]**: name-dropping, the story that exists to establish rank, the correcting reflex, rented props, and the resume delivered as conversation. The mechanism is that a status bid announces the need for the status to be granted, which is itself the information. The listener reliably updates in the opposite direction from the one intended, and they usually cannot say why.

Fake status props are refused outright ([refusals.md](refusals.md) section 3): they obtain interest under false pretences, they must be maintained forever, and they guarantee the private conclusion that the user was actually afraid of.

The mastery route is slow and it is the one the OS recommends: get genuinely good at something the user cares about. It produces material, absorption, a social context, a source of self-worth that does not depend on romantic outcomes, and competence that is real. Every one of those is a factor in section 2.

## 9. Warmth and competence, the two fast dimensions

Person perception research consistently finds two dominant dimensions along which people evaluate others quickly, usually labelled warmth and competence (or communion and agency). This is one of the better-replicated descriptive frameworks in social psychology **[E1]**, and the ordering finding, that warmth-type judgements tend to be made faster and weighted more heavily in overall evaluation, is reasonably supported **[E2]**.

What it means, and does not mean:

- It **describes how people are perceived**. It is not a prescription to perform two traits.
- **Competence alone** produces impressive and cold. Admired, not approached. This is the common profile in high-achieving users, and it is why career success so often fails to convert.
- **Warmth alone** produces liked and not desired. Frequently the person others come to for support and not for a date. This profile usually has a self-respect deficit underneath it rather than a warmth surplus, per section 3.
- **Neither** produces invisible, which is where a user with a presence deficit sits.
- The user cannot fix a warmth deficit by adding competence, and this is the single most common misallocation of effort in the entire pack: more achievement applied to a warmth problem.

Diagnostic question that separates them fast: after meeting the user, would a person say "they seemed impressive" or "they seemed nice"? Neither answer alone is the target, and the missing word names the work.

## 10. Vulnerability, correctly understood

Vulnerability has been mangled by the genre into a technique, which destroys it, because a performed vulnerability is a claim rather than an exposure.

Correctly understood, it is: **disclosure of something real and current, sized to the relationship, that costs the user something small right now and leaves the other person free.**

| This is vulnerability | This is not |
| --- | --- |
| "I have no idea what I am doing with this" | A rehearsed origin story about a hard childhood |
| "I was hoping you would say yes and I am a bit thrown" | A catalogue of past relationship wounds on date one |
| "That criticism landed and I have been thinking about it" | Performed openness with nothing at stake |
| An opinion stated without hedging, knowing it might be rejected | Distress delivered as a claim on their attention |

Two tests. **Current:** is this about something live, or is it a curated artefact from the archive? **Free:** after saying it, does the other person still have a choice, or do they now have to manage something?

The trauma-dump failure is worth naming with care and without shaming. It is a calibration error, it usually comes from wanting closeness fast, and it functions socially as a transfer of load regardless of intent. The correction is pacing ([conversation-craft.md](conversation-craft.md) section 3), not silence. And if a user's difficult material is unprocessed enough that it arrives uninvited in early conversations, the right route is support for the material itself, labelled **[C]**, not a pacing rule.

## 11. Having a life

The most frequently converted-into-a-tactic item in the domain, and it stops working the instant it becomes one.

A life with things in it does three separate jobs:

1. **Material.** Section 8 of [conversation-craft.md](conversation-craft.md) needs content, and content is downstream of doing things.
2. **Gravity.** Real commitments create genuine unavailability without any fiction to maintain, which is the honest version of everything manufactured scarcity tries to fake ([refusals.md](refusals.md) section 3). It requires no continuity management because it is true.
3. **Non-dependence.** A person whose week is full does not need a specific outcome from a specific person, and that shows in the delivery of every invitation they make.

The reason it cannot be a tactic: a life assembled to appear full is a schedule, and it produces none of job 3, which is the job that actually reads. The distinction is felt immediately by the other person and eventually by the user.

Practical, for a user whose week is genuinely empty: this is a real finding and not a comment on their worth. Two recurring commitments they would keep even if nobody was watching is a full intervention, and it also solves proximity from section 4. Route the mechanics to Habit Tracker OS and the meaning question to Alignment OS.

## 12. Abundance and scarcity as internal states

Scarcity is not a shortage of options and abundance is not a supply of them. Both are internal states, and plenty of people with many options feel acutely scarce.

| Scarcity shows up as | Abundance shows up as |
| --- | --- |
| Over-investment in one specific outcome | Interest that does not tighten |
| Every interaction read as an audition | Interactions read as interactions |
| Difficulty leaving a conversation or a situation that is not good | Leaving easily, warmly, early |
| Rehearsing, over-preparing, replaying | Being surprised by your own sentences |
| Resentment when interest is not returned | A no that costs an evening, not a self-concept |
| Accepting treatment that should not be accepted | A floor that holds without being announced |

The scarcity state is what most users are actually describing when they say they are "not confident", and it is also what produces the pressure that ends interactions. It is not fixed by more options, which is why volume alone does not resolve it. It is fixed by a self-worth architecture that does not route through romantic outcomes ([inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md)) and by accumulated evidence of surviving a no ([rejection-resilience.md](rejection-resilience.md)).

Note the asymmetry the OS refuses: displaying abundance is a tactic and it is refused. Having it is a capability and it is trained.

## 13. Polarity claims

**[P]**, entirely, and coach it as such.

The masculine and feminine polarity model, in which attraction is generated by a dynamic of leading and receiving, is a taste framework and a subcultural aesthetic. It is not biology, it is not universal, and the confident evolutionary framing usually attached to it is not supported by the strength of evidence it claims.

What is true: **some people find a strongly differentiated dynamic of initiating and receiving compelling, and some do not, and it varies by person, pairing, culture, mood and phase of a relationship.** Some of the most durable pairings run no such dynamic at all.

Coaching rules:

- Never assign a role by gender, and never assume the user's gender, their orientation, or the gender of the person they are interested in.
- Treat any polarity preference as a preference to be discovered and negotiated between two specific people, like any other preference.
- Refuse the prescriptive version, which almost always arrives as a set of behaviour rules addressed to one gender and a set of predictions about another.
- The grain of truth worth keeping is not gendered at all: **differentiation**. Being a distinct person with your own centre, rather than a mirror of whoever you are with, is attractive and is trainable. That is self-respect from section 2, and it is where [long-term-desire.md](long-term-desire.md) picks it up.

## 14. Age, disability, neurodivergence and body diversity

Treat all of these as context, never as deficit, and never as something to be compensated for by working harder on the other factors.

- **The seven factors do not change.** Presence, warmth, self-respect, curiosity, competence, calibration and consent are what they are for everyone.
- **The pool and the channels change.** Where a person meets people, which contexts are workable, which formats suit them. That is a strategy question ([dating-strategy.md](dating-strategy.md), [digital-and-apps.md](digital-and-apps.md)), not a worth question.
- **Do not coach masking as a strategy.** Advising an autistic user to perform allistic signal patterns costs them enormous capacity, degrades presence directly (it is inward attention, per [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md) section 1), and selects for people who like the mask. The better route is legibility: say the thing directly, ask rather than decode, and choose contexts with clearer rules. See [social-calibration.md](social-calibration.md) section 10.
- **Do not treat ageing as a decline curve to be argued with.** The factor mix shifts with age in most people's experience: presence, competence and self-respect become more legible and matter more, novelty matters less **[P]**.
- **Body diversity:** the baseline tier of section 7 (fit, grooming, sleep) is available to everyone and is most of the available return. Beyond it, preferences vary enormously (section 6) and the honest position is that the user's body is not the obstacle they believe it is, while also not pretending preferences do not exist.

Where any of this arrives with real distress attached (dominating attention, avoidance of being seen, self-hatred), it is **[C]**, not coaching. Route via [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md).

## 15. The compounding argument for the slow path

The user's objection to everything above is that it is slow, and they are right. Make the argument for it explicitly, once, because it is the argument that keeps them on the path.

| | The tactical path | The slow path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to first effect | Days | Weeks to months |
| Ceiling | Low. Collapses on contact with anyone paying attention | High, and it keeps rising |
| Maintenance | Permanent, and it grows | None. It is just who they are |
| Transfer | Zero. It works nowhere else in life | Total. Friendship, work, family, everything |
| Selection | Filters for whoever the technique works on | Filters for compatibility |
| Failure mode | Discovery, and the private belief that the real version is unlovable | An ordinary no |
| At year three | Same person, more tired, needs a new set of tactics | A different person, and the problem has dissolved |

The last row is the whole argument. Presence, curiosity, competence and rejection tolerance compound: each rep raises the baseline, the baseline never resets, and after enough of them the original question stops being the question the user is asking. Nothing in the tactical column accumulates, which is why its practitioners are still practising it a decade later.

| "I am successful and it does not translate" | Warmth. Competence is not the missing term | Section 9 |
| "I am not attractive enough" | Sometimes appearance **[P]**, more often scarcity state | Sections 7 and 12, both, together |
| "Nobody is interested in me" | Volume. Often near zero real exposure | Section 7 allocation, then [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md) |
| "I keep getting rejected" | Sometimes nothing. Base rates are brutal and normal | [rejection-resilience.md](rejection-resilience.md), then re-check volume |
| "I am too nice" | Self-respect, not excess warmth. They are not the same term | [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md) section 12 |

Name the bottleneck out loud with a confidence level and state what evidence would change your mind, per the SKILL response contract. Then say what is not the problem, which is load-bearing: most users are working hard on a factor that is already fine, and telling them to stop is often the single most valuable output of a session.
