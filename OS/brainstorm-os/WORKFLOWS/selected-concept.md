# Workflow: Selected concept

**Produces:** one concept chosen out of a population that was genuinely
generated, genuinely attacked, and genuinely compared, with the dissent recorded
beside the recommendation.

## Trigger

An idea exists, a choice is due, and nobody has yet produced more than one real
option. Also triggered when a prior session converged so fast that the agreement
is itself suspicious.

## Steps

1. **Recover.** Pull prior context, sources (`BS-SRC`), the project boundary and
   the locked decisions. A lock constrains this session; it is not reopened
   without an explicit instruction. Import nothing from another named project
   without evidence that it transfers.
2. **Frame.** Restate the idea, the desired change, the actor, the constraints,
   the non-goals, the stakes and the highest-impact unknown. Recover Founder DNA
   and mark each item unconfirmed, partially confirmed or confirmed. Ask at most
   one materially branching question, then proceed on labelled assumptions.
3. **Fission.** Produce structurally different frames (`BS-FRM`) before any
   solution volume: another problem, actor, scale, time horizon, ownership
   model, scarcity, worldview, and the no-interface frame. Record what each
   frame makes obvious and what it hides.
4. **Evolve.** Encode the serious directions as genomes (`BS-GEN`), mark the
   load-bearing loci, mutate at least two of the rest, cross compatible
   mechanisms, run at least one distant collision and one counterfactual world.
   Record parentage on every generation. Emit a genetic-diversity warning when
   the survivors are structurally identical.
5. **Surface.** When embodiment materially changes the concept, compare the
   candidates and record the decision (`BS-SRF`) with the rejected surfaces and
   their reasons. A multi-surface concept must give each surface a distinct role
   and name the canonical state owner.
6. **Assemble the case file.** One neutral brief: source context and
   constraints, never the preferred answer, never a hint of the intended
   conclusion.
7. **Run the three cells independently.** Expansion (first principles, user
   value, possibility space), Reality (strategy, economics, feasibility,
   incentives), Adversarial (premortem, abuse, second-order effects, reasons to
   kill). Each returns assumptions, strongest proposal, strongest objection,
   falsifier and confidence. Freeze all three positions before any cell sees
   another's conclusion. Add at most two specialist mandates, and only where one
   attacks a material uncertainty. If independent agents are unavailable, run
   clearly separated passes and disclose that limitation in the output. Never
   fabricate a transcript.
8. **Cross-examine.** Steelman the opposing direction before attacking it.
   Record each exchange as `BS-ARG` with its polarity and its rebuttal. Record
   every unresolved opposition as a tension (`BS-TEN`), intact. Do not blend two
   conflicting survivors into a third concept nobody argued for.
9. **Route the evidence.** Every claim that depends on customer demand, a
   competitor, a price, a law, a technical capability or user behaviour becomes
   a hypothesis (`BS-HYP`) with a falsifier and is routed to Research {OS},
   Market Research {OS} or Validation {OS}. It is never asserted here.
10. **Red-team the leader.** Run the red-team pass workflow. A concept that has
    not been attacked is not a survivor.
11. **Recombine.** Synthesise compatible strengths into new candidates, keeping
    the tensions visible. Preserve the strangest coherent survivor via
    `/anomaly` before ranking.
12. **Converge.** Declare the criteria and their weights before scoring. Rank
    the survivors on those criteria plus valuable surprise, founder signature
    and surface fit. Run the sensitivity check: name the one criterion the
    ranking actually turns on. Classify every decision (`BS-DEC`) as locked,
    provisional, experiment-first, deferred, incubated or rejected, each with
    its reason.
13. **Queue what is still unknown.** Turn every decisive unknown into the
    cheapest test that could settle it (`BS-EXP`), and name the decision each
    test unblocks. Leave the remaining founder questions as `BS-QUE`, and only
    the ones that materially branch.
14. **Commit the ledger.** Write frames, genomes, ideas, surfaces, hypotheses,
    arguments, tensions, decisions, experiments, incubations and questions to
    canonical state, with lineage intact and no recycled ID.

## Completion test

- At least two structurally different frames were generated, and the selected
  concept can be traced back to the frame it came from.
- The population contains at least one candidate that is not a variant of the
  first idea. Cosmetic variants do not count toward diversity.
- Three independent positions exist, each with assumptions, strongest proposal,
  strongest objection, falsifier and confidence, and each was frozen before
  cross-examination began.
- If agents were unavailable, the output says so in plain words.
- Every material statement carries a label: `EVIDENCE`, `DECISION`,
  `HYPOTHESIS`, `ASSUMPTION`, `CONSTRAINT`, `UNKNOWN` or `CONFLICT`.
- No external fact is asserted. Every one is a `BS-HYP` with a falsifier and a
  named destination.
- Every unresolved opposition appears as a `BS-TEN`, not as a blended
  compromise.
- Every rejection carries its reason, and every rejected branch is retained
  rather than deleted.
- The criteria and weights were declared before the ranking, and the sensitivity
  of the ranking is stated.
- Exactly one concept is recommended, with the dissent recorded beside it.
- Nothing in the output specifies screens, schemas, endpoints or milestones.
- Status is `BRAINSTORM CONVERGED: HANDOFF READY`, or `BRAINSTORM IN PROGRESS`,
  `BRAINSTORM BLOCKED` or `BRAINSTORM PARKED` with the reason named. No other
  vocabulary is used.
