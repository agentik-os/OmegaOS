## 1. Purpose and operating law

Use this engine when the idea space is incremental, the user asks for wilder ideation, several concepts share the same hidden frame, or the Council is polishing too early.

Optimize for **valuable surprise**: a direction should be non-obvious, causally coherent, faithful to founder intent, and capable of changing the decision. Random novelty is not imagination.

Non-negotiable laws:

1. Preserve the intended transformation while allowing every inherited mechanism to mutate.
2. Fracture the frame before multiplying solutions.
3. Change at least two genome loci for every structural mutation.
4. Keep generations separate; do not rewrite ancestors as if the new idea existed from the start.
5. Apply selection pressure only after a genuinely divergent population exists.
6. Keep one coherent anomaly alive when it challenges the dominant worldview.
7. Do not converge on familiarity merely because it is easy to explain.

## 2. Three-chamber architecture

Run the chambers sequentially. Independence applies inside each chamber.

### Chamber I — Imagination

Create alternate frames and primitives before judging feasibility. Use three incompatible cognitive priors:

- **First-Principles Inventor:** rebuild from desired state change and physical/human constraints.
- **Human Meaning Anthropologist:** explore ritual, identity, emotion, trust, status, and behavior.
- **Alien Systems Thinker:** reject category conventions, import distant mechanisms, and ask what a non-incumbent civilization would design.

Required output: 4–8 frame mutations, 6–12 mechanisms, one inversion, one anomaly, two distant collisions, and at least one non-software embodiment.

### Chamber II — Evolution

Treat concepts as a population rather than a list. Use:

- **Mutator:** changes two or more genome loci while preserving the intended transformation.
- **Crossover Designer:** recombines compatible mechanisms from distant parents.
- **Extinction Selector:** kills attractive but structurally weak concepts and explains the selection pressure.

Required output: explicit generations, parentage, mutations, selection pressure, survivors, extinct concepts, and genetic loss warnings.

### Chamber III — Council

Send only the strongest, strangest coherent survivors into Expansion, Reality, and Adversarial debate. The Council may repair or kill them, but it must not silently replace unusual mechanisms with conventional product defaults.

For `DEEP`, run at least two evolutionary generations before Council convergence. For `SPARK`, use one compact Imagination pass and stop before claiming selection.

## 3. Founder DNA

Extract founder-specific creative material. Do not invent it. Label inferred material and ask the founder to confirm only when it materially changes direction.

Capture:

- obsessions and recurring questions;
- non-negotiable beliefs and values;
- taste markers: what feels elegant, alive, cheap, false, or overbuilt;
- anti-patterns and worlds the founder refuses to create;
- unfair insights, lived experience, access, craft, or distribution edges;
- energy profile: work the founder wants to do repeatedly versus only tolerate;
- signature tension: the contradiction that repeatedly appears in the founder's best ideas.

Use Founder DNA as a selection lens, not a cage. A concept may violate a preference if it exposes a more important truth; record the conflict.

## 4. Frame Fission

Create separate frames by mutating one or more of:

| Axis | Mutation questions |
| --- | --- |
| Actor | Who benefits, acts, pays, authorizes, contributes, or is protected? |
| Progress | What transformation is actually sought beneath the requested feature? |
| Scale | What changes at one person, one group, one city, or one ecosystem? |
| Time | What if value is instant, delayed, seasonal, lifelong, or inherited? |
| Ownership | What if value is owned, rented, shared, stewarded, or disappears after use? |
| Scarcity | What if the scarce resource is attention, trust, access, time, proof, or coordination? |
| Worldview | Is the actor a consumer, member, creator, citizen, patient, player, apprentice, or node? |
| Interface | What if there is no app, no screen, no account, or no explicit interaction? |

Each frame states what it reveals, what it hides, its central tension, and the evidence that would make it superior to the original frame.

## 5. Idea Genome

Represent each serious concept with these loci:

1. actor;
2. desired progress;
3. trigger or moment;
4. core mechanism;
5. unit of value;
6. interaction model;
7. trust model;
8. distribution;
9. economic/incentive model;
10. ownership/governance;
11. enabling capability;
12. time horizon;
13. worldview or cultural meaning;
14. surface/embodiment.

Record immutable parent IDs. A mutation must state which loci changed, why, what new capability appears, what value was lost, and the new failure mode.

## 6. Evolutionary generations

Use a named selection pressure per generation. Recommended sequence:

| Generation | Selection pressure | Typical operators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G0 — Seed | Fidelity to intended transformation | abstraction, subtraction |
| G1 — Fission | Frame distance | actor swap, problem inversion, time/scale shift |
| G2 — Mechanism | Causal originality | primitive replacement, no-interface, trust inversion |
| G3 — Collision | Transfer from distant domains | analogy, symbiosis, crossover |
| G4 — Worlds | Robustness under changed assumptions | counterfactual constraints, future scenarios |
| G5 — Elegance | Magic per unit complexity | compression, staged embodiment, extinction |

Operators:

- **Mutation:** change at least two loci.
- **Crossover:** combine mechanisms from two parents; never bundle whole feature sets.
- **Speciation:** preserve two incompatible concepts because they serve different worlds or actors.
- **Subtraction:** remove a locus or dependency and rebuild the causal chain.
- **Exaptation:** give an existing capability a different job.
- **Symbiosis:** make two actors or systems create mutual value.
- **Extinction:** reject with a reason and resurrection trigger.

Stop evolving when a generation has no material delta, genetic diversity collapses, or decisive evidence is needed.

## 7. Collision and counterfactual engines

### Collision Engine

Select two structurally distant domains. Map:

`source domain → deep mechanism → target constraint → adapted mechanism → incompatibility → experiment`

Useful source families: games, rituals, logistics, immune systems, theatre, insurance, apprenticeship, hospitality, markets, libraries, urban design, sports, nature, cryptography, or public institutions. Reject copied aesthetics.

### Counterfactual Worlds

Generate 3–5 worlds by changing assumptions that matter:

- AI becomes abundant but trust becomes scarce;
- screens are socially rejected;
- distribution is free but attention is expensive;
- regulation requires human accountability;
- connectivity is intermittent;
- the user never forms the expected habit;
- the business must work at 10% of expected capital;
- success makes the original problem worse.

For each world, state which concepts survive, mutate, or die and identify a no-regret mechanism.

## 8. Incubation, anomaly, and portfolio

### Incubation

Do not delete a concept merely because its timing, evidence, or enabling capability is wrong today. Park it with:

- reason dormant;
- missing condition;
- resurrection trigger;
- next review event, not an arbitrary date;
- concept IDs that could absorb it later.

### Anomaly

Preserve one idea that initially scores poorly but reveals a new rule. Give it an anomaly thesis and a falsifier. Kill it if it is merely random or impossible for reasons central to the founder's intent.

### Portfolio coherence

When several ideas coexist, map shared primitives, audiences, data, trust, distribution, operations, brand meaning, and founder energy. Distinguish:

- compounding portfolio;
- adjacent but independent bets;
- cannibalizing or contradictory bets;
- distraction disguised as synergy.

Do not merge distinct named projects. Portfolio coherence can recommend shared infrastructure or sequencing without erasing boundaries.

## 9. Output contract

For `IMAGINATION` or `DEEP`, add:

1. Founder DNA used and uncertain;
2. frame fissions;
3. idea genome table;
4. generation tree and selection pressures;
5. collisions and counterfactual worlds;
6. surviving anomaly;
7. extinct/incubated ideas with resurrection triggers;
8. valuable-surprise score and evidence sentence;
9. exact delta from the original seed;
10. concepts sent to the Council and why.

Never expose private chain-of-thought. Show observable mutations, parentage, claims, concise rationales, and decisions.
