# Network review

Look at the whole network at once and separate the relationships that are
breathing by design from the ones that are neglected by accident.

## Trigger

The review cadence fires (monthly by default), or the user reports that the
network feels thin, one-sided or stuck in one room.

## Steps

1. **Network {OS}** loads every person record and interaction from Context &
   Memory {OS}. Produces: the review set.
2. **Network {OS}** computes reciprocity per relationship: what was given, what
   was received, over what period. Produces: a give and receive ledger.
3. **Network {OS}** computes diversity: how many distinct contexts, industries
   and vintages the network spans, and where it is concentrated. Produces: a
   diversity report.
4. **Network {OS}** lists every commitment the user made that is still open past
   its date. Produces: the neglect list, which is the only list that is a
   to-do.
5. **Network {OS}** lists every relationship with no contact inside its declared
   cadence, and asks the user one question per relationship: deliberate, or
   dropped. Produces: a dormancy classification.
6. **User** answers. Deliberate becomes a breathing state with a reactivation
   condition; dropped becomes an open loop. Produces: the classification.
7. **Network {OS}** writes the breathing states and reactivation conditions to
   Context & Memory {OS}. Produces: canonical cadence state.
8. **Network {OS}** hands the neglect list to Execution {OS} as tasks, and
   proposes at most three relationships to reactivate this period, each with a
   real reason to make contact.
9. **Network {OS}** reports what it could not assess and why, rather than
   scoring the network out of ten.

## Completion test

Every relationship in the review set carries exactly one of four states: active
within cadence, breathing with a stated reactivation condition, dropped with an
open loop, or unassessed with the reason named. A relationship left in no state,
or a summary score presented in place of the four states, is a failed review.

## Failure and abort

- Interaction history is too sparse to compute reciprocity: report the coverage
  honestly and assess only the relationships with enough evidence. Do not
  extrapolate a pattern from two data points.
- The user declines to classify a dormant relationship: leave it unassessed with
  that reason recorded. Unassessed is a legitimate state; a guess is not.
- The reactivation proposal has no genuine reason for contact: drop it. A
  reactivation with nothing to say is the drip campaign this OS refuses to be.
