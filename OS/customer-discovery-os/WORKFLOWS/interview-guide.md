# Workflow: Interview guide

**Produces:** a versioned interview guide whose questions ask about things that
already happened, including the questions that would disprove your own
hypothesis.

## Trigger

A round plan is approved and the first interview is scheduled, or a debrief
produced a change that the next session should carry.

## Steps

1. **Restate the learning goal** from the round plan at the top of the guide. A
   guide that drifts from its goal produces pleasant conversations and no
   evidence.
2. **Write the opening.** Thank them, state how long it will take, say plainly
   that you are not selling anything and that there are no right answers, then
   run the consent script: what is captured, where it goes, who sees it, how long
   it is kept, and that they can stop or withdraw at any time. Ask for recording
   permission explicitly and wait for the answer.
3. **Open with an easy factual question** about their work: what they do, how the
   team is arranged, what a normal week contains. This is calibration, and it
   costs two minutes.
4. **Write the core questions in past tense.** For the behaviour you care about:
   when was the last time it happened, walk me through it from the beginning,
   what did you do, who else was involved, what did it cost you, what did you try
   before that. Every question points at a specific occurrence.
5. **Write the probes.** Short, neutral, and reusable: what happened next, how
   did you handle it, what would have happened if you did nothing, tell me more
   about that, what did you do instead. Probes are how a judgement ("it is a
   nightmare") becomes a story with a date in it.
6. **Write at least two kill questions.** Questions whose honest answer would
   disprove your hypothesis: how much did you spend fixing it last time, what
   else did you consider and why did you not choose it, what would have to be
   true for you to keep doing it exactly the way you do now. If the guide
   contains no question that can hurt the idea, it is a pitch with question marks.
7. **Mark every future-tense question.** Rewrite it in past tense if possible.
   If a future-tense question genuinely must stay, mark it as a deliberate
   exception and note that its answers will be recorded as intent, never as
   behaviour.
8. **Add the workaround hunt.** What have you built, bought, hacked or asked
   someone to do by hand to cope with this. A workaround is a person paying in
   their own time, and it is the strongest single signal a round can produce.
9. **Write the closing.** Ask what you should have asked and did not, ask who
   else they know with the same problem, restate what happens to their data, and
   confirm the incentive if one was offered.
10. **Time it.** Read it aloud against the session length. A guide that cannot be
    covered in the booked time will be rushed at exactly the point where the
    interesting material sits.
11. **Version it.** Give the guide a version number. Every session record names
    the version it was run against, so a mid-round change is visible instead of
    invisible.

## Completion test

- Every core question asks about a specific past occurrence, or is explicitly
  marked as a labelled future-tense exception.
- At least two questions could return an answer that damages the hypothesis.
- The consent script is written out in full, in speakable words, at the top.
- The workaround question is present.
- No question describes the product, and no question contains a benefit claim.
- The guide carries a version number, and the previous version remains readable.
- Read aloud, it fits the booked session length with time left over.
