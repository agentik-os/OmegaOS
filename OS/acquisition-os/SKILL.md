# Acquisition {OS}

Buy a business: search, approach, negotiate, close.

## When to use this

Use Acquisition when there is a specific business the operator intends to buy,
or a search campaign to find one:

- the operator wants to buy rather than build, and has no written buy box;
- a target has been identified and the owner has not yet been approached;
- an owner is interested and the conversation needs a structure;
- a valuation range and an offer hypothesis are needed before an offer;
- an indication of interest or a letter of intent has to be prepared;
- exclusivity has started and the close is slipping;
- a red flag or a kill criterion has fired and the question is whether to walk.

Near neighbours, and the one line that separates them:

| If the question is | Use |
|---|---|
| which of many opportunities deserve any time at all | Deal Flow {OS} |
| what the instrument is, and what the clause says | Deal Structuring {OS} |
| whether the seller's claims are true | Due Diligence {OS} |
| why this bet works and what would kill it | Investment Thesis {OS} |
| how much may be committed, against what policy | Capital {OS} |
| what happens on the day after completion | Portfolio Management {OS} |

The rule: Acquisition owns the campaign and the calendar for one named target.
Deal Structuring owns the instrument and the clause. Deal Flow owns the funnel
that Acquisition is no longer part of.

## Capabilities

- Write a buy box with stated ranges and a stated reason for each field.
- Run a proprietary search campaign against the buy box, with a dated next
  action per target.
- Draft owner approaches for a human to send, in the operator's voice.
- Qualify seller motivation and record the reason for sale in the seller's own
  words.
- Produce a valuation range with every assumption labelled evidenced or
  unverified.
- Build the offer hypothesis: what is being paid for, out of what, and on what
  conditions.
- Prepare an indication of interest or a letter of intent as a draft for legal
  review and human signature.
- Build and run the exclusivity calendar: workstream, owner, deliverable, date.
- Escalate a slipping date the day it slips, with the options and their cost.
- Pause the calendar on a diligence red flag and present the continue or
  abandon decision.
- Drive the closing checklist to sign off, condition by condition.
- Produce the day one transition pack for Portfolio Management {OS}.
- Execute a clean abandon, with the reason, the cost and the lesson recorded.

## Procedure

1. Establish the buy box, and check its financing capacity against Capital {OS}.
2. Build the target list from proprietary search and from qualified
   opportunities handed over by Deal Flow {OS}.
3. Draft the approach. A human sends it. Record the contact and the response.
4. Qualify seller motivation before any valuation work. If motivation cannot be
   established, stop.
5. Sign the non-disclosure agreement, after human approval, and take delivery of
   the seller's information.
6. Produce the valuation range and the offer hypothesis, marking every
   assumption evidenced or unverified.
7. Send the unverified assumptions to Due Diligence {OS} as the diligence scope,
   and send the structural questions to Deal Structuring {OS}.
8. Obtain the approved commitment amount from Capital {OS} before making an
   offer. An offer without an approved amount is not an offer.
9. Prepare the indication of interest or letter of intent. A lawyer reviews it,
   a human signs it.
10. On acceptance, evidence the financing, then enter exclusivity and publish
    the dated close plan.
11. Run the close: daily owner and deliverable, red flags pause the calendar,
    changed terms route back to Deal Structuring {OS}.
12. Complete, then hand the day one transition pack to Portfolio Management {OS}
    and the governance structure to Board {OS}.
13. Or abandon: tell the counterparty, record what fired and what it cost, and
    feed the lesson back into the buy box.

## Handoffs

| Receives from | What |
|---|---|
| Deal Flow {OS} | one qualified named target, its source and its contact history |
| Investment Thesis {OS} | the written thesis and the kill criteria that bind this deal |
| Due Diligence {OS} | findings with severity, red flags, and the conditions that must be met before completion |
| Capital {OS} | the approved commitment amount, which is what makes an offer real |
| Deal Structuring {OS} | the agreed instrument and terms to carry into the negotiation |

| Hands to | What that OS expects |
|---|---|
| Due Diligence {OS} | the specific unverified assumptions that would change the decision, with a time budget |
| Deal Structuring {OS} | the offer hypothesis and every structural question the negotiation has raised |
| Capital {OS} | the commitment request with its range, its timing and its reserve implication |
| Portfolio Management {OS} | the day one transition pack: baseline numbers, contacts, reporting expectations, data rights |
| Board {OS} | the governance structure agreed in the transaction, and the first board calendar |
| Context & Memory {OS} | the buy box, the target list, the seller motivation record, the close plan and the abandon record |
