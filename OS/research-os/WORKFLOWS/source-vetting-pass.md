# Workflow: Source vetting pass

**Produces:** a vetted source ledger in which every source carries its tier, its
producing interest, its stated weakness, and its access outcome, including
everything that was blocked.

## Trigger

Sources are in hand and someone is about to quote them. Or an inherited document
cites sources nobody has looked at.

## Steps

1. **List every source, including the ones you rejected.** A source you dismissed
   in ten seconds still belongs in the ledger with the reason, so the next
   session does not spend the same ten seconds.
2. **Record the access outcome for each.** Retrieved, paywalled, login-walled,
   rate limited, geo-blocked, dead link, or forbidden by terms. A blocked source
   is a negative result with a reason and a locator, never an omission.
3. **Assign a tier.** Primary (the thing itself: filing, dataset, standard,
   court record, raw measurement, the vendor's own documentation), secondary
   (reporting on a primary), tertiary (aggregation of secondaries), or
   positional (opinion, marketing, advocacy). Tier is a fact about the source,
   not a judgement of quality.
4. **Walk back to the primary.** Where a source summarises another, retrieve the
   one it summarises. Stop when you reach something that generated the
   information rather than repeating it. Record the chain.
5. **Ask how they know.** Measurement, survey, model, expert judgement,
   compilation, or restatement. A source that cannot say how it knows is
   positional, whatever it looks like.
6. **Date it.** Date of the evidence, date of publication and date accessed.
   Then check for a newer edition, revision or correction. A superseded figure
   quoted without its date is a misleading claim even when quoted accurately.
7. **Name the interest.** Who produced it, who paid for it, and what they gain if
   it is believed. A funded benchmark stays usable, cited with its funding. The
   source is not banned; the interest is never hidden.
8. **Check independence against the rest of the ledger.** Mark sources that share
   an origin, a dataset, an author or a funder. This is what stops a corroboration
   count from inflating.
9. **State one weakness per source you keep.** Every source has one: sample,
   recency, scope, method, incentive, or reach. A source with no stated weakness
   has not been vetted, it has been accepted.
10. **Check the terms before any repeat or automated access.** If the terms
    forbid it, stop and route it to the human approval boundary with the licensed
    or manual alternative named.
11. **Decide keep, keep-with-caveat, or reject,** and write the decision into the
    ledger with its reason. Rejected sources stay visible.
12. **Report the coverage gap.** Which sub-questions now have no usable source,
    what kind of source they would need, and what it would take to reach it.
    That gap feeds the unknown register directly.

## Completion test

- Every source encountered appears in the ledger, including rejected and blocked
  ones, each with a reason.
- Every entry has a locator, a date accessed and an access outcome.
- Every kept source has a tier, an answer to "how do they know", both relevant
  dates, and exactly one stated weakness.
- Citation chains are walked back to a primary, or the record states where the
  walk stopped and why.
- Producing interest is disclosed wherever one exists.
- Sources sharing an origin, dataset, author or funder are marked, so
  corroboration counts cannot be inflated.
- No source was accessed against its terms without approval, and any such
  request is recorded with its alternative.
- The coverage gap is stated per sub-question, including the sub-questions with
  no usable source at all.
