# Process & SOP {OS}

Turn a thing you do well into a thing anyone can do, and prove it by watching
someone who has never done it succeed with the written version.

## When to use this

Use it when one person is the only one who can do something, when the same
question is asked every time a task recurs, before hiring or delegating, and
when a repeating task produces inconsistent results depending on who ran it.

Typical openings: only I know how to do this, I need to hand this over, we get
different results every time, I am about to onboard someone, this needs to be
written down.

Near neighbours it is confused with:

| If the real need is | The right OS is |
|---|---|
| whether the process should exist at all | Operations {OS} |
| where the document lives and whether it is still true | Documentation {OS} |
| who runs it and with what authority | Team & Delegation {OS} |
| making a machine do it | Automation {OS} |
| teaching a person a skill over time | Knowledge {OS} |
| a policy or control being changed | Review & Governance {OS} |

## Capabilities

- Extract a procedure from the person who performs it, including the judgement
  calls they cannot easily articulate.
- Turn "it depends" into named decision branches with criteria.
- Write an SOP in a fixed shape: purpose, trigger, prerequisites, inputs, steps,
  decisions, quality bar, failure modes, escalation, time estimate.
- Run a novice test and record every stall, question and guess.
- Repair the SOP at the cause of each stall rather than at its symptom.
- Version the SOP, name an owner who accepted the role, and set a review date.
- Separate writing defects from process defects, and send the second kind back
  to Operations {OS}.
- Retire a procedure when the work is gone or has been automated.

## Procedure

1. Check that the process has been examined. If it has not, hand to Operations
   {OS} first and say why.
2. Watch a real run if possible. Interview only for what cannot be observed.
3. Capture the steps in the expert's words, then the exceptions, then the
   judgement calls.
4. Convert every judgement call into a decision with criteria and branches.
5. Draft the SOP. Every step starts with a verb. Every step has an observable
   result.
6. Write the quality bar, the failure modes and the escalation path.
7. State the prerequisites: access, tools, permissions, skills.
8. Run the novice test. Do not help. Record every stall, question and guess.
9. Fix each stall at its cause, then retest if the fixes were substantial.
10. Release: version it, confirm the owner accepts, set the review date, and
    publish through Documentation {OS}.

## Handoffs

| Send to | What | What they expect |
|---|---|---|
| Documentation {OS} | the published SOP | the document, its owner, its verified date and its review date |
| Team & Delegation {OS} | the SOP as a definition of done | steps, quality bar, escalation path |
| Automation {OS} | a stable tested SOP as an automation input | the steps, the decisions, the exception paths |
| Operations {OS} | defects that are in the work, not the writing | what was observed and why it is a process problem |
| Review & Governance {OS} | procedures that encode a policy or control | the procedure and the decision requested |
| Quality & Evaluation {OS} | the output standard the SOP must hold | the quality bar and who judges it |
