# Workflow: expert to SOP

Produces a drafted procedure in which the expert's judgement has been made
explicit, so that someone else can exercise it.

## Trigger

One person is the only one who can do something that recurs, and that is now a
constraint: on delegating, on hiring, on going on holiday, or on consistency.

## Inputs

- The expert, and a bounded amount of their time.
- A real run to observe, with consent.
- The output standard: what an acceptable result looks like and who judges it.
- The simplified target model from Operations {OS}, if the process has been
  diagnosed.
- Whatever already exists on the topic, from Documentation {OS}.

## Steps

1. **Check the work has been examined.** If Operations {OS} has never looked at
   it, say so. Writing a careful SOP for work that should be deleted is a
   permanent cost.
2. **Observe first, interview second.** People describe the path they intend,
   not the path they take. Watch the run, then ask about what you saw.
3. **Capture the steps in their words.** Do not tidy the language yet; tidying
   too early deletes the detail that matters.
4. **Hunt the judgement.** Every time the expert says "it depends", stop and
   extract: depends on what, what are the possible answers, and what do you do
   in each. This is the highest-value part of the whole workflow.
5. **Hunt the exceptions.** What happens when the input is wrong, the system is
   down, the client is late, or the numbers do not reconcile.
6. **Ask what they check.** Experts verify constantly and rarely mention it.
   Their checks become the quality bar.
7. **Ask what goes wrong for other people.** The expert has watched others fail
   at this. Those are the failure modes.
8. **Write the draft in the house shape:** purpose, trigger, prerequisites,
   inputs, steps, decisions, quality bar, failure modes, escalation, time
   estimate.
9. **Make each step actionable.** A verb, an object, and an observable result.
   Remove every sentence that describes a state rather than an action.
10. **Read it back to the expert** and ask one question: what would go wrong if
    someone followed exactly this. Their answer is the last extraction.

## Completion test

- The draft covers the normal path, the named decisions with their criteria, and
  the enumerated exceptions.
- Every step starts with a verb and has an observable result.
- Prerequisites are listed, including access and permissions.
- The quality bar names what acceptable looks like and who judges it.
- Failure modes and the escalation path are written.
- A time estimate is stated.
- The expert has confirmed nothing important is missing.

## Failure paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| the expert cannot articulate a step even after observation | record it as a judgement point that still requires them, and scope the SOP around it honestly |
| the expert has no time | capture one observed run and write from it, and label the draft as based on a single run |
| two experts do it differently | stop; that is a process decision for Operations {OS}, not something to average in prose |
| the process changes while you are writing it | say the process is unstable, shorten the review cadence, and consider whether it is ready to standardise at all |
