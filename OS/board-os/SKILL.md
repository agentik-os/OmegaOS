# Board {OS}

Governance at the board level: papers, cadence, real oversight.

## When to use this

Use Board when the question is about how a company is governed from the board
seat:

- a board exists on paper and meetings are improvised;
- packs go out the night before, so the meeting is spent reading;
- meetings are full of updates and short of decisions;
- nobody can say which decisions management may take without asking;
- actions roll over meeting after meeting;
- a decision needs a record that will hold up in two years, including who
  dissented and who declared a conflict;
- the board has never tested whether it is producing oversight or ceremony;
- a transaction has just created board seats, reserved matters and information
  rights that now have to be operated.

Near neighbours, and the one line that separates them:

| If the question is | Use |
|---|---|
| what the owner should know about the position from outside | Portfolio Management {OS} |
| what the reserved matter or information right should say | Deal Structuring {OS} |
| the operator's own change control and review cadence | Review & Governance {OS} |
| whether to commit more capital | Capital {OS} |
| how management executes a board decision | Execution {OS} |

The rule: Deal Structuring writes the right, Board exercises it. Portfolio
Management reports from outside, Board governs inside.

## Capabilities

- Build a delegated authority matrix in which every decision class belongs to
  management, a committee or the board.
- Draft committee terms of reference and a composition map.
- Lay out the annual board calendar, including the items that only appear once
  a year.
- Assemble a board pack and issue it at a stated notice period.
- Enforce the notice rule by proposing deferral rather than late issue.
- Design an agenda ordered by decision, moving no decision items to writing.
- Capture decisions live, with attendance, dissent and declared conflicts.
- Draft minutes and resolutions for human approval and for the company
  secretary's record.
- Maintain the action register, and put open actions before new business.
- Run the oversight test: are risks tracked, are actions closed, is management
  actually challenged.
- Run a board effectiveness review with owned, dated findings.
- Escalate a decision taken outside the authority matrix, and propose the
  amendment.

## Procedure

1. Load the constitutional documents and the rights created at the transaction,
   then build or refresh the delegated authority matrix until no decision class
   is unassigned.
2. Lay out the annual calendar: standing items on every agenda, annual items
   placed on a specific meeting.
3. For each meeting, request papers against a deadline derived from the notice
   period, not from the meeting date.
4. Assemble the pack. Every paper states the decision it asks for. Papers
   asking for no decision go to the written section.
5. Issue the pack at the notice period after human approval. If a paper is
   missing, issue what is ready and propose deferring its item.
6. Build the agenda ordered by decision, with open actions first.
7. Run the meeting: capture each decision with attendance, dissent and any
   declared conflict, and capture each action with an owner and a date.
8. Draft the minutes and any resolutions, marked as drafts, and route them for
   human approval.
9. Hand the approved record to the company secretary, who holds the statutory
   record and makes any filing.
10. Between meetings, chase the action register and run the oversight test.
11. Annually, run the effectiveness review and give every finding an owner and
    a date.
