## What this engagement is

You are not buying a software subscription, a consulting deck, or a chatbot plugged
into your Slack. You are hiring a senior operator who embeds with your company, maps
how your C-suite actually works, builds a real AI system around those people, and then
transfers full ownership to your teams. When we leave, the capability stays.

Three principles shape every decision we make together.

**1. Centralization.** Every AI workflow, every agent, every dashboard lives on one
dedicated server your company owns. There are no scattered SaaS accounts, no vendor
lock-in, no black box you cannot inspect. If you ever want to leave, you take the
server keys and go.

**2. C-Level interconnection.** Each executive gets one function-specific dashboard
built for their real job — not a generic screen repainted seven times. And those
dashboards talk to each other: when the COO flags a fulfilment delay, the CFO sees
the cash-flow impact in real time. The system is federated, not siloed.

**3. Internal mastery.** The engagement does not end when the system is built. It ends
when your own people can add an agent, connect a new tool, and adjust a report —
unaided, without calling us. You become the operational guardians. We deliberately
make ourselves unnecessary.

**Our stance on black boxes.** Every dashboard, agent, and automated report shows its
sources, its logs, its cost, and its confidence score. No algorithm produces a number
your team cannot trace. "Trust the AI" is not a governance posture; "inspect the AI"
is.

## The six steps

### Step 1 — Before we start: Readiness and fit
*Duration: a free 30-minute call*

Before any contract is signed we run an honest qualification. We assess your company
across nine dimensions — executive sponsorship, tooling, data readiness, compliance,
culture, and more — and give you a clear verdict: **Go**, **Not yet**, or
**Redirect** (you might need a point-SaaS, a data engineer, or a hire first).

If the answer is not yet, we give you a Gap-to-Target plan and a re-qualify date. We
do not collect a fee to tell you that. An engagement that starts on the wrong footing
fails expensively for both parties; an honest gate at the front is the most useful
thing we offer.

**What you receive:** an AI Readiness Scorecard, a Go / Not-yet / Redirect brief,
a recommended engagement shape, and an indicative investment figure.

**What we need from you:** 30 minutes with the person who holds the budget and the
authority to say yes. The company website. An honest answer to "what triggered this
conversation?"

---

### Step 2 — Discovery: First the work, then the tools
*Duration: 1–5 days depending on the number of C-Levels in scope*

We interview each executive in scope — one person at a time, in their professional
language, without AI jargon. We open with "walk me through your last Monday", not
"what AI tools do you use." We learn their actual week, their repetitive tasks (with
time and frequency), their handoffs, their tools, their frustrations, and the gap
between where they are and where they want to be.

**What you receive:** a standardized dossier per person — role, weekly rhythm, daily
actions, tools, integrations, frictions, and a current-versus-ideal gap analysis. We
validate our write-up with each person before it leaves the room. A consolidated
company rollup maps all the seams between people.

**What we need from you:** 15–25 minutes per executive we interview. A company
website URL beforehand. Honest answers — especially the tedious, unglamorous parts.
The more your team describes what they hate, the better the system gets.

A note on fear: this step is about removing the parts of the job people dread, not
removing the people. We say this plainly at the start of every interview.

---

### Step 3 — Blueprint: Your Company AI OS
*Duration: 3–7 days*

With the discovery dossiers in hand we map the whole picture: every tool and its
integration status, every data source and its readiness, every automation opportunity
scored by impact, frequency, feasibility, and risk. Then we design the system.

The blueprint specifies:
- the single centralized server (region, stack, the export path so you can always leave)
- which C-Level micro-SaaS dashboards get built, each tied to the real job that person does
- the inter-dashboard API contract — which metric on one dashboard triggers which alert on another
- the six Composio connectors that map to your real systems of record
- an implementation roadmap with 30/60/90 milestones
- a projected ROI per workflow (hours × loaded cost × frequency — every number cited)

**What you receive:** a ten-file Company AI OS blueprint — executive summary,
stakeholder interview plan, workflow inventory, tool map, data map, opportunity
backlog, agentic system blueprints, dashboard feature specs, implementation roadmap,
and ROI governance doc.

**What we need from you:** a named executive sponsor who reviews and approves the
architecture before build begins. No approval, no build — this is not bureaucracy,
it is the gate that protects you from building the wrong thing.

---

### Step 4 — Build: An operational system, not a demo
*Duration: typically 2–4 weeks*

We build what the approved blueprint specified. One centralized server, provisioned
and client-owned from day one. One micro-SaaS dashboard per C-Level who exists in
your org (a company with no CDO does not get a CDO dashboard). The inter-dashboard
federation wired so dashboards consume each other's real metrics. Composio connectors
each proven by a live data read, not an enabled checkbox. Automated reports falling
out at the exact cadence your executives need. Monitoring with named alert thresholds
on five health dimensions. And a built-in baseline instrumentation so ROI can be
re-measured later.

Each dashboard ships the moment its acceptance test passes against your real data —
not when a demo looks good. You see value in week one, not a proof-of-concept in
month three.

**What you receive:** a build dossier — architecture realization spec, server
provisioning runbook, per-dashboard build plan, inter-dashboard API contract,
integration wiring guide, automated report specs, monitoring setup, ship-gate ledger,
and a sponsor communication plan.

**What we need from you:** data access (read-only first; we document every
permission), your executive sponsor at key milestone demos, and a decision when a
build gate surfaces a choice (region, integration priority, HITL thresholds).

---

### Step 5 — Enablement and transfer: Internal mastery
*Duration: 1–3 weeks*

A working system that nobody uses is worse than no system — you paid for it twice.
This step ensures that does not happen.

Phase 3 of the engagement is adoption. Every audience gets onboarding pitched at
their real job and their actual AI literacy: executives see the strategic view and
the approval queue; managers see their team's workflows; operators do their own real
task through the system, unaided, until they reach the aha-moment; internal technical
owners learn the runbook, the code pointers, and the extension paths.

Phase 4 is transfer. We teach three specific motions until your team performs them
without us: add an agent, connect a new tool, adjust a report. Transfer is not
declared complete when a quiz is passed or a demo is applauded — it is declared
complete when a named member of your team does all three unaided, in real conditions,
while we observe with our hands off the keyboard.

**What you receive:** onboarding session plans (per audience), an internal
documentation pack, an end-user training curriculum, a validated use-cases log,
an extension playbook sized to your team's actual technical level, an ownership
handover checklist, and an Autonomy-Readiness Gate report with objective evidence.

**What we need from you:** named owners per component before we leave (no
CAIO-only credentials may remain), your team's genuine participation in the unaided
extension motions, and a weekly guardian routine your team runs twice before we sign
off.

---

### Step 6 — Run, measure and expand
*Duration: ongoing — a deliberately light 1 hour per week*

Once the system is running and your team owns it, we stay available for one
strategic hour per week. That hour is for direction, not operations: should you expand
the support agent to billing? Should you add the next department? Is the ROI
projection holding or not?

Every quarter we produce a Quarterly Business Review — the system's actual ROI by
workflow cohort, measured against what the blueprint projected. Every number is
traceable to a telemetry event or a receipt. If a workflow's savings are decaying, we
say so plainly and fix the leak before proposing the next wave.

When the system is healthy and saturating its current scope, the natural next move is
the adjacent department — the loop closes back into discovery, with the advantage that
the federated server is already in place.

**What you receive:** an ROI measurement model, a monitoring health spec, an
optimization loop cadence, a weekly quota agenda, a Quarterly Business Review, and
an expansion play when the numbers support it.

**What we need from you:** access to live telemetry, your executive sponsor at the
quarterly review, and honest feedback when the system is or is not delivering.

## The investment model

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Setup (one-time) | €2,500 |
| Monthly recurring | €2,500 per member per month |
| Minimum commitment | None — monthly, cancel any time |

"Member" is a person covered by the engagement: an executive whose dashboard we
build or whose workflows enter the system. A three-member engagement running one
intensive month costs €2,500 + 3 × €2,500 = €10,000. A five-member engagement over
three months costs €2,500 + 5 × €2,500 × 3 = €40,000.

The monthly, no-minimum structure means you pay for months that are delivering value.
There is no annual commitment to sign before you have seen the system run.

## The internal mastery promise

The engagement is not done when the system is live. It is done when your teams are
its operational guardians — not hostages to a vendor who holds the keys.

Concretely: when this engagement ends, your company holds the server, the code, the
credentials, the runbooks, and the institutional know-how to add an agent, connect a
new integration, and adjust any report without calling us. If three months from now
you want to extend the CMO dashboard to include a new data source, your internal
technical owner does it by following the extension playbook we leave behind.

We measure this with an Autonomy-Readiness Gate — objective, evidenced, not a
feeling. If the gate does not pass, the engagement is not finished.

## How to prepare

**Appoint a sponsor.** The engagement requires one named executive with the authority
to approve the architecture and attend the milestone demos. Without a sponsor, the
engagement stalls at Step 3.

**The three-reports tip.** Before our qualification call, ask each C-Level in scope:
"What are the three reports or dashboards you look at most often, and how many hours
per week does your team spend producing them?" Those answers are the fastest path
to the highest-value automations.

**What to gather (all optional, but useful):**
- A list of the tools each C-Level uses daily
- Any existing automation or reporting workflows (even informal ones)
- A rough sense of where the most manual, repetitive work sits today

**To start:** book the free 30-minute qualification call. We will be honest about
fit before either party commits.
