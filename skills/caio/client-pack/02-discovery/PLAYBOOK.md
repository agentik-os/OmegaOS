# Phase 02 — Discovery

> *First the work, then the tools.*

---

## Purpose

Before any automation is designed, we learn how your company actually runs. This
phase produces a standardized dossier per executive in scope — their role, their
week, the repetitive work (with time and frequency), the handoffs between people,
the tools they use, the things that break, and the gap between where they are today
and where they want to be. The consolidated rollup rebuilds the real value chains
of your company from the seams between people.

---

## What happens

1. **Company scan.** Before the first interview we look at your website and any
   documentation you share — org chart, SOPs, existing process maps — so we arrive
   informed rather than asking you to explain the obvious.

2. **Consent and framing.** Each participant is told exactly why the interview is
   happening, how the notes will be used, and whether they can request anonymization.
   The opening message is always the same: this is about removing the parts of the
   job people find tedious, not removing the person. Honest answers are the point.

3. **The 14-topic interview (15–25 minutes per person).** We cover: identity and
   role, typical week and month, repetitive versus one-off daily tasks (with time
   × frequency for each repetitive item), handoffs upstream and downstream, tools
   and systems used, how those tools do or do not talk to each other, existing
   automation and any unofficial workarounds, frictions, what must not be touched,
   improvements wanted, the current situation and how it feels, the ideal situation
   and how that would feel, and our synthesis of the gap between the two.

4. **Validation.** Before any notes leave the room, we read back a five-line summary
   to the participant: role, biggest friction, the gap, and one or two places where
   automation could help phrased in their own language. They correct it or confirm it.

5. **Bundle export.** Each person's dossier is exported as a standardized set of
   files — identical structure for every person so insights stack cleanly across the
   whole C-suite.

6. **Company rollup.** Once all interviews are complete, a consolidation script
   produces a company-level rollup: tools cited by frequency, frictions grouped by
   job family, the reconstructed reporting and handoff map, and the spread of current
   versus ideal feelings. This is the input that feeds the blueprint phase.

---

## What you receive

Deliverables in this phase's `templates/` folder — one set per interviewee:

- **`summary.md`** — the one page your CAIO reads first: role, biggest friction, gap,
  top automation opportunities.
- **`00-identity.md`** through **`13-gap-analysis.md`** — the fourteen interview
  chapters in structured, consistent form.
- **`company-context.md`** — the company scan (sector, business model, size signals,
  brand tone) built from the website review.
- **`transcript.md`** — the lightly-cleaned full question-and-answer record,
  preserving verbatim quotes.
- **`metadata.json`** — machine-readable header (role, tools cited, frictions, gap
  summary, AI literacy/appetite) used by the consolidation step.

---

## What we need from you

- 15–25 minutes of calendar time per executive we interview, uninterrupted
- A company website URL and any org chart or process documentation that exists
- Each participant's honest account of their day — including the unglamorous,
  repetitive, or frustrating parts
- Consent from each participant before the interview begins

---

## Duration

One to five working days depending on the number of executives in scope. Each
interview is 15–25 minutes; analysis and bundle export add roughly half a day per
person.

---

## How you will know this phase is complete

Every in-scope executive has a completed, validated dossier. The company rollup
exists and maps the seams between people. Your CAIO can describe, per executive,
the top one or two places where automation would reclaim real time — with a named
friction, a time × frequency estimate, and the person's own words as the evidence.

If a dossier is missing any of those three elements, the interview for that person
is not complete.
