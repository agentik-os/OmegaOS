## 1. Scope and priorities

This module provides general wellbeing guidance, not personalized medical treatment. Use the priority order:

```text
SAFETY -> SLEEP -> REGULAR FOOD/HYDRATION -> MOVEMENT -> TRAINING
       -> RECOVERY -> OPTIONAL OPTIMIZATION
```

Do not add supplements, extended fasting, extreme temperatures, intense breathwork, or sleep restriction before basics. Refer to a clinician for symptoms, conditions, medication interactions, pregnancy, eating-disorder history, or concerning exercise responses.

## 2. Sleep and circadian system

**E1:** Most adults need at least seven hours; many function best around 7–9 hours. Personal need varies.

Protect:

- a consistent wake time and adequate sleep opportunity;
- morning outdoor light when possible;
- regular movement during the day;
- a cool, dark, quiet sleep environment;
- a 30–60 minute downshift with reduced work and bright screens;
- caffeine timing appropriate to sensitivity, generally avoiding late-day use;
- alcohol awareness: sedation is not equivalent to restorative sleep;
- a written shutdown to reduce open-loop rumination.

Red flags requiring medical evaluation can include suspected sleep apnea, severe insomnia, dangerous daytime sleepiness, persistent fatigue, or major changes in sleep need.

Never frame chronic sleep restriction as a millionaire habit. If ambition requires destroying sleep indefinitely, the system is badly designed.

## 3. Movement and training

**E1:** Use public-health guidance as a floor: adults should generally accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75–150 minutes vigorous activity weekly, plus muscle strengthening at least twice weekly, adapted to ability and health.

Build four capacities:

- daily low-intensity movement and walking;
- strength across major patterns;
- aerobic base;
- mobility, balance, and tissue tolerance as relevant.

Use progressive overload and recovery. Stop and seek assessment for chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, acute injury, or other warning signs.

### Minimum / standard / deep

- Floor: 5–10 minute walk or mobility plus one set of a safe movement.
- Standard: planned training session or meaningful aerobic movement.
- Deep: optional longer session only if recovery supports it.

Do not use exercise as punishment for eating or as proof of worth.

## 4. Nutrition and hydration

**E1 general pattern:** emphasize a varied diet with vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains where tolerated, nuts/seeds, adequate protein, and mostly minimally processed foods; limit excessive free sugars, sodium, saturated fat, trans fat, and alcohol.

Personalization must consider culture, preference, allergies, medical conditions, budget, body-composition goals, and eating history.

Core behaviors:

- eat regularly enough to support stable energy and training;
- include protein and fiber-rich foods across the day as appropriate;
- make the desired default easy to access;
- distinguish physical hunger, emotion, convenience, and social eating without shame;
- hydrate according to thirst, conditions, training, and medical advice;
- plan high-risk situations rather than relying on restraint.

Do not prescribe extreme calorie restriction or a multiday fast. Extended fasting requires medical supervision and is unsuitable for many people. Avoid moral labels such as “clean” and “cheat.”

## 5. Breathwork and nervous-system practices

**E2:** Slow, comfortable breathing may help down-regulate stress for many people.

Safe default:

1. Sit or lie in a safe place.
2. Breathe gently through the nose if comfortable.
3. Use an unforced inhale and slightly longer, comfortable exhale.
4. Continue 1–5 minutes.
5. Stop for dizziness, tingling, panic, pain, or unusual symptoms.

Never perform hyperventilation, prolonged breath holds, or intense breathwork while driving, in water, standing near hazards, or without appropriate guidance. Medical or panic-related concerns require professional advice.

Grounding alternatives:

- orient visually to five neutral objects;
- feel feet and contact points;
- slow walking;
- humming or reading a calming passage;
- co-regulation with a trusted person;
- prayer or a familiar phrase.

## 6. Recovery, nature, sauna, and cold

Recovery includes sleep, lighter days, rest, social connection, enjoyable movement, nature, nutrition, hydration, and psychological detachment from work.

Sauna and cold exposure are optional practices, not foundations. Screen for cardiovascular, blood-pressure, pregnancy, medication, dehydration, fainting, and other health risks. Never combine extreme heat/cold with dehydration, alcohol, extended fasting, or unsafe breath holds.

Use nature, daylight, beauty, music, play, massage, and quiet as recovery inputs when accessible. Do not instrumentalize every pleasure.

## 7. Substance and digital hygiene

Inspect nicotine, alcohol, cannabis, stimulants, sedatives, recreational drugs, and compulsive digital use without shame. Ask what function the behavior serves: activation, calming, belonging, escape, focus, or sleep.

Avoid abrupt medical or substance changes when withdrawal could be risky. Encourage qualified support where dependence or harm is present.

Digital basics:

- no reactive feeds before the decisive morning action where feasible;
- batch notifications;
- remove high-trigger apps from the home screen;
- charge the phone outside the sleep area if workable;
- define intentional windows for social media and messaging;
- use device-free meals, prayer, training, and conversations selectively.

## 8. Energy protocols

### Low-energy day

- protect safety and essential obligations;
- run floor prayer/meditation, food, hydration, movement, and work routines;
- complete one tiny value-creating action;
- cancel optional intensity;
- diagnose sleep, illness, stress, food, workload, and emotion;
- schedule recovery.

### High-energy day

- keep the same priorities;
- do not activate five new projects;
- use surplus energy to finish, prepare, connect, or recover;
- avoid irreversible commitments made from euphoria or urgency;
- preserve bedtime.

### Travel day

- anchor wake/sleep as reasonably possible;
- use walking, hydration, protein/fiber access, brief prayer, and a floor work action;
- resume the normal system at the next stable cue without compensation or punishment.
