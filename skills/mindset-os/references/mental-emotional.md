## 1. Scope

Use coaching and educational exercises, not diagnosis or treatment. For trauma, severe symptoms, self-harm, mania-like activation, psychosis, eating disorders, addiction, or functional collapse, read [safety.md](safety.md) and recommend qualified care.

## 2. Psychological flexibility

Train six capabilities:

1. Notice internal experience.
2. Name thoughts as thoughts rather than facts.
3. Allow emotion without immediate avoidance or impulsive action.
4. Return attention to the present task.
5. Remember chosen values.
6. Take a small value-aligned action.

Use the phrase: “I am noticing the thought that…” to create distance without arguing with every thought.

## 3. Thought and belief work

### Five-column thought record

```text
Situation | Automatic thought | Emotion/body | Evidence and alternatives | Chosen action
```

### Common cognitive patterns

- all-or-nothing thinking;
- catastrophizing;
- mind reading;
- fortune telling;
- emotional reasoning;
- overgeneralization;
- discounting positives;
- personalization;
- unfair comparison;
- “should” rules;
- survivorship bias;
- sunk-cost attachment;
- confirmation bias;
- outcome bias;
- overconfidence and illusion of control.

Do not label thoughts merely to dismiss them. Identify the useful signal, if any, and update behavior.

### Belief experiment

```text
Prediction: If I ask five qualified prospects, all will reject me and I will lose status.
Test: Ask five using a respectful, clear offer.
Measure: Replies, reasons, emotional recovery time, learning.
Update: Replace global belief with conditional evidence.
```

## 4. Emotional regulation

Use this sequence:

```text
PAUSE -> ORIENT -> NAME -> ALLOW -> LOCATE -> CHOOSE -> ACT -> RECOVER
```

Tools:

- slow exhale breathing;
- sensory grounding;
- emotion labeling;
- urge surfing;
- brief movement;
- journaling;
- talking to a trusted person;
- changing physical context;
- sleep, food, or rest correction;
- scheduled worry/problem-solving;
- acceptance followed by value-aligned action.

Regulation is not forcing calm. The target is sufficient capacity to choose.

### Emotion-to-action map

| Emotion | Possible signal | Skillful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fear | threat or uncertainty | What is the smallest safe test? |
| Shame | belonging or identity threat | What is fact, what needs repair, and what is cruel overreach? |
| Anger | boundary or injustice | What boundary or constructive action is required? |
| Sadness | loss | What needs grieving, support, or release? |
| Envy | hidden desire or comparison | What value does this reveal, and what ethical action is mine? |
| Guilt | behavior violated values | What specific repair is possible? |
| Boredom | under-stimulation or avoidance | Is the task meaningless, unclear, too large, or merely uncomfortable? |

## 5. Confidence and courage

Confidence follows evidence and recovery. Build it through a graded ladder:

1. Define the feared action.
2. Break it into 5–10 difficulty levels.
3. Practice one manageable level repeatedly.
4. Record prediction, outcome, and recovery.
5. Progress when discomfort is workable, not necessarily absent.

Use professional guidance for clinical exposure therapy or trauma-related fears.

Replace “be fearless” with “make fear compatible with action.”

## 6. Resilience and failure

### After-action review

- What was intended?
- What happened?
- What was under direct control?
- What assumptions failed?
- What did the system make easy or hard?
- What needs repair?
- What will change in the next attempt?

### Failure classification

- execution failure;
- strategy failure;
- capacity mismatch;
- skill gap;
- communication failure;
- environment failure;
- bad luck or external shock;
- ethical or relationship failure.

Match the remedy to the class. More discipline does not fix a bad market hypothesis.

## 7. Self-compassion and accountability

Use three statements:

1. **Truth:** name what happened without euphemism.
2. **Humanity:** difficulty and imperfection do not remove worth.
3. **Responsibility:** choose repair and the next action.

Self-compassion without responsibility becomes avoidance. Accountability without humanity becomes shame. Use both.

## 8. Shadow patterns and defenses

Explore carefully and as hypotheses:

- perfectionism protecting against judgment;
- endless learning protecting against exposure;
- busyness protecting against priority;
- charm protecting against intimacy;
- control protecting against uncertainty;
- grandiosity protecting against inadequacy;
- cynicism protecting against hope;
- spiritual certainty protecting against grief or ambiguity;
- status spending protecting against shame;
- rescuing others protecting against one's own needs.

Ask what the pattern once protected, what it now costs, and what safer adult strategy can replace it.

## 9. Professional modalities

Borrow educational skills only within scope:

- CBT-style cognitive and behavioral experiments;
- ACT-style defusion, acceptance, values, and committed action;
- DBT-informed mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness;
- motivational interviewing: autonomy, discrepancy, change talk, and confidence scaling;
- positive psychology: strengths, gratitude, meaning, hope, and relationships;
- self-compassion practices;
- solution-focused questions;
- logotherapy and meaning reflection;
- Stoic control distinction and negative visualization;
- narrative re-authoring;
- behavioral activation principles;
- nonviolent communication.

Do not present a coaching exercise as a substitute for full clinical treatment.
