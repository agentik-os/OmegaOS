## 1. Interpretation rules

- Guidelines describe population-level baselines, not individualized prescriptions.
- Systematic reviews summarize heterogeneous studies; effect sizes and quality vary.
- Association does not establish causation.
- A useful practice can have a modest average effect and still matter when well matched.
- Spiritual meaning does not require scientific proof, but scientific claims do.
- Update recommendations when current health guidance or stronger evidence changes.

## 2. Behavior and goals

### Implementation intentions

Implementation intentions link a specified cue to a response: “If X, then Y.” Research supports their role in goal attainment through cue accessibility and cue-response association.

- Webb & Sheeran research summary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18096108/

### Mental contrasting with implementation intentions

MCII/WOOP combines a desired outcome with realistic obstacles and an if-then plan. Evidence is promising across several behavior domains, with effects dependent on context and study quality.

- Bedtime procrastination randomized trials: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31403339/
- Physical activity and weight loss study: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29265873/

### Habit formation

Repetition in a stable context supports automaticity. Formation time varies; avoid universal day counts.

- Systematic review and meta-analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/
- Context stability study: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9226889/

## 3. Motivation and needs

Self-Determination Theory emphasizes autonomy, competence, and relatedness as important for motivation and wellbeing. Use the framework to design supportive contexts, not to reduce all human motivation to three labels.

- Foundational Ryan & Deci paper: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf
- Basic Psychological Needs overview: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/topics/application-basic-psychological-needs/

## 4. Sleep, movement, and diet

### Sleep

Adults generally need at least seven hours, with individual variation. Regularity, environment, light, caffeine/alcohol timing, and medical sleep disorders matter.

- CDC sleep overview: https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html
- CDC adult sleep duration: https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/data-research/facts-stats/adults-sleep-facts-and-stats.html

### Physical activity

WHO recommends regular activity, generally 150–300 minutes moderate or 75–150 minutes vigorous activity weekly for adults, plus muscle strengthening on two or more days, adapted to ability and conditions.

- WHO guidelines: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128
- WHO physical activity overview: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity

### Diet

WHO guidance emphasizes dietary variety, vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, and nuts, while limiting excessive free sugars, sodium, saturated fat, and industrial trans fats. Individual needs vary.

- WHO healthy diet fact sheet: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet
- WHO healthy diet topic: https://www.who.int/health-topics/healthy-diet

## 5. Meditation, breathing, and gratitude

### Meditation and mindfulness

Evidence suggests mindfulness programs can help some people with stress, anxiety, depression, or pain, but study quality, effects, and individual response vary. Meditation is not risk-free for everyone.

- NCCIH evidence and safety overview: https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety
- Systematic review/meta-analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4142584/

### Breathing

Slow, comfortable breath practices may reduce stress or anxiety and affect autonomic regulation. Protocols and study quality vary; intense breathwork has additional risks.

- Breathing practices review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10741869/
- Slow breathing systematic review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/

### Gratitude

Gratitude interventions show small-to-moderate or context-dependent wellbeing benefits in reviews. Avoid using gratitude to suppress grief, injustice, or needed action.

- Systematic review/meta-analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10393216/
- Broad 2025 meta-analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12280877/

## 6. Spirituality and manifestation

### Spirituality/religiosity

Research often finds associations between spirituality/religiosity and aspects of wellbeing, while outcomes depend on context; negative religious coping or spiritual struggle can also correlate with distress. Spiritual practice should be user-chosen.

- Review of spirituality, religiousness, and mental health: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8462234/
- Review of religion, spirituality, and health: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3671693/

### Manifestation/law of attraction

There is no established evidence that thoughts cosmically cause desired financial events. A 2023 study found manifestation belief associated with greater perceived success and aspiration, but also with attraction to risky investments, get-rich-quick beliefs, and reported bankruptcy. It was not evidence that manifestation causes bankruptcy; it highlights overconfidence risk.

- Primary research article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11616226/
- Publication DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672231181162

Retain useful components—optimism, intention, gratitude, visualization, and action—while applying probability, downside protection, and feedback.

## 7. Jim Rohn and philosophical sources

Jim Rohn's framework is used as a practical philosophical model, not as clinical science or proof that mindset alone determines results. Official concept pages identify the five-part sequence of philosophy, attitude, activity, results, and lifestyle, alongside written goals, self-education, discipline, financial independence, time, communication, and seasons.

- Five Major Pieces: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/concepts/five-major-pieces
- Core Concepts: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/concepts
- Art of Setting Goals: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/concepts/setting-goals
- Journal practice: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/articles/keep-a-journal
- Marketplace value: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/articles/how-to-become-more-valuable-marketplace
- Personal development plan: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/articles/personal-development-plan-that-works
- Associations: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/articles/average-of-five-people-you-spend-time-with
- Mental inputs: https://www.jimrohn.com/wisdom/articles/stand-guard-at-the-door-of-your-mind

Translate claims into hypotheses and practices. Preserve personal responsibility, written goals, reflection, value creation, and disciplined action while accounting for health, systems, market conditions, structural constraints, and luck.

## 8. Evidence limitations

Mindset {OS} intentionally combines:

- high-confidence public-health basics;
- evidence-informed psychological techniques;
- coaching heuristics;
- philosophical practices;
- user-chosen spiritual practices;
- personalized experiments.

Do not blur these categories. When the user requests a medical, clinical, or financial claim, verify current authoritative guidance and recommend qualified professional review where stakes are high.
