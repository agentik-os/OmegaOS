## 1. STORY test

Use **STORY** as a diagnostic, not a fill-in formula:

- **S · Specific** — a concrete moment, decision, image, artifact, or observable behavior.
- **T · Tension** — an unresolved question with meaningful pressure.
- **O · Ownership** — an honest point of view, cost, and responsibility.
- **R · Reversal** — a change in state, belief, relationship, identity, or decision.
- **Y · Yield** — meaning the audience can infer and use.

A story may be quiet and still pass. Spectacle is unnecessary. If R is absent, classify it as an anecdote or example. If S is absent, it is likely abstraction. If O is absent, it may feel borrowed or manipulative.

## 2. Story DNA

Freeze these fields before adaptation:

1. **Core change** — X became Y, or belief A became B.
2. **Pressure** — what made the change costly or necessary.
3. **Choice/event** — the causal hinge.
4. **Proof detail** — the detail that makes it real.
5. **Meaning** — the audience update.
6. **Truth boundary** — what is known, remembered, interpreted, or reconstructed.
7. **Voice marker** — one quality that must survive.
8. **Non-negotiable dignity** — what must not be exploited or distorted.

Every derivative may change order, length, medium, and emphasis. It may not change Story DNA without becoming a new story object.

## 3. Structure library

Select by function. Never present one structure as universal.

### Moment → Meaning

Use for short personal stories and content.

`ordinary world → disruption → response → choice → afterimage → meaning`

Strength: intimacy and specificity.

Risk: overexplaining the lesson.

### Hook → Problem → Pursuit → Payoff

Use for teaching, creator content, or work lessons.

`honest open question → felt problem → attempts/search → earned insight/result`

Strength: clear attention and learning path.

Risk: turning real life into a clean hack; retain failed attempts and uncertainty.

### Desire → Obstacle → Choice → Consequence

Use as the universal causal skeleton.

Strength: makes agency visible.

Risk: false individualism when systems or chance dominate.

### Before → Fracture → Attempts → Pivot → After

Use for transformation and identity stories.

Strength: shows change across time.

Risk: hindsight certainty and false overnight transformation.

### Decision Under Pressure

`context → options → constraint → cost → choice → result → operating rule`

Use for leadership, founder, and professional stories.

Strength: converts story into judgment.

Risk: omitting alternatives or luck.

### Origin with Proof

`unacceptable reality → personal encounter → commitment → first action → first evidence → present mission`

Use for founders, products, communities, and movements.

Strength: joins meaning to action.

Risk: retrospective mythmaking. Keep early ambiguity and evidence.

### Failure → Correction

`belief → confident action → contradiction → cost → diagnosis → changed rule → later test`

Use for teaching and trust.

Strength: proves learning through behavior.

Risk: humblebragging when the failure has no real cost.

### Customer Change

`customer context → stakes → attempted alternatives → decision → intervention → result → remaining limit`

Strength: proof through lived use.

Risk: stealing customer agency or implying sole causality.

### StoryBrand SB7

`character → problem → guide → plan → call to action → avoid failure → achieve success`

Use for message clarity and customer journeys.

Strength: makes the customer's problem and action legible.

Risk: flattening complex relationships and turning every brand into a guide. Use only when the role mapping is true.

### SUCCESs idea model

`simple → unexpected → concrete → credible → emotional → stories`

Use to diagnose memorable ideas.

Strength: communication qualities.

Risk: a checklist cannot replace truth, causality, or a story.

### Hero's Journey

Use for mythic, long-form, identity, and adventure narratives when the material genuinely contains departure, trials, transformation, and return.

Strength: maps separation and reintegration.

Risk: formula, individual hero bias, cultural overreach, and false completion. Never force everyday content into twelve or seventeen beats.

### Save the Cat beat sheet

Use for commercial screen or genre diagnostics when pacing needs a detailed beat map.

Strength: production-friendly specificity.

Risk: mechanical sameness and page-count fetish. Treat beats as diagnostic functions, not mandatory timestamps.

### Truby moral-psychological arc

Use for character-driven long-form work where weakness, need, desire, opponent, plan, battle, self-revelation, and new equilibrium interact.

Strength: joins plot to moral and psychological change.

Risk: over-engineering short stories or assuming every protagonist needs a neat revelation.

### McKee value-charge model

Track scenes by a meaningful value shifting from positive to negative, negative to positive, or a stronger degree.

Use when scenes feel static.

Strength: tests actual movement.

Risk: labeling without understanding human causality.

### Moth personal-story model

Emphasize a true story, personal stakes, clear change, scene, vulnerability with agency, and live telling.

Strength: human connection and oral delivery.

Risk: manufacturing disclosure or turning pain into performance currency.

### Duarte presentation sparkline

Alternate **what is** and **what could be**, widening tension until a new bliss or action becomes possible.

Use for speeches and vision.

Strength: makes contrast and movement visible.

Risk: utopian future without mechanism or proof.

### Data story

`decision question → expected baseline → observed signal → comparison → driver/uncertainty → consequence → action`

Strength: aligns evidence with decision.

Risk: anthropomorphizing data, cherry-picking, or using a human anecdote as statistical proof.

### Story Circle

`comfort → want → unfamiliar situation → adapt → get → pay → return → change`

Use for compact episodic narratives.

Strength: simple cycle and cost.

Risk: false universality; keep as a rough map.

## 4. Scene mechanics

A scene needs a state change. Diagnose with:

`ENTRY STATE → ACTIVE WANT → RESISTANCE → BEHAVIOR → TURN → EXIT STATE`

### Detail hierarchy

Prefer details that:

1. prove the event;
2. reveal character;
3. increase tension;
4. orient the audience;
5. carry motif or meaning.

Remove details that exist only to look cinematic.

### Dialogue

Use exact quotes only when recorded, written, or confidently recalled and confirmed. Otherwise:

- paraphrase without quotation marks;
- label “words to the effect of” when important;
- summarize the exchange;
- omit if accuracy or privacy matters.

Dialogue must change information, power, relationship, or choice. Decorative banter is expensive in short formats.

### Time

Use scene for decisive moments and summary for travel between them. Compress repeated actions transparently. Do not create a single scene from events that occurred across days without labeling the compression.

## 5. Tension and stakes

Tension is the gap between what the audience wants to know and what the story has responsibly revealed.

Forms:

- outcome: what will happen?
- decision: what will they choose?
- identity: who are they really becoming?
- moral: what is the right action?
- relational: will trust hold?
- epistemic: what is actually true?
- procedural: how can this work?
- status: will standing rise or fall?

Stakes are the meaningful cost of failure, not loud adjectives. Test across:

- practical;
- relational;
- identity;
- moral;
- financial;
- time/opportunity;
- physical/safety;
- communal/systemic.

Use only supported stakes. Small but intimate stakes often outperform inflated stakes.

## 6. Character and change

Separate:

- **want** — conscious external objective;
- **need** — deeper capacity or truth, if supported;
- **belief** — model of self/world guiding behavior;
- **strategy** — how they pursue the want;
- **pressure** — what exposes limitations;
- **choice** — behavior revealing priority;
- **change** — new state or updated belief;
- **residue** — what does not resolve.

Avoid diagnosing real people. Describe observed behavior and the storyteller's interpretation separately.

Change may be:

- external success/failure;
- new information;
- belief update;
- identity claim;
- relationship shift;
- value clarification;
- increased capacity;
- surrender or acceptance;
- refusal to change, with consequence.

## 7. Hooks and endings

### Honest hook mechanisms

- concrete contradiction;
- consequence before cause;
- specific object/image with unanswered meaning;
- costly choice;
- status reversal;
- precise claim with proof path;
- withheld context, not withheld truth;
- direct audience tension;
- unusual comparison grounded in the story.

The hook contract: **the story must answer the question the opening creates**.

### Ending functions

Choose one:

- reveal changed meaning;
- show changed behavior;
- return to opening image with new significance;
- leave an earned unresolved question;
- state a compact operating rule;
- invite a proportionate action;
- hand the story to the audience through a transfer question.

Avoid:

- “and that's why you should never give up”;
- three morals;
- motivational inflation;
- unrelated CTA;
- explaining every symbol;
- claiming permanence from one moment.

## 8. Meaning and teaching

Use this ladder:

1. **Event** — what happened?
2. **Pattern** — what recurred or was revealed?
3. **Principle** — what mechanism may explain it?
4. **Boundary** — when would it not apply?
5. **Transfer** — what real decision changes?

Distinguish:

- “This happened to me.”
- “This is what I think it means.”
- “This may generalize under these conditions.”
- “Here is the evidence beyond my story.”

A story earns attention and interpretation. It does not automatically prove the principle.

## 9. Anti-formula rules

- Let structure diagnose missing functions; do not let it dictate facts.
- Mix frameworks only when each solves a distinct problem.
- Never force a hero, villain, redemption, breakthrough, or happy ending.
- Preserve chance, systems, collaborators, and privilege when causally relevant.
- Do not convert complexity into “I finally realized...” unless a real behavioral update followed.
- Prefer one deep turn over many decorative beats.
- Stop shaping when further polish reduces truth or voice.
