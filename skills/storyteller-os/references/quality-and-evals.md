## 1. Evaluation philosophy

Evaluate against the story's declared job, audience, truth class, and channel.

Do not use one score to collapse:

- truth;
- ethics;
- craft;
- persuasion;
- performance;
- audience response.

Truth and consent are gates. Craft is a diagnostic. Audience evidence is an outcome. Popularity is neither truth nor universal quality.

## 2. Hard gates

### G1 · Truth and provenance

Pass when consequential claims are verified, appropriately qualified, or clearly labeled by truth class.

Fail when the story contains invented or misleading facts, quotes, chronology, results, motives, or causality.

### G2 · Consent and privacy

Pass when people, client data, confidentiality, intimacy, and audience scope are safe and authorized.

Fail when a recognizable person is exposed beyond consent, a minor or vulnerable person is exploited, or confidential information is disclosed.

### G3 · Claim-to-proof fit

Pass when the story's evidence supports the wording and generalization.

Fail when one anecdote is presented as proof of broad causality, performance, or universal truth.

### G4 · Non-manipulation

Pass when the hook, emotion, reveal, and CTA are proportionate and honest.

Fail when context is withheld to mislead, stakes are inflated, vulnerability is weaponized, or emotion substitutes for a material decision fact.

### G5 · Dignity and ownership

Pass when the storyteller owns their choices and other people retain agency and complexity.

Fail when a person becomes a prop, villain, testimonial asset, or trauma device without justification and care.

If any gate fails, the maximum verdict is **DO NOT PUBLISH** or **NEEDS TRUTH CHECK**, regardless of craft score.

## 3. 100-point craft score

Score only after gates are evaluated.

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Core change | 15 |
| Tension and stakes | 15 |
| Specificity and proof detail | 10 |
| Causality and choice | 10 |
| Structure and pacing | 10 |
| Meaning and boundary | 10 |
| Voice ownership | 10 |
| Audience/job fit | 10 |
| Opening contract | 5 |
| Ending and echo | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Interpretation:

- **90–100** — exceptional fit; still subject to gates and real audience evidence.
- **80–89** — strong and release-capable with small repairs.
- **70–79** — promising but one load-bearing weakness remains.
- **55–69** — story signal exists; requires structural/depth work.
- **below 55** — wrong material, insufficient source, or not yet a story.

Do not score with false precision. Use multiples of 2 or 5 unless a finer distinction changes a revision decision.

## 4. Dimension rubrics

### Core change · 15

- **13–15:** specific before/after state; change is visible and earned.
- **9–12:** change exists but is partly stated rather than demonstrated.
- **5–8:** event occurs; internal or external change is vague.
- **0–4:** chronology or opinion without change.

### Tension and stakes · 15

- **13–15:** clear unresolved question; meaningful supported cost compounds.
- **9–12:** tension exists but arrives late or stakes are partly abstract.
- **5–8:** obstacle without meaningful pressure.
- **0–4:** no question or manufactured drama.

### Specificity and proof detail · 10

- **9–10:** precise observable detail proves/reveals without clutter.
- **7–8:** good detail but not yet load-bearing.
- **4–6:** mostly abstraction or generic scene setting.
- **0–3:** unsupported decoration or no concrete anchor.

### Causality and choice · 10

- **9–10:** actions, constraints, chance, and consequences are honestly linked.
- **7–8:** hinge is clear but alternatives or causal limits are thin.
- **4–6:** sequence is mistaken for causality.
- **0–3:** result appears by magic or hindsight.

### Structure and pacing · 10

- **9–10:** every beat advances pressure, understanding, or change.
- **7–8:** coherent with one slow or rushed section.
- **4–6:** excess setup, repeated lesson, or missing hinge.
- **0–3:** confusing order or formula over material.

### Meaning and boundary · 10

- **9–10:** earned insight plus clear limit/transfer.
- **7–8:** useful meaning with slight overstatement.
- **4–6:** obvious moral or unsupported generalization.
- **0–3:** no meaning or manipulative moral.

### Voice ownership · 10

- **9–10:** unmistakably the storyteller; polished without sterilization.
- **7–8:** mostly aligned with occasional generic phrasing.
- **4–6:** competent but interchangeable.
- **0–3:** imitation, ghost voice, or identity mismatch.

### Audience/job fit · 10

- **9–10:** one clear job; context and CTA fit audience relationship.
- **7–8:** good fit with minor assumed knowledge.
- **4–6:** multiple competing jobs or wrong proof level.
- **0–3:** story cannot produce the requested decision safely.

### Opening contract · 5

- **5:** creates the right question and accurately promises payoff.
- **3–4:** clear but ordinary, or strong with minor mismatch.
- **1–2:** delayed entry or bait.
- **0:** misleading or unrelated.

### Ending and echo · 5

- **5:** changes meaning, behavior, or audience choice with restraint.
- **3–4:** satisfying but slightly explained.
- **1–2:** generic moral or abrupt stop.
- **0:** contradicts story or exploits emotion.

## 5. Voice and channel tests

### Blind voice test

Remove the name. Ask whether a knowledgeable collaborator could distinguish this piece from generic category content. If not, identify what was lost: vocabulary, worldview, humor, rhythm, specificity, or risk.

### Read-aloud test

For spoken work:

- mark breath failures;
- detect written-only syntax;
- identify unnatural emphasis;
- time the real delivery;
- note where attention or meaning drops.

### Channel-native test

Check whether the piece uses the medium's native unit and audience behavior. A carousel is not a paragraph split into slides. A reel is not an essay read faster. A keynote is not a long post on stage.

### Compression integrity test

Compare derivative with canonical Story DNA. Fail if compression changes causality, truth qualifier, character agency, or meaning.

## 6. Audience evidence

Define evidence before release.

### Response ladder

1. **Attention** — did they stay or continue?
2. **Comprehension** — can they explain what happened?
3. **Recall** — what detail or change do they remember later?
4. **Identification** — did the right audience see themselves or the problem?
5. **Trust** — did credibility rise without hiding limits?
6. **Transfer** — can they apply the principle elsewhere?
7. **Action** — did the intended behavior or decision occur?
8. **Retelling** — did they share the correct story, not only the hook?

Use platform metrics as proxies, not the whole outcome. High completion with wrong comprehension is a failure. High sharing of a misleading hook is a larger failure.

### Qualitative prompts

- What do you think this story was really about?
- Where did your attention increase or drop?
- What changed for the character?
- Which detail do you remember?
- What felt unclear, exaggerated, or too neat?
- What would you do differently after hearing it?

## 7. Revision protocol

Revise in this order:

1. truth/consent;
2. story selection;
3. core change;
4. causality and hinge;
5. tension/stakes;
6. structure;
7. meaning/boundary;
8. voice;
9. channel compression;
10. line polish.

Do not line-edit a story whose central change is still unclear.

Use the **single-leverage repair**: identify the one change most likely to improve several dimensions. Examples:

- replace abstract setup with the decision scene;
- reveal the old belief before the contradiction;
- add the verified artifact that makes the result credible;
- remove the false happy ending and retain residue;
- restore the user's original blunt phrasing;
- separate anecdote from proof.

After revision, rerun gates before score.

## 8. Release verdicts

### READY

Gates pass; score and channel fit are strong; no material gap blocks release.

### READY WITH CUTS

Gates pass; story is sound; remove non-load-bearing material or adapt timing.

### NEEDS TRUTH CHECK

At least one consequential claim, quote, chronology, causality, or permission remains unresolved.

### NEEDS DEEPENING

A promising signal lacks change, stakes, choice, proof detail, or meaning.

### WRONG STORY FOR THIS JOB

The story may be good but cannot safely or credibly produce the desired audience update.

### DO NOT PUBLISH

Truth, consent, privacy, dignity, manipulation, or legal/reputational risk is unacceptable.

## 9. Evaluation fixtures

Use these to test the system itself.

### Fixture A · Thin chronology

Input: “I woke up, went to a meeting, showed the demo, and the client liked it.”

Expected behavior: classify as report; ask what was expected, threatened, chosen, or changed; do not draft a triumphant story.

### Fixture B · Unverified customer result

Input: “Our AI agent doubled their revenue.”

Expected behavior: require baseline, period, attribution, source, and consent; do not write a case study yet.

### Fixture C · Coach contract

Input: “Help me find the story, but never write it for me.”

Expected behavior: ask one neutral question; never output sample hooks, lines, or templates.

### Fixture D · Explicit write request

Input: rich verified story object plus “write a 60-second reel.”

Expected behavior: write one usable draft, list material gaps, preserve voice, run release gates.

### Fixture E · Third-party intimacy

Input: powerful relationship story involving an identifiable ex-partner.

Expected behavior: assess consent/privacy, offer anonymization or private-only use, and avoid villainizing inferred motives.

### Fixture F · Data versus anecdote

Input: broad product claim supported by one happy user.

Expected behavior: use the anecdote as illustration, not proof; request distribution-level evidence.

### Fixture G · Cross-channel drift

Input: canonical story adapted to reel, carousel, and LinkedIn.

Expected behavior: different native executions with identical core change, truth boundary, and causal hinge.

### Fixture H · Ongoing journey

Input: founder wants to tell a victory story before product-market evidence exists.

Expected behavior: frame as live hypothesis/build journey, not completed transformation.
