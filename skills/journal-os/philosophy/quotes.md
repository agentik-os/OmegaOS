# Quote corpus

The only lines MIRROR may quote. **If a line is not in this file, it is not
quoted.** Fewer well-sourced lines beat a large unreliable pile, and the
internet's supply of "Lao Tzu" and "Marcus Aurelius" aphorisms is substantially
fabricated.

## Rules

1. **At most one line per journal entry, and the default is none.** A quotation
   is optional inside a reflection that is itself optional. Most reflections
   that fire should carry no quotation at all.
2. **Never invent a quotation, an author, a work or a locator.** If you are not
   confident a line is genuine, say so inline or leave it out.
3. **Evidence first, line last.** A reflection that opens with a quotation is
   malformed by construction. The order is always: the day's evidence, the
   observation, then at most one line.
4. **Never retranslate.** The entry may be in French; the quotation stays in the
   rendering given here and is glossed in the entry's language if needed. A line
   the model translated itself is no longer a quotation and the attribution
   stops being true.
5. **Never merge two lines** into one, never trim a line so it says something the
   passage does not say, and never attribute a paraphrase as a quotation.
6. **Never use a line to close an unresolved contradiction.** See
   `reflection-engine.md`, refusal 3.
7. **Prefer the plain sentence.** A quotation only lands for a reader who
   already respects the source. Otherwise it is an unearned appeal to authority
   sitting on top of the actual point.
8. Renderings here are taken from public-domain translations where one exists
   (George Long for Marcus Aurelius and Epictetus, Richard Gummere for Seneca's
   *Letters*). Excerpts are single sentences. For the Chinese texts the original
   is given with a literal gloss, because the popular English versions are the
   least reliable part of that tradition.

## Labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[primary source]` | Located in the source text at the stated locator. |
| `[translation varies]` | Genuine passage; the English wording depends on the translator. Do not treat the exact words as fixed. |
| `[seminar-sourced, wording varies]` | Jim Rohn's spoken corpus. Consistent with his documented teaching, but no page citation exists and recordings differ. |
| `[popular attribution, unverified]` | Circulates widely, cannot be located in the named source. Use only with the marker visible, or not at all. |
| `[modern slogan]` | A later condensation of a real passage. The passage is real; the slogan is not a quotation. |

## A. The day, and the practice of reviewing it

> "I examine my entire day and go back over what I have done and said, hiding
> nothing from myself, passing nothing by."
> **Seneca, *De Ira* III.36.3** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> Latin: *totum diem meum scrutor factaque ac dicta mea remetior; nihil mihi
> ipse abscondo, nihil transeo.*
> Use once, when explaining what this OS actually is. Not a nightly refrain.

> "What bad habit have you cured today? What fault have you resisted? In what
> respect are you better?"
> **Sextius, reported by Seneca, *De Ira* III.36.1** `[primary source]`
> The three questions the practice descends from. Their value here is that
> "nothing today" is a permitted answer to all three.

> "Do not let sleep close your tired eyes until you have gone over the day's
> deeds three times: where did I go wrong, what did I do, what duty did I leave
> undone?"
> **Pythagorean *Golden Verses*, lines 40 to 44** `[primary source]
> [translation varies]` Authorship and date are uncertain (a late compilation).
> Epictetus quotes these verses to his students in the *Discourses* (III.10;
> the locator varies by edition).

> "The unexamined life is not worth living for a human being."
> **Socrates, in Plato, *Apology* 38a** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> Use almost never. It is true, famous, and so worn that it reads as filler.

## B. Contradictions

> "It is not things that disturb people, but their judgments about things."
> **Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 5** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For the moment a conclusion has been recorded as an event. Best deployed as a
> question rather than a quotation: what is the observation underneath that?

> "No longer talk at all about the kind of man that a good man ought to be, but
> be such."
> **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 10.16** `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` For a declared value with no behavioral evidence behind it. Sharp,
> and it can cut too hard on a depleted day. Check the state first.

> "Every habit and faculty is confirmed and strengthened by the corresponding
> actions."
> **Epictetus, *Discourses* II.18.1** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> The mechanism behind a repeating gap, stated without blame.

> "Failure is a few errors in judgment, repeated every day."
> **Jim Rohn** `[seminar-sourced, wording varies]`
> Only when a count has actually crossed its threshold, and only after the
> season check. Never on a single day's miss.

> "Knowing others is wisdom; knowing oneself is clarity."
> **Daodejing 33** (知人者智，自知者明) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> Rarely useful. Included because it is genuine and constantly misquoted.

## C. Failures and bad outcomes

> "Of things that exist, some are up to us and some are not up to us."
> **Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1.1** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> The core split for a bad outcome. Almost always better as the question: which
> part of that was yours?

> "If you are pained by any external thing, it is not this that disturbs you,
> but your own judgment about it, and it is in your power to wipe out this
> judgment now."
> **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.47** `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` Use this instead of the fabricated "you have power over your mind"
> line, which says something similar and is not a real quotation.

> "We suffer more often in imagination than in reality."
> **Seneca, *Letters* 13.4** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> Latin: *saepius opinione quam re laboramus.* For a day spent dreading
> something that then took eleven minutes. Not for a day of real loss.

> "There is nowhere a man can retreat to more quiet or more free from trouble
> than his own soul."
> **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 4.3** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> Restricted: this is a consolation line, and consolation is what this layer is
> most often wrong to offer. Only when the user has already stated the facts
> plainly and is not being helped past them.

> "We must all suffer one of two things: the pain of discipline or the pain of
> regret and disappointment."
> **Jim Rohn** `[seminar-sourced, wording varies]`
> Only alongside two real numbers, the cost of moving it and the cost of doing
> it, both supplied by the user.

## D. Lessons, and the refusal to manufacture one

> "To know that you do not know is best."
> **Daodejing 71** (知不知，上) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> The strongest line in the corpus for an honestly undetermined day, and the
> only quotation that is ever appropriate when the LESSONS section is empty.

> "In pursuing learning, something is gained daily; in pursuing the Way,
> something is dropped daily."
> **Daodejing 48** (為學日益，為道日損) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For the weekly subtraction question: what came off the list, not what got
> finished.

> "If it is not right, do not do it; if it is not true, do not say it."
> **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 12.17** `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` A standard, not a consolation. Fits an integrity lesson with real
> evidence behind it.

> "Everyone knows the usefulness of the useful, but no one knows the usefulness
> of the useless."
> **Zhuangzi 4** (人皆知有用之用，而莫知無用之用也) `[primary source]
> [translation varies]` For a day whose value does not appear in any objective,
> when that is genuinely true and not an excuse being constructed.

## E. Recovery and rest

> "It is the hollow that makes the vessel useful."
> **Daodejing 11**, paraphrasing the received text `[primary source]
> [translation varies]` Full closing formula: 有之以為利，無之以為用, "what is
> there provides the benefit, what is not there provides the use." The argument
> for unscheduled time and for the three-mission cap.

> "One who stands on tiptoe is not steady; one who strides cannot keep going."
> **Daodejing 24** (企者不立，跨者不行) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For a forcing day. A claim about sustainability, not about virtue.

> "The mind must be given relaxation; it will rise better and keener."
> **Seneca, *De Tranquillitate Animi* 17** `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` Seneca defending rest, which is useful precisely because he is the
> last person expected to. Only for a user who moralizes against their own rest.

> "The work completed, withdraw."
> **Daodejing 9** (功遂身退) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For an inability to stop after the thing was actually finished.

## F. People

> "The best way of avenging yourself is not to become like the wrongdoer."
> **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 6.6** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For a conflict the user is still rehearsing at 23:00.

> "Precisely because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with
> him."
> **Daodejing 22** (夫唯不爭，故天下莫能與之爭) `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` For pressure that hardened a position. Never for a situation where
> the user needs to set a boundary; yielding is not the answer to being
> mistreated.

> "You are the average of the five people you spend the most time with."
> **Jim Rohn** `[popular attribution, unverified]`
> Universally attributed to him and consistent with his association teaching,
> but the original wording and occasion are not documented and it is a
> rhetorical figure, not a measurement. **Do not present it as a statistic.**
> Mindset OS explicitly corrects the arithmetic: do not assume exactly five
> people determine a person.

> Marcus Aurelius opens the *Meditations* with Book 1, a list of specific debts
> to named people and what each taught him. `[primary source]`
> Not a quotation, a structure worth borrowing. The most famous private journal
> in the canon begins with gratitude to individuals, by name, with the specific
> thing attached.

## G. Tomorrow

> "Nothing ought to be unexpected by us. Our minds should be sent forward in
> advance to meet all problems."
> **Seneca, *Letters* 91.4** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> *Premeditatio malorum* feeding the Tomorrow Protocol. Bounded: one mission,
> one or two named obstacles, one rehearsed response. Contraindicated when the
> user is anxious or depleted.

> "If one does not know to which port one is sailing, no wind is favourable."
> **Seneca, *Letters* 71.3** `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For a day of high activity with no objective attached to any of it.

> "It is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste much of it."
> **Seneca, *On the Shortness of Life* 1.3** `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` Restricted: reads as a reproach. Only when the user raised the theme
> themselves.

> "A journey of a thousand li begins beneath one's feet."
> **Daodejing 64** (千里之行，始於足下) `[primary source]`
> The common rendering "begins with a single step" is loose; the text says
> "beneath one's feet". Heavily worn. Use only if the user has framed a mission
> as too large to start.

## H. The identity vote

> "Such as are your habitual thoughts, such also will be the character of your
> mind; for the soul is dyed by the thoughts."
> **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 5.16** `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` The vote is cast by repetition, not by intention.

> "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."
> **James Clear, *Atomic Habits* (2018), chapter 2** `[primary source]`
> The modern formulation the IDENTITY VOTE line descends from. In the house
> canon (Mindset OS Tier A). Attribute it to Clear, not to anyone ancient.

> "Return again to the uncarved block."
> **Daodejing 28** (復歸於樸) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> For an Identity Contract being carved so fine that ordinary weeks now count as
> failures.

> "Success is nothing more than a few simple disciplines, practiced every day."
> **Jim Rohn** `[seminar-sourced, wording varies]`
> Only in a weekly rollup with the counts visible. Nightly it is a poster.

## I. Privacy, and the anti-content rule

> "Fame or your own person: which is closer to you?"
> **Daodejing 44** (名與身孰親) `[primary source] [translation varies]`
> The securely sourced line for the anti-content rule. Chapter 44 continues
> 知足不辱，知止不殆, "know contentment and you will not be disgraced, know when
> to stop and you will meet no danger."

> "One who shows himself off is not illumined; one who boasts has no merit."
> **Daodejing 24** (自見者不明，自伐者無功) `[primary source] [translation
> varies]` For a day visibly shaped to be shown.

> The *Meditations* carries the traditional Greek title *Ta eis heauton*, "to
> himself". It has no dedication, no addressee, and no argument aimed at a
> reader. `[primary source, textual fact]`
> Not a quotation. The strongest single argument in the layer for the journal
> being private by default: the most quoted work of practical philosophy in the
> Western canon is a private notebook that was never launched.

## Deliberately excluded

These are genuine, and this OS still does not use them.

- **"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes
  the way."** *Meditations* 5.20 `[primary source]`, popularized as "the
  obstacle is the way", which is Ryan Holiday's 2014 book title `[modern
  slogan]`. **Excluded because it is the single most effective way to convert an
  honest failure into comfort**, which is exactly the artificial positivity this
  OS forbids. See the worked bad example in `README.md`.
- **"What does not kill me makes me stronger."** Nietzsche, *Twilight of the
  Idols*, "Maxims and Arrows" 8 `[primary source]`. Genuine Nietzsche, not
  Stoic, and forbidden here as a consolation closer.
- **"Amor fati."** Nietzsche, *The Gay Science* 276 `[primary source]`. The
  phrase is Nietzsche's, applied to Stoicism retroactively. Usable as a concept
  label with that caveat stated, never presented as an ancient Stoic term. The
  genuine Stoic equivalents are *Meditations* 7.57 and *Enchiridion* 8.
- **"The philosopher's school is a surgery: you ought not to leave it in
  pleasure, but in pain."** Epictetus, *Discourses* III.23.30 `[primary
  source]`. Excluded because MIRROR does not have Epictetus's standing with this
  user, and "do not shame, investigate" outranks it.

## Known fabrications and misattributions: never use

Each of these circulates widely. None may appear in a journal entry, including
with a hedge.

| Line | Attributed to | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| "Watch your thoughts, they become words; watch your words, they become actions..." | Lao Tzu (also Gandhi, Thatcher, the Talmud) | Not in the Daodejing. Traced in various forms to a 1977 newspaper item credited to Frank Outlaw. |
| "Nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished." | Lao Tzu | Not locatable in the Daodejing in this form. A modern paraphrase. |
| "When I let go of what I am, I become what I might be." | Lao Tzu | Stephen Mitchell's interpretive version, which he presents as a version rather than a translation. Not a rendering of any located passage. |
| "You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength." | Marcus Aurelius | No located passage renders this. A modern condensation. Use *Meditations* 8.47 instead, which says something close and is real. |
| "Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose..." | Viktor Frankl | Not found in Frankl's published work; the Frankl institute has stated it is not his. Popularized through Stephen Covey. |
| "When the student is ready, the teacher will appear." | Lao Tzu, or the Buddha | Neither. Modern theosophical provenance, no classical source. |
| "Motivation is what gets you started. Habit is what keeps you going." | Jim Rohn, or Zig Ziglar | Attribution unresolved between them. Do not attribute. |
| "Fall down seven times, stand up eight." | Confucius | A Japanese proverb (七転び八起き), not Confucius. |
| "Everything happens for a reason." | Various | No source, and forbidden separately as a motivational cliché. |
| "Discipline is the bridge between goals and accomplishment." | Jim Rohn | Consistent with his teaching and widely attributed, but unsourced. If used at all, mark `[seminar-sourced, wording varies]`. Prefer the discipline-against-regret line, which is better attested. |

## When in doubt

Say so, inline, in the entry. "A line often attributed to Marcus Aurelius, which
I cannot source, says roughly this" is an acceptable sentence and an honest one.
**Dropping a doubtful line silently is also acceptable. Presenting it as
sourced is not.**
