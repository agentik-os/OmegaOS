# Style and self-presentation

The pre-verbal layer. Treat it as craft, never as a moral upgrade. Someone who dresses badly has not failed at being a person, they have not been taught a skill that most people are never explicitly taught, and the coaching only works if you hold that line in your tone as well as in your content.

Almost everything in this document that concerns taste is **[P]**. There is no universal correct look, and a coach who invents one is doing the user harm dressed as help.

## 1. What self-presentation actually communicates

Clothes and grooming are a fast, low-bandwidth signal that other people read before a word is spoken and continue to read afterwards. What they actually carry **[E3]**:

| Signal | How it is read |
| --- | --- |
| Care | Whether the user takes their own life seriously enough to attend to it |
| Self-respect | Whether they treat themselves as worth a small daily effort |
| Effort level | How much of a production this was, and whether it was calibrated to the occasion |
| Taste | A set of choices that says something about how they see |
| Subculture membership | Which rooms they belong in, and which they are visiting |
| Context awareness | Whether they read the room before they entered it |

That is the honest list. Notice that all six are about the user, not about the observer's approval. The signal that matters most is the first one, and it is also the cheapest to send.

The effect size is real but bounded and mostly front-loaded: presentation changes the first minute a great deal and the fifth hour very little **[E3]**. Coach it as a bottleneck-removal job, not as a growth engine. See [attraction-fundamentals.md](attraction-fundamentals.md) for where it sits among the other factors.

## 2. What it does not communicate

It does not communicate worth. It is not evidence about whether the user deserves affection, and a user who has fused the two is working on the wrong problem in the wrong document. When a style question arrives wrapped in self-loathing, name that out loud and route half the work to [inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md), because a better coat on an unchanged self-concept produces a well-dressed person who still cannot be looked at. It also does not communicate income, and never has: fit and condition read as put-together at every price point, and expensive-but-badly-fitted reads worse than cheap-but-well-fitted, reliably **[E3]**.

## 3. The order of operations

Money and effort spent out of order is money wasted. The sequence that actually pays **[E3]**:

```text
1. FIT          does the garment match this body
2. CONDITION    clean, unworn-out, unstained, pressed, intact
3. MAINTENANCE  hair cut on a schedule, nails, teeth, shoes resoled
4. COHERENCE    the pieces agree with each other and with the occasion
5. COLOUR       a palette that works on this person and this skin
6. SPEND        anything that costs real money, last, and only after 1 to 5
```

Nobody skips to 6 and improves. A user asking which brand to buy is usually asking a step-6 question with a step-1 problem, and the correct coaching move is to say so once, plainly, then fix step 1.

## 4. Fit, concretely

Fit is the single biggest variable and the one most people have never had explained. It is also almost entirely objective, which makes it the part of this document that is not **[P]**.

| Garment | The test | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shirt, blouse | Shoulder seam sits at the edge of the shoulder bone; you can pinch a small amount of fabric at the ribs but not a handful | Bought for the neck or the chest, so the body is a tent |
| T-shirt | Sleeve ends mid-bicep, hem ends around mid-fly, shoulder seam on the shoulder | Sleeves to the elbow, hem to mid-thigh |
| Jacket, blazer | Shoulder seam at the shoulder, sleeve shows a little shirt cuff, buttons without strain lines from the fastening | Sized up for comfort, so it hangs off the frame |
| Trousers | Waist stays up without a belt fighting it, rise sits where your body actually creases, hem breaks lightly on the shoe or ends clean above it | Too long, pooling on the shoe, which shortens the whole silhouette |
| Jeans | Same as trousers, plus a leg opening that suits the shoe worn with them | A leg opening that swallows the shoe |
| Dress, skirt | Hangs from the shoulder or waist without pulling across the widest point, hem at a length chosen rather than inherited | Pulling horizontally anywhere, which always reads as too small |
| Knitwear | Follows the torso without gripping, sleeve ends at the wrist bone | Body-length sweaters worn as tunics by accident |
| Coat | Fits over the layers actually worn under it, sleeve covers the wrist | Bought to fit over nothing, then worn over a jumper |

Bodies, briefly, without prescribing an ideal:

- **Broad shoulders or a full chest or bust:** buy for the widest point, then have the waist and body taken in. This is the single most common tailoring job.
- **Narrow or slight frames:** structure helps (a shoulder seam that holds its shape), volume does not. Layering adds presence without bulk.
- **Long torso or short legs, and the reverse:** rise and hem placement do the work here, more than any other variable. A higher rise lengthens the leg line, a lower one shortens it.
- **Larger bodies:** fit and drape matter more, not less. Oversized clothing to hide a body reliably makes the body read larger, and the honest version of this advice is about proportion, not concealment.
- **Very tall or very short:** the off-the-rack size range was not built for you, so tailoring stops being optional and becomes the whole game.
- **Seated fit, for wheelchair users:** the rack is cut for standing bodies. Jacket length, trouser rise, back rise and bunching at the hip are the four things to solve, and a tailor solves all four.

## 5. Tailoring, the highest-return spend in this document

Say this plainly to any user who will listen: taking three garments they already own to a tailor will do more for how they look than several hundred spent on new clothes **[E3]**. Hemming trousers, taking in a shirt body, shortening a sleeve and adjusting a waist are inexpensive, fast, and the difference is immediately visible to everyone except the person wearing it.

Coaching sequence: pick the three garments they wear most, get them altered, wait two weeks, then reassess the wardrobe. Most users discover they need far less new clothing than they thought.

## 6. Grooming and hygiene, stated once, plainly

This section exists because some adults were genuinely never told, and the cost of not saying it is high while the cost of saying it once, neutrally, is nothing. State it as mechanics. Do not moralize, do not imply the user has failed, do not repeat it later in the session.

The list, complete and unembellished:

- Wash the whole body daily, or on the schedule the user's skin and climate require, with soap on the areas that need it.
- Deodorant or antiperspirant daily, applied to clean skin, reapplied on long days.
- Teeth twice a day, plus flossing or interdental brushes. Breath is downstream of teeth and gums, not of mints.
- Nails short and clean, hands and feet.
- Hair washed at whatever interval suits its type, which varies enormously and is not a moral scale.
- Clean clothes every wear for anything against the skin. Socks and underwear daily. Outerwear on its own schedule.
- Clothes that smell clean rather than of the machine, the drying rack, or last month's cupboard.
- Body hair, wherever it grows: a choice, not a rule. The only rule is that whatever the user chooses should look chosen rather than neglected.

If a user reports that this is genuinely hard to maintain, that is information rather than a failure. Persistent difficulty with basic self-care is a common feature of depression, burnout, ADHD and chronic illness, and it is **[C]**: name it gently, do not build a grooming plan on top of it, and route via [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md).

## 7. Hair, skin and teeth as maintenance

Frame all three as upkeep on a schedule, not as transformation projects. Transformation framing produces one dramatic attempt and then nothing.

- **Hair.** The cut matters more than the product, and the cadence matters more than the cut. A good cut that has grown out for four months is not a good cut. Find someone who asks questions before cutting, tell them what you actually do to your hair each morning (thirty seconds, or fifteen minutes), and let them design for that. Hair loss is a normal thing that happens to a lot of people, and the two workable responses are a shorter cut that stops fighting it or a medical conversation with a professional. Comb-overs and concealers read exactly as what they are **[P]** on the styling question, **[C]** if the distress is significant.
- **Skin.** Wash, moisturize, use sun protection. That is the whole floor and it covers most people. Persistent acne, rosacea or eczema is a dermatologist question, not a product question, and the routing is worth more than any routine you could suggest.
- **Teeth.** Cleaning and a hygienist appointment do most of the visible work. Whitening is **[P]** and easy to overdo. Alignment is expensive, slow, and genuinely changes how people smile in photographs, which is a real consideration and a large expense that belongs to the user alone.

## 8. Glasses, facial hair, and the features people are self-conscious about

Treat every one of these as a design variable rather than a defect to be corrected. The framing is the intervention.

- **Glasses.** A frame is one of the few items that sits on the face all day, so it is worth more attention than a shirt. Choose for the actual head, get them adjusted so they sit level, keep the lenses clean. Glasses are a feature, and a well-chosen frame is one of the fastest visible upgrades available **[P]**.
- **Facial hair.** A choice with maintenance attached. The only failure mode is the un-chosen version: a neck untended, a shape that grew rather than got designed, patchiness fought rather than worked with.
- **Features the user dislikes.** A nose, a scar, a height, a hairline, a chest, a birthmark, a stoma, a prosthesis, a visible difference of any kind. Two truths held together: presentation can be designed around any of them, and the amount of attention the user pays to the feature is not the amount anyone else pays. Do not join the user in treating the feature as the problem. If the preoccupation is intense, intrusive, or driving avoidance or repeated checking, stop the style coaching and treat it as body dysmorphia, **[C]**, per [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md).

## 9. Context and subculture beat the abstract ideal

There is no universally attractive way to dress, and any source that says otherwise is selling something **[P]**. There are only rooms, and how a person reads inside them.

Coach the user to answer three questions before any purchase:

1. **Which rooms am I actually in?** Not aspirational rooms. The ones on the calendar this month.
2. **What is the grammar of those rooms?** Every scene has one: a climbing gym, a conference, a jazz bar, a mosque, a startup office, a village pub, a queer club night. Reading it is a social skill, and it is the same skill as [social-calibration.md](social-calibration.md) applied to objects.
3. **Where do I want to sit inside that grammar?** Fluent and unremarkable, fluent with one signature, or deliberately outside it. All three are legitimate. Only the fourth option, accidentally outside it, costs something.

Dressing for an imaginary generic observer produces the beige result that everyone recognizes and nobody remembers.

## 10. Building taste instead of copying a template

A copied template gives one outfit and no capability. Taste is trainable, and the method is unglamorous **[E3]**:

```text
COLLECT   50 to 100 images of people whose look you react to.
          People in real clothes, not campaign photography. No commentary yet.
SORT      Group them. Do not name the groups until they are grouped.
EXTRACT   Find the common thread. It is usually 3 or 4 concrete variables:
          a silhouette, a palette, a level of formality, a texture, a shoe.
TRANSLATE Write it as rules you can shop with, in plain words.
TEST      Buy or borrow ONE piece against the rules. Wear it three times.
OBSERVE   How you feel in it, whether you reach for it, what people say.
ITERATE   Keep, adjust, or discard the rule. Re-run monthly, not daily.
```

Two calibration instruments the user already has and does not use:

- **Repeated compliments.** A one-off compliment is noise. The same comment from three unrelated people is a signal about what is working. Have the user keep the list.
- **The reach rate.** What they actually put on when they are late and not thinking. That garment is telling the truth about fit and comfort, and it is usually the template for the next purchase.

All of this is **[P]**. Your job is to help the user find their own thread, not to hand them yours.

## 11. The uniform strategy

For users who find clothes tedious and want to spend zero ongoing attention on them, the uniform is the correct answer and not a lesser one **[E3]**.

```text
Pick ONE silhouette that fits and suits the rooms you are in.
Buy 3 to 5 of the top in colours that all work with
2 to 3 of the bottom, plus 2 pairs of shoes (one casual, one better).
One good jacket. One coat. Done.
Every combination works because you built it that way.
Re-decide once a year, not once a morning.
```

The uniform removes decisions, guarantees coherence, makes the wardrobe cheaper over time because pieces get replaced rather than accumulated, and reads as intentional. Its only real cost is variety, which many people do not want anyway.

## 12. Budget realism and the second-hand path

Never build a plan that requires money the user did not say they had. The genuinely low-cost sequence, in order of return per unit currency:

1. Wash, repair and press what already exists, then discard or donate everything that does not fit and never will. Both free, and the second one improves every subsequent morning.
2. Tailor the three most-worn items (small), then resole or replace the shoes actually worn. Shoes and their condition are noticed far more than people expect **[E3]**.
3. Second-hand and charity shops, where the trick is that the fit rules from section 4 let you filter a rack fast. Good second-hand plus tailoring beats new mid-market on both fit and material.
4. Only then, new pieces, one at a time, against the rules from section 10.

## 13. Body composition, handled carefully

Say all of this, in this order, and do not let it expand to fill the session:

- It is one factor among many, and it is neither necessary nor sufficient for being wanted.
- It is slow. Months, not weeks, and the presentation levers above are faster and cheaper.
- Training earns its place in this OS mainly through energy, sleep, mood and posture, which feed presence directly ([presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md)), rather than through appearance alone **[E2]**.
- The programming itself is not this OS's job. Hand it to Health & Energy OS and stop.
- Clothing that fits the current body improves the current body's presentation today, which is the highest-return move available while anything slower is underway.

Hard stop: if the user describes food restriction, compulsive exercise, purging, body checking, weighing several times a day, or intense distress about a specific body part, this is **[C]**. Stop the appearance coaching entirely, say why in one honest sentence, and route through [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md). Do not negotiate an appearance plan with someone in that state, however reasonable their request sounds.

## 14. The smell layer

Smell is processed fast, remembered well, and almost never coached **[E3]**.

The base is hygiene and clean fabric, and it is not optional, because fragrance over an unwashed base makes things worse rather than better. Fragrance itself is **[P]** entirely: if the user wants one, the method is to test on their own skin and wait several hours (skin chemistry changes a scent) and to buy small. Dosage is the actual error most people make. It should be discoverable at conversational distance, not across a room, and if it arrives before the user does it is too much. Some people are sensitive or allergic, so in a small office, a gym, a clinic or a long car journey, unscented is the considerate default.

## 15. Posture, the upgrade no garment provides

No clothing fixes a collapsed chest, a tucked chin or a body braced against the room. Posture and ease change how every garment hangs and how approachable a person reads, for free.

This is the one section where the answer lives elsewhere: run [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md). Coach relaxation and openness rather than rigidity. A held military posture reads as tension, not confidence, and tension is legible at ten metres.

## 16. The procrastination trap

Watch for this, because it is common and it feels like progress. A style project is measurable, controllable, purchasable and completely safe, which makes it the perfect place to hide from the actually frightening thing, which is talking to a person.

Diagnostic signals: a third week of research with nothing bought, a wardrobe overhaul proposed before a single conversation has happened, a request to postpone all social exposure until the new clothes arrive, or the belief that a specific purchase is the precondition for going out.

The coaching move: cap self-presentation at one change per cycle (the OS-wide rule), keep the exposure reps running in parallel and unchanged, and name the pattern once, without mockery. If the avoidance is the real problem, the document is [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md), not this one.

## 17. Photographs of yourself, and honest feedback

Most people have no accurate idea what they look like to others, because the mirror is reversed, familiar and lit by a room they chose.

Collect: full-length front, both profiles, and a seated shot, in daylight, in the three outfits they actually wear most, taken by another person rather than held at arm's length. This is the "before" evidence, and it is what makes any later change assessable rather than vibes.

Getting feedback that is worth anything **[E3]**:

- Ask a specific question, never "do I look okay". Ask "which of these three reads most like a person who has their life together" or "where does this jacket look wrong to you".
- Ask several people separately, then look for agreement. One opinion is taste, three matching opinions is signal.
- Ask people whose own presentation the user admires, which is a different set from people who love them.
- Ask for a rank order rather than a verdict. Ranking produces honesty that yes-or-no questions do not.
- Anyone whose feedback is only ever reassuring is out of the sample, kindly.

Photos for dating apps are a separate technical problem with different rules. Route to [digital-and-apps.md](digital-and-apps.md) and [digital_profile_audit.md](../protocols/digital_profile_audit.md).

## 18. Running the audit

Full procedure in [self_presentation_audit.md](../protocols/self_presentation_audit.md). What it needs before it can say anything useful:

```text
BEFORE EVIDENCE
  photos            front, both profiles, seated, daylight, three real outfits
  wear log          what was actually worn for 14 days, and what was reached for
  fit pass          every regularly worn garment against the section 4 tests
  condition pass    stains, wear, hems, shoes, everything binary and objective
  context list      the rooms actually attended this month
  compliment list   what people have actually said, more than once
  budget            a real number, stated by the user
  constraints       body, climate, faith, workplace rules, sensory needs, disability
OUTPUT
  one fit fix, one condition fix, one context fix, one maintenance schedule
  everything else to a NOT NOW list
```

One change per cycle. An audit that returns fourteen actions returns zero actions.

## 19. Floor, Standard, Deep

| Tier | Practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Floor** | Clean, fitting clothes and the section 6 basics, every day. Two minutes. Survives a bad week and requires no money. |
| **Standard** | The Floor plus: one maintenance schedule kept (haircut, shoes, laundry cadence), one tailoring job done, the wear log running, the reference collection growing. |
| **Deep** | A full wardrobe audit, a taste thread extracted and written down, a uniform designed, a second-hand sweep with the fit rules in hand, honest feedback collected from three people. |

Deep is optional forever. Floor is not a moral test, it is the level at which nothing about the user's presentation is doing damage.

## 20. Refused here

- **Fake status props: rented cars, borrowed watches, staged wealth, invented biography.** Refused, with the reasoning in [refusals.md](refusals.md). It obtains interest under false pretences and it comes due.
- **Peacocking**, deliberately odd clothing engineered to provoke a comment. Corrected, not refused: it buys attention from strangers and spends credibility with everyone.
- **Any universal claim about what is attractive.** Refuse to make one. Taste is **[P]**, culture and subculture vary, and inventing a universal is how this genre earns its reputation.
- **Appearance coaching for someone in body-image distress.** Refused while the distress is live. **[C]**, route out, come back later if they want to.
- **Ranking the user against other people.** It teaches a comparison habit that damages the inner game the OS is trying to build ([inner-game-and-self-worth.md](inner-game-and-self-worth.md)) and it improves nothing.
