# Dating strategy

The campaign layer: where the user meets people, how the early meetings are designed, how pace and honesty are handled, and how a season is judged. Everything here sits on top of [ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md), and the gate in [consent_check.md](../protocols/consent_check.md) runs before any output in this mode.

The organizing idea: this is a search for fit between two free people, not a funnel with a conversion rate. Every failure mode in this document comes from importing the funnel metaphor and then acting on it.

## 1. The reframe, and why the funnel metaphor breaks

Most users arrive asking how to get more dates. The better question is how to meet enough people, in enough depth, that a genuine match becomes likely, and then how to recognize one.

The funnel metaphor breaks on three points **[E3]**:

- **It makes the other person a conversion.** Which changes the user's behaviour in ways that are visible, and repels precisely the people who are paying attention.
- **It optimizes the wrong quantity.** More first dates with poorly matched people is more work, more disappointment, and no more relationships.
- **It hides the real bottleneck.** Which is almost never persuasion. It is either exposure (the user meets nobody new) or fit (the user meets people, and the matching is poor), and the interventions for those two are opposite.

Diagnose which one is live before designing anything. If the user has had no new social contact in six weeks, no amount of date-craft is the answer.

## 2. The honest mathematics of meeting people

Volume matters. It matters less than people think, and it cannot be skipped **[E3]**.

```text
Compatibility is rare and roughly independent of effort per encounter.
So the number of genuine possibilities scales with people met, not with
effort spent on any one of them.

BUT: quality of context multiplies the base rate enormously.
100 strangers in a bar and 100 people met through a shared activity
are not the same 100. The second set is pre-filtered on availability,
values, schedule and interest, and the encounters are repeated.

And: the ceiling is real. Nobody has infinite attention.
A dozen serious conversations a season is a lot. Two hundred matches is not.
```

The two claims to make to a user, both true and rarely said together: there is no route to a relationship that does not pass through meeting people, and grinding volume in low-quality contexts is the most common way to burn out before the search has properly started.

## 3. Where people actually meet, ranked

Ranked by yield per unit of effort, honestly, including the costs **[E3]**:

| Context | Yield | Speed | Signal quality | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Repeated shared activity** (sport, choir, climbing, class, volunteering, a regular night, a team) | Highest | Slowest | Highest | Proximity plus repeated exposure do real work on liking **[E1]**, and you see people behave over time rather than perform for an hour |
| **Social graph** (friends of friends, parties, weddings, colleagues of friends) | High | Medium | High | Pre-vetted by someone who knows both people, and social cost keeps behaviour honest |
| **Apps** | Medium | Fast | Lowest | Unmatched for volume and for reaching outside the user's graph, worst for signal. See [digital-and-apps.md](digital-and-apps.md) |
| **Interest-adjacent public spaces** (a gig, a gallery, a conference, a game shop) | Low to medium | Slow | Medium | Shared context gives something real to talk about, but no repetition |
| **Cold contexts** (a street, a train, a supermarket) | Lowest | Fast | Very low | High refusal cost for the other person, high anxiety cost for the user, tiny yield. Not forbidden, rarely worth building a strategy on |

Two honest notes on the ranking. The top row is slowest, which is exactly why people abandon it for the third row and then complain about the third row. And the bottom row is where most of the classic material in this genre concentrates its attention, which tells you more about the material than about the world.

## 4. Building recurring social surface instead of hunting

The highest-leverage intervention in this document is not a technique, it is a schedule change: put two recurring commitments in the week where the same people show up repeatedly and the user is doing something they actually want to do **[E3]**.

Why it outperforms hunting:

- The user is met as a participant rather than as an approacher, which changes the entire framing for both people.
- Repetition removes the do-or-die quality of a single interaction, which collapses approach anxiety without any anxiety work at all ([anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md)).
- It produces friendships, which produce introductions, which is the second-ranked context above.
- It survives a dating season being bad, because the user was going anyway.
- It is the only intervention here that improves the user's life whether or not it produces a relationship, which is the test the OS applies to everything.

Coaching form: one activity that is physical or skill-based, one that is social or communal, both recurring weekly, both chosen for genuine interest. If the user picks something purely as a hunting ground, it will read that way to everyone there, and they will stop going by week four.

## 5. Knowing what you actually want

Most users can produce a long preference list and have never separated it from the short list that actually determines whether a relationship works.

```text
THE LONG LIST      height, job, hobbies, humour type, aesthetics, taste,
                   politics of a mild kind, a specific vibe
                   Useful for attention. Almost useless for prediction.

THE SHORT LIST     2 or 3 items only. Test: if this one is wrong,
                   nothing else can compensate, and it will not change.
                   Usually drawn from: wanting or not wanting children,
                   geography, life pace and ambition, monogamy versus a
                   negotiated structure, religion or its absence, how
                   conflict is handled, whether the person is kind under
                   stress and available in the way the user needs.
```

Two coaching moves. Have the user write the short list before the season starts, because writing it afterwards is just describing whoever they liked. And have them notice which long-list items they have already contradicted in someone they were genuinely happy with, because that is the fastest way to see the difference between a preference and a requirement.

## 6. Designing the first meeting

Design it so that both people can find out whether there is anything there, cheaply, and leave easily if there is not. Full procedure in [date_design.md](../protocols/date_design.md).

| Property | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Short** (about an hour, with a stated end) | Removes the obligation to sit through a mismatch, and makes it easy to say yes to |
| **Low-stakes and inexpensive** | Cost creates obligation, and obligation contaminates consent. An expensive first date is a bill placed on the table |
| **Talkable** | Somewhere you can hear each other. A loud bar, a cinema and a concert all prevent the only thing a first meeting is for |
| **Walkable or side-by-side** | Movement and a shared direction reduce the interview quality of face-to-face seating, and give the eyes somewhere to go **[E3]** |
| **Daytime is fine** | Coffee, a walk, a market. Lower stakes, easier logistics, and safer for both people |
| **Public and easily left** | Section 7 |
| **Ends while it is good** | Leave with momentum rather than exhausting the material. Ending on time is a signal of a full life and it makes the next meeting easy to propose |

The most common design error is scale: a long, expensive, formal evening on the first meeting. It raises the price of a no, makes both people perform, and produces less information than forty minutes of walking.

## 7. Safety logistics as part of the design

Build these in as normal design elements, for both people, never as a special precaution requested by one:

- **Public place, first meeting.** Always, and without needing a reason.
- **Own transport, both ways, for both people.** Nobody is collected from home and nobody is dropped off on the first meeting unless both want it.
- **A friend knows where and when.** Normal, sensible, and the user should be doing it too.
- **A video call beforehand is legitimate** and confirms the person is who their profile says.
- **Own drink, own tab, own pace.** The user should never be managing anyone else's consumption, including their own case for a clear head.
- **No pressure, ever, to deviate from any of the above.** If a user reports irritation at a match's safety requests, correct it firmly: those requests are reasonable, the correct response is to make them easy, and irritation at them is itself a signal that other people read accurately.

If the user is the one who feels unsafe, that is not shyness and it is not a game problem. Route to [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md).

## 8. Who pays

Entirely **[P]**: it varies by culture, by generation, by orientation, by local convention and by the two people involved. Do not import a rule.

A workable default that avoids most of the friction **[E3]**: whoever proposed the meeting offers to pay, the other person offers to split, both accept the outcome without a negotiation, and it evens out over the next few meetings. Two hard lines regardless of custom: nothing paid for is ever leverage, and a user who is keeping a ledger of what they have spent is building the resentment that spine commitment 2 exists to prevent. Keeping the first meeting cheap (section 6) removes most of this problem before it starts.

## 9. The second and third meetings

They have distinct jobs, and knowing which job is live prevents the drift described in section 10.

- **First:** is there anything here at all. Attention, ease, curiosity, whether either person is glad they came.
- **Second:** is it real, or was it the novelty of a first meeting. Longer, somewhere with a bit more texture, some actual activity. The second is where the user learns how the other person is with the world, rather than only with them.
- **Third:** does it survive ordinary conditions. Something unstructured, some genuine disclosure, some early sense of how disagreement goes. By the end of the third, both people should know roughly what the other is looking for (section 11), because after this point the cost of a mismatch starts compounding.

None of this is a schedule to enforce. It is a set of questions to notice going unanswered.

## 10. Pacing, and the two failure modes

Almost every pacing problem is one of two shapes, and they need opposite corrections.

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rushing into intensity before knowledge** | Daily contact from day two, weekends together by week two, declarations before either has seen the other tired, angry or busy. Feels wonderful, then collapses when the actual people arrive | Slow the contact rate to something sustainable, keep existing commitments, let intensity track how much is actually known. Not a tactic, a correction |
| **Drifting for months without stating anything** | Regular meetings, real warmth, no conversation about what this is, a growing private hope on at least one side | Say the true thing plainly, once, and accept the answer. Section 11 |

The first is often mislabelled as passion and the second as respect for the other person's pace. Both are avoidance: one avoids being alone, the other avoids being answered.

## 11. Saying what you are looking for

Early, plainly, and as information rather than as a negotiation. Somewhere around the second or third meeting, before either person is invested enough that the answer costs a lot **[E3]**.

Four properties: **owned** (this is what I want, not what people should want), **non-demanding** (an answer, not a request for a commitment), **specific enough to be answerable** (a relationship, something casual, not sure yet and honest about it, actively wanting children within a few years), and **followed by a genuine pause**, because the whole value is in what comes back.

Two failures to name. Hinting, which produces months of mutual inference and no information. And stating a position designed to be attractive rather than true, which works exactly until it does not, and which strands both people in a relationship one of them was never in.

If the answers are incompatible, that is a successful outcome for the conversation. It is the entire reason to have it early.

## 12. Exclusivity is a conversation, not an assumption

Nothing is exclusive until both people have said so out loud. Assuming it and being wrong produces a betrayal narrative about something that was never agreed, and this is one of the most common early ruptures **[E3]**.

- The conversation is short and does not require ceremony. It happens when one person wants it to, and wanting it earlier than the other person is not a flaw.
- A relationship structure is a negotiation, not a default. Monogamy, negotiated non-monogamy, open, polyamorous, and everything else are all things people agree to, and the OS supports whatever the user and the people involved actually agree, without commentary.
- Whatever is agreed is then kept. The OS does not help conceal a breach of an agreement, and it does not help build cover ([ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 13).
- Renegotiating is legitimate. Doing it unilaterally and retroactively is not.

## 13. Seeing several people at once

Legitimate in the early stages, common, and ethical under conditions that are easy to state.

- **Honest if asked, and asked early rather than discovered.** Not a broadcast to everyone, and not a concealment either. If someone asks, the answer is true, immediately.
- **No implied exclusivity through behaviour.** Acting exclusive while not being exclusive is the deception, whatever was technically said.
- **Attention that stays real.** Seeing enough people that nobody gets genuine attention is the funnel error from section 1 with extra logistics.
- **No comparison talk.** Nobody wants to hear about the others, and mentioning them as leverage is manufactured jealousy, refused in [refusals.md](refusals.md).
- **It ends when exclusivity is agreed**, and the ending is actual rather than gradual (section 15).

Practical ceiling: most people cannot hold more than two or three of these with any honesty, and past that the season stops being a search and becomes administration.

## 14. When to stop pursuing something that is not moving

The signals, all of them observable rather than inferred **[E3]**: the user is initiating every contact, plans are agreed and then dissolve, meetings keep being rescheduled with warmth and never rescheduled with a date, replies are warm but slow and shrinking, or every step forward is followed by a week of distance.

The rule: **one clear, low-pressure statement, then stop.** Name the interest, propose a specific thing, and if the answer is not a clear yes with a time attached, let it go completely. No second campaign in a new register, no waiting a month and trying again, no re-opening it after a haircut or a promotion.

What makes this hard is rarely the person and usually what the user has built on top of them: a season's worth of hope resting on one possibility, which is a volume problem wearing a romance costume. The correction is section 4, not more attempts.

## 15. Ending things well

A skill, a kindness, and the part of dating most people never practise. It matters more than almost anything else here, because it determines whether the other person leaves the interaction intact.

```text
CLEAR        Say it is ending. Not "things have been busy".
SHORT        Three or four sentences. Length invites negotiation.
KIND         One true positive thing, if there is one. No consolation prize.
NON-NEGOTIATING   State it, do not argue it. It is not a request.
NO DIAGNOSIS      Do not explain what is wrong with them.
DELIVERED    In the medium the relationship lived in. A few dates: a message
             is fine and often kinder. Months: in person or a call.
ONCE         Then stop. Follow-up explanations are for the sender's comfort.
```

Refused: **ghosting** as a default (with one exception below), the **manufactured slow fade** where replies are deliberately thinned until the other person gives up, and any ending designed to make the other person do the ending so the user does not have to.

The exception: ghosting is entirely legitimate where the user feels unsafe, has been harassed, or where a clear ending would invite escalation. Safety outranks etiquette, always, and no user should ever be coached into a conversation with someone who frightens them ([safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md)).

## 16. Re-entry after a long relationship

After a long relationship, a divorce or a bereavement, the re-entry has its own pacing and its own failure modes.

- **The first few meetings are recalibration, not evaluation.** Everything feels enormous because it has been years. Say so in advance so the user does not read normal nerves as evidence about themselves.
- **The context has changed, the user has not become worse at this.** Apps, norms and expectations move. That is a learning curve, not a verdict.
- **Grief and divorce have their own timeline**, and it is not the same as readiness. The honest question is not how long it has been, it is whether the user is looking for a person or looking for relief. Both are human. Only one produces a good outcome for whoever they meet.
- **Do not use a new person as the recovery mechanism.** It is unfair to them, and it reliably produces a short intense relationship that ends badly.
- **Children in the picture:** section 17.
- If the user is still in acute distress, this is not a dating problem and the coaching stops. **[C]**, route out.

## 17. Constrained circumstances

The honest version, without pretending the constraint away.

- **With children.** Time is the binding constraint, so the design changes: fewer, better, shorter meetings, daytime where possible, and no introductions to children until the relationship is established and both adults have thought about it. Say early and plainly that children are part of the picture. It is filtering information, not an apology.
- **A demanding job or shift work.** The seasonal approach in section 18 exists for this. A dating season that fits between two busy quarters beats a permanent low-grade effort that never gets real attention.
- **A small town or a rural area.** The pool is small and the graph is dense, which raises the value of context 2 (social graph) and reduces the yield of apps. Travel to a larger place at a realistic cadence, and accept that fewer possibilities means slower, not worse.
- **A small or closed community** (a faith community, a diaspora, a village, a professional niche, a small queer scene, a disability community). Everyone knows everyone, so every interaction has a social afterlife, and endings are semi-public. Two adaptations: keep the early stages genuinely private, and end things especially well (section 15), because reputation here is built out of endings, and this is the context where a bad one costs the most.
- **Any of the above plus limited energy** (chronic illness, disability, caregiving). Design for the actual energy budget, not the one the user wishes they had, and treat the constraint as filtering information rather than as something to hide.

## 18. Burnout and the seasonal approach

Dating burnout is real, common, and produced by continuous low-grade effort rather than by intensity **[E3]**. Deliberate on and off periods beat grinding.

```text
A SEASON     6 to 10 weeks. Apps on, exposure up, plans made,
             a stated number of reps, a review at the end.
AN OFF       4 weeks or more. Apps deleted. No profile, no swiping,
             no obligation. Recurring activities from section 4 CONTINUE,
             because they are life rather than campaign.
REVIEW       Between the two, run section 19 and change one thing.
```

Signals that an off period is due, not a motivation problem: dread before opening an app, indifference on the way to a meeting the user arranged, describing people in categories rather than as people, or relief when someone cancels. Rest is the intervention, and it is the one users resist because stopping feels like giving up. It is not: the recurring social surface stays on, which is where the good outcomes come from anyway.

## 19. Scoring the season on process

Score what the user controls. Outcomes depend on other people's free choices, and scoring them teaches the wrong lesson in both directions.

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Reps: new people genuinely met | Whether the exposure bottleneck is actually open |
| Contexts used, and their mix | Whether the user is over-indexed on apps (usually yes) |
| Calibration accuracy: predicted interest against what happened | The single most improvable skill in the pack |
| Clean asks made, clean exits taken | The behaviour the OS is actually training |
| Endings handled well | Whether people leave intact |
| Did the user enjoy any of it | The one nobody tracks, and the best predictor of whether the season continues |
| Short-list clarity after the season | Whether the search is getting smarter |

A season with twelve real conversations, accurate reads, two clean endings and no relationship is a good season. A season with a relationship that arrived through pressure or a persona is not, and the OS says so.

## 20. Refused here

- **Volume tactics that treat people as throughput:** mass messaging, copy-paste at scale, a pipeline dashboard for humans. Refused, see [refusals.md](refusals.md).
- **Manufactured scarcity and strategic delay** to manage someone's read of the user's availability. Refused. A real full life needs no maintenance.
- **Manufactured jealousy**, mentioning other dates as leverage. Refused.
- **The slow fade and the disappearance** as a way to avoid one uncomfortable message. Corrected, not refused, and the alternative is in section 15.
- **Pursuing after a clear no**, in a new frame, a new context, or after a period of silence. Refused. A no ends the question.
- **Any pressure on another person to skip a safety step.** Refused outright, and it is a signal about the user worth examining.
- **Concealing relationship status or intent** to keep a possibility open. Refused ([ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 13).
