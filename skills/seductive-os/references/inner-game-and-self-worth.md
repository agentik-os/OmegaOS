# Inner game and self-worth

The layer under everything else in this pack. Presence, conversation, calibration and flirtation are all downstream of one question: where the user's sense of worth is sourced while they are talking to someone they want. Get that wrong and every technique above it becomes a way of asking for a verdict.

Written on top of [ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md). Where this appears to conflict with the spine, the spine wins.

## 1. What inner game actually is, without the mysticism

The genre wraps this layer in language that cannot be checked: frame, abundance, vibe, state. None of it is falsifiable, so none of it is coachable. Strip it and three observable things remain, all trainable **[E3]**: where worth is sourced in the moment, what attention is doing, and what the user will pay in self-erasure to keep the answer open.

| The genre says | The observable behaviour underneath |
| --- | --- |
| "Strong frame" | They finished their own sentence when interrupted, and did not apologize for an opinion |
| "Abundance mentality" | Their week contains other things they want, so this evening is not load-bearing |
| "They lost state" | Attention moved from the person to the scoreboard |
| "High value" | They have a life they like, and can decline something without a production |
| "Outcome independence" | The next hour of their mood does not hinge on one answer from one person |

Coach the right column. It is concrete, it can be scored in [interaction_debrief.md](../protocols/interaction_debrief.md), and it requires the user to adopt no worldview at all.

## 2. Where worth is sourced, and what it costs

**Externally sourced:** worth is an output, recomputed continuously from the other person's face. A laugh raises it, a glance at a phone lowers it. The user is running an evaluation loop at conversational speed while also trying to talk.

**Internally sourced:** worth is a standing input. Their response is information about fit, timing and mood, which is genuinely interesting rather than a verdict being read out.

The cost of the first is mechanical, not moral **[E3]**. It hands the other person the examiner's role, which nobody enjoys and most people feel within a minute. It turns every silence into evidence, so the user fills silences, which is the commonest way a promising conversation gets flattened. It makes warmth conditional on being rewarded. And it makes a no expensive to give, which corrupts every signal the user then receives ([ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 6).

Say early that nobody is fully internally sourced. This is a dial, not a switch, and anyone claiming total indifference to how they land is either lying or unpleasant to be near.

## 3. Outcome dependence: the central mechanic

Define it so it can be measured: **the degree to which the user's state in the next hour depends on one specific answer from one specific person.** Wanting the outcome is not the problem. Needing it is. Coach that distinction explicitly, because users hear "outcome independence" as "stop caring", fake indifference, and produce something worse than the original nerves.

```text
THE DIAL
0  No interest here. Nothing at stake, and it reads as coldness.
3  I would enjoy this going well. My evening is fine either way.
5  I want this, I notice wanting it, I can still hear what they say.
7  I am tracking their face for the verdict. My questions stopped landing.
9  The rest of my week rides on this. I am negotiating, not talking.
Target band: 3 to 5. Not 0.
```

| Where it shows | Body | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Breath high and short, shoulders lifted | Answering before they finish, laughing a half-beat early |
| Attention | Eyes scanning their face rather than resting | Cannot recall what they said ninety seconds ago |
| Voice | Rising pitch on statements, volume dropping mid-sentence | Every statement becomes a request for agreement |
| Space | Leaning past comfortable distance | Closing gaps the other person did not open |
| Content | Fidget, or freeze | Agreeing, softening, apologizing for the last sentence |

Why it is felt immediately: it transfers work. The other person now manages the user's state as well as their own evening. Their private description is rarely "not confident". It is "this is effortful" **[E3]**. The body half is trained in [presence-and-embodiment.md](presence-and-embodiment.md); the belief half is here, and neither substitutes for the other.

## 4. Self-abandonment

The most common failure in the pack, and the one users are most surprised by, because from the inside it feels like being nice. The pattern: agreeing with what you do not agree with, laughing at what is not funny, softening every opinion into a question, dropping your own plans before being asked, saying "whatever you want" to choices you have a view on, discovering a sudden interest in whatever they like, never mentioning the thing you actually care about, apologizing for taking up conversational space.

Why it reliably kills attraction **[E3]**:

1. **It deletes the information.** Attraction attaches to particulars, and self-erasure removes the particulars, so there is nothing left to be attracted to.
2. **It makes being chosen impossible.** They cannot choose the user, because they have not met the user.
3. **It transfers the whole steering load.** Every topic, decision and silence is now their job, and work is not attractive.
4. **It is read correctly**, as either fear or dishonesty, and both readings are unflattering in the same way.

The cruelty is that the people who do this are usually the most considerate in the room. Do not let the coaching imply otherwise. Agreeableness is a real virtue; self-erasure is a different thing wearing its clothes.

| Situation | Considerate | Self-erasing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They suggest a film you dislike | "Not my thing, but happy to watch it with you" | "Love that one" |
| They state a view you disagree with | "I see it differently, want to hear why?" | Nodding, changing subject |
| They cancel late, twice | "Fine. Third time I will assume the timing is wrong" | "No worries!" then silence and resentment |
| They tease something you are proud of | Laughing, then "I am actually serious about it though" | Joining in against yourself |

The repair is not disagreeableness. It is one true sentence per interaction, minimum, which is why the process scorecard asks for it by name.

## 5. Neediness, defined correctly

The genre uses the word as a slur against wanting anything, which is cruel, wrong, and produces users ashamed of the most human thing about them. The OS defines it narrowly: **neediness is extracting reassurance about your worth, your standing or their interest, from someone who did not offer it.**

Not in the definition, and never shamed: wanting connection (it is the point), wanting a relationship, being lonely (a circumstance, and often an accurate signal), missing someone, texting first, or saying you like someone, which is legibility and the OS asks for it.

| The healthy want | The extraction version |
| --- | --- |
| "I had a good time, I would like to do it again" | "Did you have a good time? Was I too much?" |
| Saying you find them attractive | Fishing until they say it back |
| Asking for what you want in a relationship | Asking to be told you are wanted, repeatedly, and not believing it |
| Noticing they went quiet and asking once | Three follow-ups, then a message about the follow-ups |
| Feeling insecure and naming it to a friend | Handing it to the person who caused it, to fix |

The last row is load-bearing. Insecurity is not a flaw to hide, it is a state to regulate somewhere it does not become the other person's job: friends, practice, [reset_5_min.md](../protocols/reset_5_min.md), and where relevant a professional.

## 6. Validation seeking versus expressed interest

Same sentence, two different acts. Expressed interest **gives**; validation seeking **takes**.

```text
THE DIRECTION TEST
1. What response would make this a success?
   If it is "they say something good about me", it is validation seeking.
2. Would I still say it knowing there would be no reply at all?
3. Am I asking them to tell me something about me?
```

| Expressed interest | Validation seeking |
| --- | --- |
| "That thing about your brother stayed with me" | "I hope that was not a weird thing to say" |
| A compliment about something they chose or did | A compliment fished back with an expectant pause |
| "I would like to see you Thursday" | "You are probably busy Thursday, right?" |
| Sharing an opinion they might dislike | Sharing an opinion after checking theirs |
| Silence, comfortably | "Sorry, I am talking too much" |

Pre-loading the refusal ("you are probably busy") looks like politeness. It is a discount applied before the answer, and it makes a yes harder to give, since they must now disagree with the user before agreeing with them.

## 7. Diversifying the sources of worth

A portfolio argument. Worth sourced from one place is fragile in exactly the way a business with one customer is fragile, and the romantic domain is the worst possible single source: high variance, slow feedback, largely outside the user's control.

Sources that load-bear, roughly in order of reliability **[E3]**: competence at something difficult that others can see; a body used for something rather than evaluated in a mirror; friendships predating this romantic season; a craft with a visible artifact; service to someone who needs it; family or chosen family; standards kept when nobody was watching (section 15).

```text
WORTH PORTFOLIO (actual weights, summing to 100, not aspirational)
Work / competence __  Body __  Friendship __  Craft __
Service __  Family __  Romantic response __
```

If romantic response is above roughly 40 percent, that is the bottleneck, and no conversational technique will move it. Say so plainly and reroute the season: building a second and third source will do more for the user's magnetism than any amount of practice.

Honest evidence note: the literature on whether a more differentiated self-concept buffers setbacks is real but mixed, and effects depend heavily on measurement **[E2]**. The portfolio frame is craft that reliably helps people, not an established finding.

## 8. The entitlement trap and the covert contract

The belief, rarely said out loud: **investment converts into obligation.** Time, kindness, listening, being reliable, lifts given, messages answered at 2am. This is the "nice guy" pattern in its most discussed form, but it is not gendered and the OS does not treat it as such. The general name is the covert contract: terms the other party never saw, never agreed to, and are nonetheless in breach of.

```text
1. GIVE       Kindness offered, framed internally as investment.
2. ACCRUE     A ledger the other person cannot see and never signed.
3. PRESENT    The ask, made late, carrying the weight of the ledger.
4. REFUSE     They decline, as they were always free to.
5. RESENT     "I did everything right and it did not work."
6. GENERALIZE "People do not want kindness." The belief hardens.
```

**It is a consent failure.** Commitment 2 of the spine: interest is never a debt. A covert contract makes a no into a breach of an unsigned agreement, which is exactly how refusal starts to feel like injustice, and injustice is what licenses pressure.

**It also fails on the user's own terms.** Instrumental kindness is detectable well before step 3, with a particular quality: attentive but keeping count. People withdraw from it without being able to say why, which then feeds step 6 with false evidence, and it poisons the generosity underneath, which was real before it went on the books.

The exit: (1) make the want legible early, since interest stated in week one costs an awkward minute and interest revealed in month nine costs the friendship ([ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 9, [flirtation-and-escalation.md](flirtation-and-escalation.md)); (2) give only what you would give knowing nothing romantic will ever happen; (3) audit for the invoice, since any sentence starting "after everything I" is one; (4) accept that a no is not a breach, which is the whole exit and the other three are scaffolding. Users deep in this arrive angry. Do not moralize, they have already been told they are a bad person and it did not help.

## 9. Comparison and the ranking frame

The belief that people occupy one ordered scale, that the user has a position on it, and that the interaction is a check on whether their position is high enough.

It degrades presence mechanically: it puts a third party into a two-person interaction, and attention does not divide for free; it points attention inward at the exact moment outward attention is the skill; it produces supplication when the user ranks below and quiet contempt when above, which is the same coin and read just as fast; and the person in front of them can usually tell they are being measured.

It is also a category error. Attraction is not a scalar over a total order, it is fit: a joint property of two people, a context and a moment **[E3]**. The honest counterweight, because denying it makes the OS untrustworthy: some traits really are broadly valued in aggregate **[E1]**, and some contexts really do have selection pressure. The error is the real-time computation of one's own rank mid-conversation, which is useless, uninformed and expensive. Replace "am I good enough for them" with "do we actually enjoy this", which is answerable tonight from evidence.

## 10. The internal monologue, and the only real exit

What is actually running: what do I say next, do they like me, that was stupid, how do I look, say something, they are bored. Every item is about the user, and that is the problem: attention is finite, and the monologue is consuming the exact resource the skill needs.

The exit is attention outward, and it works for a capacity reason rather than a mystical one **[E3]**. Two immediate consequences: the monologue quiets because it has less room to run in, and the material appears, since the next thing to say comes from what they just said. A user who is genuinely listening does not run out; a user monitoring themselves always does.

```text
THE ATTENTION LOOP
LAND    Feet, one long exhale. Two seconds.
LOOK    One specific detail about them that is actually there.
LISTEN  Their whole last sentence, including the part after the point.
ASK     A question that could only follow from what they just said.
REPEAT  When you notice the monologue, do not fight it. Run LOOK again.
```

The diagnostic, usable in any debrief: **name three specific things they said in the last two minutes.** Not topics, sentences. A user who cannot was not there. Pair with [conversation-craft.md](conversation-craft.md), which turns the same attention into material. Do not instruct the user to silence the monologue. Deliberate thought suppression tends to backfire, and while the size of that effect is contested it is a poor bet either way **[E2]**. Redirect capacity instead.

## 11. Self-compassion versus self-esteem

**Self-esteem is a judgment about the self**, and judgments need evidence, so it requires wins. It is therefore least available at the moment it is most needed: after being turned down, after freezing, after a bad evening. **Self-compassion is a way of treating yourself** and does not require the verdict to be good, so it works exactly where self-esteem fails.

Stated with the honesty the pack requires: self-compassion measures correlate with lower anxiety and faster recovery from social setbacks, and brief interventions show modest effects, but most of the literature is self-report, much is cross-sectional, effect sizes are moderate at best, and the field carries the usual publication incentives **[E2]**. Coach it because it is useful and low risk, not because it is settled.

Three moves, matching the house pattern: **truth** (name what happened without euphemism or inflation, since "I got nervous and left early" is truth and "I humiliated myself" is inflation), **humanity** (failure does not remove worth, and the fluent-looking people find this hard too), **responsibility** (choose the repair and the next action). Self-compassion without responsibility becomes avoidance; accountability without humanity becomes shame. Users usually run one and not the other, so supply the missing half rather than doubling the one they have.

## 12. Standards, and becoming someone who also chooses

Most users describe themselves purely as an applicant. Every question is about qualification, and they have deleted the half of the interaction where they choose.

The test: **name three things that would make you decline someone who wanted you.** Not cruelties, real preferences: someone unkind to staff, someone who cannot be alone, someone whose week has no shape. If the user cannot answer, their yes is not a decision, it is relief, and that is felt as strongly as anything else here.

Three constraints. Standards are internal and mostly about how the interaction feels, so "I want to feel relaxed and interested" is a standard while a list of attributes and numbers is a filter, labelled **[P]** and not built here. Standards are never announced as demands, because announced high standards are usually the same insecurity in a different coat and a person genuinely choosing is warm about it. And standards are not a ranking of people (section 9).

The behavioural version matters more than the list: during the interaction, actually evaluate whether you like them. Users report their nerves drop, and the mechanism is obvious once said, since it is difficult to be examined and examining at the same time, and the second is more interesting.

## 13. Boundaries as a magnetism skill

A person who can say no is a person whose yes means something. That is not a metaphor, it is a fact about signals: one that is never withheld carries no information.

Three mechanisms **[E3]**. The yes becomes informative, because agreement to everything says nothing about what the user likes or is. They become safe to be near, because someone who cannot refuse cannot be trusted to have told the truth about anything, including their enthusiasm. And it removes a burden, since people with a clear no do not have to be handled.

```text
THE SMALL-NO LADDER
1. Decline a free thing you do not want (a drink, a lift, a leaflet).
2. State a preference where you would normally defer.
3. Say "I do not know" instead of manufacturing an answer.
4. Disagree once, warmly, without softening it into a question.
5. Decline a small invitation, with no invented excuse.
6. Say "that does not work for me" about something that genuinely does not.
7. End a conversation you are not enjoying, kindly, before it ends itself.
```

This runs both directions and the pack means it: the user's own no is final, they may withdraw at any point, and their discomfort is data rather than a fault in their game. See [ethics-and-consent.md](ethics-and-consent.md) section 14 and [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md).

## 14. Courage first, confidence second

Users arrive with the ordering "get confident, then act". It is backwards, and it produces indefinite delay, because the evidence that would produce confidence is generated only by the action being deferred.

```text
ACTION -> EVIDENCE -> CONFIDENCE -> EASIER ACTION      (not the reverse)
```

Three things to coach. **You will not feel ready**, because readiness is a receipt issued afterwards and users who wait for it wait forever. **Courage is a behaviour, not a trait**: say the sentence, make the ask, stay ten seconds past the urge to leave. **Confidence is domain-specific**, so a user who is formidable at work and mute at a party is not missing a general quantity, they are missing reps in one domain **[E3]**.

The avoidance half of this loop, including why relief makes it worse, belongs to [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md). Route users who never actually go there before any conversational coaching.

## 15. The self-trust ladder

Identity is built from evidence, and the cheapest evidence is a promise kept to yourself when nobody was watching.

```text
1  A promise so small that failing is not plausible. Ten minutes, today.
2  The same promise, three days running.
3  A promise kept on a bad day, at reduced size, not skipped.
4  A promise kept when the reason to skip was legitimate.
5  A promise involving another person, kept without a reminder.
6  A promise about the thing you actually avoid. One rep, this week.
7  Ten kept promises in one domain. Now there is a sentence about
   yourself with evidence behind it, and it is not affirmation.
```

Rules that keep the ledger honest. Never make a promise you are 50/50 on, because a coin-flip promise corrupts the ledger faster than making none. Size down, never skip: five minutes kept beats thirty abandoned, since the ledger records kept or not kept. A broken promise gets a repair, never a self-attack, and the repair is a smaller promise kept today. Promises made silently count double, because that is where self-trust actually accrues.

Hand the stickiness mechanics to Habit Tracker OS. Keep the interpretation here: the user is not building discipline, they are building the belief that their own statements mean something, which is the same belief that lets them say a true thing to someone attractive.

## 16. Authenticity, handled properly

Two failure modes, opposite directions. **"Just be yourself" as advice** is useless when the self on offer has been curated for approval since adolescence; many users genuinely do not know what they think, because they have checked the room first for twenty years. **"This is just who I am" as a shield** protects avoidance, poor grooming, rudeness and refusal to learn, and would exempt every unpleasant person alive.

The correct frame: **authenticity constrains the method, it does not prohibit change.** The pack asks for change constantly. It asks that the changes survive being known (spine, commitment 3).

| The change | Verdict against "would it survive them knowing, and would I keep it single?" |
| --- | --- |
| Learning to ask better questions | Growth. Survives both, useful everywhere |
| Getting fit | Growth if they would keep it single. Otherwise a costume with a gym membership |
| Dressing better | Growth. Presentation is communication and everyone is already doing it |
| Practising being turned down | Growth, and the most transferable thing in the pack |
| A memorized opener | Mask. Fails the first question immediately |
| Pretending to be busier than you are | Mask. Refused by name in [refusals.md](refusals.md) |
| Hiding an interest because it is uncool | Mask, and it deletes the particulars attraction attaches to |

For users who resist all self-presentation work as inauthentic: choosing not to present is also a presentation, and not a neutral one. See [style-and-self-presentation.md](style-and-self-presentation.md).

## 17. Envy of the socially fluent

Normalize it, then convert it. Envy names a value, and the response is neither shame nor indulgence but extraction of the specific.

Two honest facts. **Fluency is mostly reps:** the naturally easy person usually had a childhood, family or job supplying thousands of low-stakes repetitions before they were twenty, so the user is missing a training history rather than a gene, and training histories can be started late **[E3]**. **You are comparing your inside to their outside**, with full access to your own anxiety and none to theirs.

```text
ENVY -> SPECIFIC
1. Name who. One person.
2. Name what they actually did. Three or four behaviours you could film.
3. Pick the one you could try this week.
4. Try it in the lowest-stakes context available.
```

What step 2 usually yields: they asked a follow-up instead of switching to their own story, they were slower than everyone else, they let a silence sit for two seconds, they said the mildly disagreeable thing warmly, they greeted the person nobody was talking to. All learnable, all boring, none of them a personality trait.

**The failure mode:** the pivot from "they are better at this than me" to "they are shallow and people are stupid for liking them". That is the resentment exit, it feels much better for about a week, and it terminates learning permanently. Name it when it appears, without contempt, because the user is in pain and the pivot is what pain does.

## 18. Handoffs, and where coaching stops

| The need | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| Identity, purpose, worth as a life-wide question, self-talk | Mindset OS |
| Sleep, training, the physical substrate under presence | Health & Energy OS |
| Making the self-trust ladder stick | Habit Tracker OS |
| Values, a hard relational decision, meaning | Alignment OS |
| Friendship and professional social capability | Relationship & Network OS |
| Whether a specific person is interested | [social-calibration.md](social-calibration.md) |
| What to do after a no | [rejection-resilience.md](rejection-resilience.md) |
| Never actually going, freezing, avoidance | [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md) |

**Route out and stop coaching** **[C]** when any of the following is present, because this document in particular can be mistaken for therapy by someone who needs the real thing: depression, persistent hopelessness or loss of function; self-harm or any thought of not being here; panic attacks or panic disorder; social anxiety at a disabling level (functional markers in [anxiety-and-avoidance.md](anxiety-and-avoidance.md)); body dysmorphic disorder, an eating disorder, or compulsive mirror and photo checking; obsessive rumination about one specific person, or any monitoring, tracking or surveillance of them; stalking ideation in any form; crisis after a breakup; alcohol or any substance as the precondition for social contact.

Say it plainly, once, without drama: this is outside what coaching can do, it is treatable, and a qualified professional is the right tool. Do not diagnose, do not name a condition as though assessing for it, and do not keep running practice plans alongside. Route to [safety-and-boundaries.md](safety-and-boundaries.md) and hand judgment back.

Last constraint, the anti-dependency one: this is not a course of study. Every session that touches it ends in one small real action ([weekly_practice.md](../protocols/weekly_practice.md)), and a user who has been reading about inner game for six months with no real interactions is doing avoidance with better vocabulary.
